### Part (a): Saturated-Salt Humidity Chambers and Humidity Effects on Powder Flow

#### 1. Accuracy of Saturated-Salt Humidity Fixed Points

The definitive reference for saturated-salt humidity fixed points is Greenspan (1977), who compiled equilibrium relative humidity (RH) data for 28 binary saturated aqueous solutions using 1,106 measurements from 21 investigations, fitted as polynomial functions of temperature (greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 1-2, greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 2-3). Uncertainties were assigned as three times the standard deviation of the predicted values, with individual datum uncertainties propagated through a root-sum-square combination of contributions from temperature, pressure, equilibrium attainment, and sensor calibration (greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 7-8, greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 6-7). At 25 °C, the reported uncertainties for salts commonly used in humidity chambers are: LiCl 11.3 ± 0.3% RH; MgCl₂ 32.8 ± 0.2% RH; Mg(NO₃)₂ 52.9 ± 0.2% RH; NaCl 75.3 ± 0.2% RH; KCl 84.3 ± 0.3% RH; KNO₃ 93.6 ± 0.6% RH; and K₂SO₄ 97.3 ± 0.5% RH (lu2007uncertaintyevaluationof pages 1-2, greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 4-5). Lu and Chen (2007) used these Greenspan values to calibrate humidity sensors with vessels maintained at 25 ± 0.2 °C and 12-hour equilibration periods, reporting that the dominant contribution to combined uncertainty was the predicted calibration uncertainty of the sensor itself rather than the salt reference (lu2007uncertaintyevaluationof pages 1-2).

#### 2. Temperature Sensitivity

The temperature coefficient dRH/dT varies considerably among salts. From the polynomial coefficients in Greenspan's Table 1 (greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 2-3, greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 3-4), NaCl has a relatively small temperature sensitivity of approximately −0.07% RH/°C near 25 °C, whereas Mg(NO₃)₂ is approximately −0.30% RH/°C—meaning that a ±1 °C temperature excursion shifts the nominal RH by ∓0.3 percentage points for Mg(NO₃)₂ but only ∓0.07 for NaCl. MgCl₂ has an even flatter coefficient (approximately −0.06% RH/°C), making it one of the most temperature-insensitive fixed points (greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 4-5). Rockland (1960) emphasized the desirability of salts with low temperature coefficients and warned that even small temperature variations can cause significant changes in equilibrium RH (rockland1960saturatedsaltsolutions pages 1-1). Rörig-Dalgaard and Svensson (2016) cautioned that heat of solution during salt dissolution or crystallization can locally change temperature and thus calibration RH, noting that inadequate moisture control can produce calibration uncertainty as large as ±2.5% RH (rorigdalgaard2016highaccuracycalibration pages 3-4).

#### 3. Equilibration Time and Effect of Mixing Fans

Equilibration time depends strongly on the salt, chamber volume, sample loading, and air circulation. In dynamic vapor sorption (DVS) calibration, salt-specific test durations ranged from approximately 6 hours for MgCl₂ to 20 hours for LiCl and KNO₃, with NaCl and Mg(NO₃)₂ requiring approximately 10 hours (rorigdalgaard2016highaccuracycalibration pages 6-8). For static desiccator-type chambers, equilibration can require days to weeks, particularly at high RH (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 1-5, imre1964themeasurementof pages 1-4). Rockland (1960) explicitly evaluated the effect of mechanical convection (a mixing fan) on equilibration rates and reported that fans decreased the time to achieve equilibration by 10 to 50 times compared to still-air conditions (rockland1960saturatedsaltsolutions pages 1-1). This strongly supports including a small circulating fan within each humidity chamber to accelerate recovery after opening.

#### 4. Sample Size and Layer Thickness for Gravimetric Sorption

Traditional saturated-salt-solution (SSS) gravimetric sorption methods typically require large samples of 10–100 g, with equilibration periods of days to weeks (burnettUnknownyearmoisturesorptionproperties pages 1-2). Dynamic vapor sorption (DVS) instruments use much smaller samples, typically 20–100 mg, with equilibration at each RH step achievable in hours rather than days (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 5-9, munizlerma2018acomprehensiveapproach pages 3-5). For the proposed few-gram setup using the doser, intermediate between these extremes, the key consideration is ensuring that the powder layer is thin enough for vapor to penetrate the bed rapidly. Arlabosse et al. (2003) found that for materials with apparent diffusion coefficients above approximately 10⁻⁹ m²/s, good agreement between static and dynamic methods was obtained; a 500 µm powder slab of microcrystalline cellulose gave diffusion coefficients of 1.7–4.4 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s and produced consistent results (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 9-13, arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 5-9).

#### 5. Agreement Between DVS and Saturated-Salt Methods

Good agreement between DVS and SSS methods has been reported for several powder materials, particularly at low to intermediate RH. For microcrystalline cellulose, pharmaceutical granules, and barley straw, DVS and SSS isotherms generally agreed well (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 9-13, bui2017comparisonofthe pages 6-9, bui2017comparisonofthe pages 1-6). Disagreement can arise at high water activity (above ~0.6–0.8) where slow internal diffusion limits the sample's ability to reach thermodynamic equilibrium within DVS step times (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 9-13). The DVS mass-stability criterion is typically 100 times less stringent than the SSS criterion, which partly accounts for DVS being faster but potentially less accurate at high RH (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 5-9). For materials with dense or laminated structures, the methods may disagree because DVS results become dependent on sorption rate rather than thermodynamic equilibrium (schmidt2012comparisonbetweenwater pages 1-4).

#### 6. Humidity Effects on NaCl (Deliquescence Near 75% RH)

Sodium chloride has a deliquescence relative humidity (DRH) of approximately 75–76% at 25 °C (langlet2011kineticsofdissolution pages 1-3, afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 29-33). Below this threshold, the crystalline phase is thermodynamically stable; at or above DRH, water uptake initiates dissolution and formation of a saturated aqueous solution. The dissolution rate is proportional to the difference between imposed RH and DRH (langlet2011kineticsofdissolution pages 1-3, langlet2011kineticsofdissolution pages 3-4). At 80% RH, approximately 25.5% of NaCl dissolves after four days; at 85% RH, 57.3%; and at 90% RH, 80.3% (langlet2011kineticsofdissolution pages 6-9). Operating at exactly 75% RH places NaCl dangerously close to its DRH: small temperature or humidity fluctuations can trigger partial dissolution, formation of liquid bridges at particle contacts, and subsequent recrystallization upon drying, producing solid bridges and irreversible caking (langlet2011kineticsofdissolution pages 1-3, afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 29-33). Dense NaCl powder aggregates can take days or weeks to deliquesce completely because vapor transport is restricted within the powder bed (linnow2006investigationofsodium pages 58-61, linnow2006investigationofsodium pages 54-58). This suggests that the NaCl chamber should ideally be omitted for conditioning NaCl powder itself, as the equilibrium RH of saturated NaCl coincides with NaCl's own DRH.

#### 7. Humidity Effects on Metal AM Powders (AlSi10Mg, Ti-6Al-4V)

Among metal AM powders, AlSi10Mg is the most susceptible to moisture uptake, with moisture content increasing up to six times the initial value after conditioning at 50 °C and 80% RH for 72 hours (cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 10-11, cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 1-2). The irregular and rough particle morphology of AlSi10Mg promotes moisture adsorption through both physisorption and chemisorption, leading to thicker oxide layers, agglomeration via capillary bridges, and severely degraded spreadability (cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 10-11, cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 2-3). DVS measurements on similar AlSi7Mg powders showed that fine particles contribute most to moisture sorption due to their greater specific surface area; removing fines by sieving substantially reduced vapor uptake (munizlerma2018acomprehensiveapproach pages 5-8). Ti-6Al-4V, with its spherical and homogeneous particle morphology, shows very low moisture pickup in all tested states, indicating much lower sorption susceptibility (cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 10-11, cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 4-5). Nevertheless, even Ti-6Al-4V can form moisture-mediated agglomerates via capillary bridges (cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 10-11). Drying practices in the literature typically use 80–190 °C, with vacuum drying at 85 °C for 12 hours producing effective moisture removal (cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 4-5, cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 2-3).

#### 8. Humidity Effects on Pharmaceutical Powders and Caking

Pharmaceutical powders such as lactose are particularly susceptible to humidity-induced caking. Amorphous fractions within otherwise crystalline powders are hygroscopic and can crystallize when exposed to humid air, releasing water and forming solid bridges (afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 39-43). The caking mechanism involves condensation, dissolution, and recrystallization at particle contacts, driven by the difference between ambient RH and the material's critical RH or DRH (afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 29-33, afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 39-43). Caking strength correlates with the rate and extent of crystallization, and repeated humidity cycling generally worsens the effect. Storage of hygroscopic powders over saturated solutions of MgCl₂, NaBr, NaCl, and KCl has been used to study caking at controlled RH levels (afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 29-33).

### Part (b): Faraday-Cup Measurement of Powder Charge-to-Mass Ratio

#### 1. Cup and Shield Design

The standard Faraday cup for powder charge measurement consists of a conducting inner measurement cup surrounded by a larger grounded conducting outer cup, with an electrically insulating material (typically Teflon) separating them (peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 5-7, sowinski2012thestudyof pages 108-114, cruise2023triboelectricchargingof pages 47-51). The outer cup functions as a Faraday cage, reducing electromagnetic background noise (cruise2023triboelectricchargingof pages 47-51, sowinski2012thestudyof pages 56-59). A BNC or coaxial cable connects the assembly to the measurement electronics: the cable shield connects to the grounded outer cup, while the center conductor connects to the inner cup (cruise2023triboelectricchargingof pages 47-51, cruise2023triboelectricchargingofb pages 47-51). Very high insulation between the inner and outer cups is required to minimize charge decay (peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 5-7). Sowinski (2012) described concentric cylindrical copper vessels with inner dimensions of 0.15 m diameter × 0.25 m height and outer dimensions of 0.20 m diameter × 0.33 m height, isolated by Teflon bolts and connected to a Keithley 6514 electrometer (sowinski2012thestudyof pages 108-114). For the proposed bench-top setup with a 3 mm doser outlet, a smaller aluminium cup inside a grounded can is appropriate.

#### 2. Collection Efficiency for Fines

Collection efficiency for fine particles is a recognized challenge. Sowinski (2012) used a reusable filter bag with only 26–30% capture efficiency for 1 µm particulates (sowinski2012thestudyof pages 108-114). Sun et al. (2019) designed a mini-Faraday cup with an internal metal disc filter specifically to achieve high collection efficiency for charged ultrafine particles in the 50–300 nm range, with measured current showing good linearity with particle concentration (sun2019designandevaluation pages 6-7). For the proposed setup collecting auger-dosed powder, ensuring that all fine particles land in the cup rather than being lost to air currents or electrostatic adhesion to walls is important; a deep cup geometry and controlled drop height help maximize collection.

#### 3. Electrometers Versus Home-Built Charge Amplifiers

Commercial electrometers (e.g., Keithley 6514) provide turnkey charge/current measurement with proven specifications but at high cost (sowinski2012thestudyof pages 108-114). For a home-built charge amplifier, the critical component is an electrometer-grade CMOS op-amp with ultra-low input bias current (mortuza2017picocurrentmeasurementchallenges pages 4-6, mortuza2017picocurrentmeasurementchallenges pages 1-2). Two leading candidates are:

- **LMP7721** (Texas Instruments): 3 fA typical input bias current, 17 MHz gain-bandwidth product, rail-to-rail output. Its ultra-low bias current makes it suitable for sub-picoampere transimpedance and charge-integrator circuits (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 17-20, currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 22-25).
- **ADA4530-1** (Analog Devices): ~20 fA typical input bias current, designed specifically for electrometer applications with on-chip guarding (sharmaUnknownyearabootstrappedesd pages 51-57).

The charge amplifier operates as an integrator: a known feedback capacitor C_F between the output and inverting input converts input charge to voltage according to q = C_F × V_F (cruise2023triboelectricchargingof pages 47-51). The feedback capacitor value determines both sensitivity and dynamic range; smaller capacitors give higher sensitivity but saturate sooner. A parallel high-value resistor or periodic reset switch prevents drift from integrating bias current (mortuza2017picocurrentmeasurementchallenges pages 1-2). Input capacitance (~11 pF for LMP7721) combined with PCB stray capacitance forms a pole with feedback components that can cause instability; a feedback capacitor may be needed for compensation, with the value determined experimentally (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 17-20).

#### 4. Leakage, Guarding, and Practical Implementation

At femtoampere current levels, leakage from PCB traces, connectors, and insulation dominates measurement error (sharmaUnknownyearabootstrappedesd pages 51-57). Guarding is essential: a driven guard trace surrounds the high-impedance input and feedback nodes, maintained at approximately the same potential as the protected node, so that voltage differences across insulation are eliminated and leakage current is diverted into the low-impedance guard rather than the input (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 20-22, mortuza2017picocurrentmeasurementchallenges pages 4-6). The guard should form a complete conductive enclosure including guard planes connected through vias, with an external ground plane or shield protecting it from noise and leakage (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 20-22). Triaxial cables extend guarding along cables to the Faraday cup (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 20-22). Cleanliness is critical for sub-picoamp performance: flux residues, moisture, solvents, and contamination degrade leakage performance; PCB materials with low moisture absorption should be selected, and solder mask should be removed around guard structures (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 20-22, sharmaUnknownyearabootstrappedesd pages 51-57). Teflon is the preferred insulating material for ultra-low-leakage applications (mortuza2017picocurrentmeasurementchallenges pages 4-6). Supply filtering with a low-noise linear regulator, ~100 Ω series resistance, and ≥100 µF bypass capacitor is recommended to prevent supply noise from coupling through ESD capacitance (~0.5 pF) at each input (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 22-25).

#### 6. Triboelectric Series Considerations for the PLA Auger

In the conventional triboelectric series, nylon is positioned on the strongly positive side, while PTFE (Teflon) is at the most negative extreme; polyethylene, polypropylene, and PVC occupy intermediate negative positions (peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 2-4, prawatya2018multivariateoptimisationand pages 21-24). PLA, though not always explicitly listed in older triboelectric series, has been shown experimentally to charge polymers either positively or sign-reverse relative to stainless steel: ABS charged negatively against stainless steel but positively against PLA, while PE and PP charged negatively against both (dani2024effectofmoisture pages 4-5). This sign reversal demonstrates that the PLA auger tube will produce different charging polarities than a stainless-steel auger for the same powder, which is directly measurable with the proposed Faraday cup.

#### 7. Humidity Dependence of Triboelectric Charging

Humidity has a profound effect on triboelectric charging through multiple mechanisms: (i) adsorbed water layers increase surface conductivity, enabling charge dissipation; (ii) water bridges between contacting surfaces facilitate ion-mediated charge transfer; (iii) the dielectric strength of humid air is reduced, lowering the saturation charge on particles (cruise2023triboelectricchargingof pages 100-104, sutka2023engineeringpolymerinterfaces pages 5-5). Experimentally, Dani et al. (2024) found that humid conditioning (96 hours at 82% RH) reduced the absolute charge-to-mass ratio to approximately 50–70% of normal values across all tested polymers (dani2024effectofmoisture pages 4-5). Schella et al. (2017) demonstrated that polymer granulates became highly charged at low humidity (<30% RH) but acquired almost no charge above 80% RH, with the transition RH depending on the material (sutka2023engineeringpolymerinterfaces pages 5-5). For polymer contacts, charging was found to reach a maximum near 30% RH and decrease above 40% RH (sutka2023engineeringpolymerinterfaces pages 5-5). Bailey (1993) reported that for polyethylene, dry conditions produced bipolar charging (coarse positive, fines negative), while high humidity caused all particles to charge negatively; at ~35% RH, net charge was approximately zero (bailey1993chargingofsolids pages 6-9). These findings indicate that the Faraday cup measurements should be performed at controlled and recorded humidity, ideally pairing them with the humidity chambers from test (6) to systematically map the charge–humidity relationship for each powder.

### Summary and Practical Recommendations

For **test (6)**, the saturated-salt humidity chambers provide thermodynamic fixed points with Greenspan-tabulated uncertainties of ±0.1–0.5% RH at 25 °C (lu2007uncertaintyevaluationof pages 1-2, greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 4-5). Temperature control to ±0.5 °C is advisable, particularly for Mg(NO₃)₂ whose dRH/dT is approximately −0.3% RH/°C (greenspan1977humidityfixedpoints pages 4-5). Including a small circulating fan reduces equilibration time by 10–50× (rockland1960saturatedsaltsolutions pages 1-1). After opening a chamber to load or remove a sample, re-equilibration with fan assistance should be verified by monitoring the SHT45 sensor until readings stabilize to within ±0.5% RH. For gravimetric sorption on a few grams of powder, thin layers (< ~1 mm) on the balance pan will help ensure that vapor diffusion is not rate-limiting, as agreement with DVS is good when bed diffusivity exceeds ~10⁻⁹ m²/s (arlabosse2003comparisonbetweenstatic pages 9-13). NaCl powder should not be conditioned at the NaCl-saturated chamber (~75% RH) because this coincides with its own DRH, risking irreversible caking (langlet2011kineticsofdissolution pages 1-3, afrassiabian2019multiscaleinvestigationof pages 29-33). AlSi10Mg is the most moisture-sensitive of the metal AM powders and should be prioritized for humidity-dosing characterization (cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 10-11, cordova2020measuringthespreadability pages 1-2).

For **test (10)**, the Faraday cup should use a deep aluminium inner cup inside a grounded can, connected to a femtoampere-grade charge amplifier via guarded cabling (cruise2023triboelectricchargingof pages 47-51, currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 20-22). The LMP7721 (3 fA bias) or ADA4530-1 (~20 fA bias) are suitable op-amps for a home-built integrating charge amplifier, with feedback capacitor values chosen to match the expected charge range of ~0.1–100 nC per dose (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 17-20, currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 22-25). Driven guard traces, Teflon insulation, triaxial cables, and meticulous cleanliness are essential for meaningful sub-picoampere measurements (currentUnknownyearlmp77213femtoampereinput pages 20-22, mortuza2017picocurrentmeasurementchallenges pages 4-6). Expected charge-to-mass ratios from auger feeding through a PLA tube are likely in the 1–10 nC/g range for most powders, with significant humidity dependence (dani2024effectofmoisture pages 4-5, bailey1993chargingofsolids pages 6-9). Pairing Faraday-cup measurements before and after humidity conditioning will provide a direct link between tests (6) and (10).

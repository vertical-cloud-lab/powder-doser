## Verdict

The central explanation is **partially correct**, but its causal attribution is too strong. The uploaded twin shows that the 35 mg offset is necessary under that model. It does **not** show that the Kalman filter “buys speed, not safety.” The filter supplies the mass estimate used by the stop rule, and the current ablations change both feedback and stopping behavior. The claimed dominant 26 mg passive drain is generated by an unvalidated twin law.

The most serious hardware sensitivity is `BAL_TAU_S`: if the balance time constant is really ~0.16 s rather than 0.7 s, the filter’s lag correction and the apparent cost of using raw mass change by tens of milligrams. At 0.042 g/s, the difference in nominal correction is

$$
(0.70-0.16)\,\mathrm{s}\times 0.042\,\mathrm{g/s}=22.7\,\mathrm{mg}.
$$

The three bench summaries in `spotcheck_claims.md` have no uploaded raw observations, so I treated them as reported evidence, not independently verified results.

## C1. Attribution: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

**Reason.** The 35 mg term is the largest additive term and removing it changes nominal-twin overshoot from 31% to 94%. That supports “the fixed margin is necessary in this implementation.” It does not establish that the margin or tap endgame is *the* safety mechanism:

* A term’s magnitude is not its causal contribution. Crossing times are nonlinear, and a 5 mg shift can move a trajectory across a discrete avalanche or tap event.
* The reported 24%/33% shares are descriptive accounting, based on sums of marginal medians, not counterfactual safety effects.
* Removing the entire filter also raises overshoot to 76%, although that comparison is itself confounded.
* `tap_finish()` cannot remove an overshoot. It converts an intentional undershoot into a final dose, and caused roughly half of strict overshoots by a tap crossing the target.

**Correction.**

* In `diag_trickle_stages.py::report_budget`, lines 185–208, rename “KF-derived share” to **median threshold composition** and remove safety-attribution language such as “what each term is worth.”
* In `bangbang.py::trickle_tap`, lines 261–263, revise the comment to: “The fixed offset and uncertainty term bias the handoff earlier; their overshoot effects require paired counterfactual evaluation.”
* Replace the claim with: “In the present twin, the 35 mg offset is necessary, while the separate safety contributions of lag correction, rate prediction, covariance cushion, and tap policy have not been isolated.”

## C2. One-signedness: **WRONG as stated**

**Reason.** `r_hat*tau` is nonnegative because `MassRateLagKF.update()` clamps `r_hat` at zero (`bangbang.py`, line 100), and `k*sigma` and the fixed margin are also nonnegative. But one-signed terms do not make rate error harmless. Underestimating rate reduces the early-stop allowance and can cause a later cutoff and overshoot. The two-sided error in `m_hat` directly shifts the crossing time; its median absolute error is 9.0 mg.

The first-crossing operation also selects favorable noise excursions. This is an optional-stopping/threshold-crossing issue, not eliminated by making one term nonnegative.

**Correction.** In the explanation around `bangbang.py::trickle_tap`, lines 260–264, say:

> The rate contribution is constrained to be nonnegative, so it cannot explicitly command a stop beyond the uncorrected mass threshold. Rate underestimation can nevertheless remove needed afterflow compensation, and mass-estimation error remains two-sided. Safety must be assessed from the distribution of settled mass after first crossing.

## C3. “Self-correcting” uncertainty: **WRONG**

**Reason.** The suspected criticism is correct. For this linear filter, with fixed `F`, `H`, `Q`, and controller-selected `R`, the covariance follows a Riccati recursion independent of the innovation values. `pred_sigma()` (`bangbang.py`, lines 103–107) adapts only to:

1. the programmed quiet/noisy value of `R`;
2. elapsed predict steps and fresh versus missed measurement frames;
3. initialization and any changes in `F` caused by whether an input is supplied.

It does not increase because the observed balance samples happened to be erratic. The ~4.8 mg term is therefore a model-scheduled cushion, not an empirical data-quality monitor.

Removing it can still move strict overshoot from 31% to 47% because it shifts the first-crossing boundary by ~5 mg. That is comparable to the tolerance, can change which avalanche/tap is encountered, and changes handoff from −12.8 to −9.0 mg. The identical reported p95 of 15.3 mg also cautions against interpreting the percentage change as broad tail improvement. With 90 runs, approximate unpaired 95% intervals are wide: 31% corresponds to ~22–41%, and 47% to ~37–57%; the paired outcomes needed for a proper interval were not saved.

**Correction.**

* Change `diag_trickle_stages.py::report_budget`, line 196, from “grows when the filter is unsure” to “is determined by the assumed covariance model and update schedule.”
* To deliver the claimed behavior, log innovations $\nu_k=z_k-H\hat x^-_k$ and $S_k=HP^-_kH^T+R_k$. Monitor normalized innovation squared, $\nu_k^2/S_k$, and innovation autocorrelation.
* Use a bounded, slow covariance-matching update for `R`, preferably by actuator/rpm regime, rather than reacting to one residual. For example, estimate innovation variance over a prespecified window and set $R_k$ to the positive residual after subtracting $HP^-H^T$, with lower/upper bounds and forgetting.
* If residuals are colored, augment the observation model with a vibration/bias state. Simply inflating scalar `R` does not make colored noise white.
* Validate normalized innovation squared coverage on bench data and normalized estimation error squared against twin truth. Adaptive `R` must not silently make the filter overconfident.

## C4. Continuum-rate regime: **WRONG / unsupported**

**Reason.** There is no principled basis in the uploads for the 0.01 g/s boundary, and “4× above it” does not establish a continuum regime. A rate can remain meaningful as a long-window conditional mean while being a poor 0.30 s committed-mass predictor.

A useful marked-point-process criterion is based on the prediction horizon $T$:

$$
N_T=\lambda T,\qquad
P(N_T=0)=e^{-\lambda T},\qquad
\mathrm{CV}[M_T]=\frac{\sqrt{E[Y^2]}}{E[Y]\sqrt{\lambda T}},
$$

where $Y$ is event mass. A continuum approximation is credible only when many effectively independent events fall inside the relevant horizon and their upper prediction quantile is small relative to the allowed error.

Using the reported salt marks as a rough proxy, $E[Y]=6.4$ mg, SD = 15.9 mg, and mean flow 42 mg/s imply $\lambda\approx6.6$ events/s. The 0.30 s lookahead then contains only ~2 expected events, with a compound-Poisson SD of ~24 mg and ~14% probability of no event. At 18 rpm there are only 0.72 commanded 45° increments per 0.30 s. Those calculations do **not** show a comfortable continuum regime. They are provisional because the n=64 increment test may not match the 20° cutoff condition and its raw data were not uploaded.

Published low-dose feeder work explicitly describes intermittent granular flow from small screw systems and evaluates rate over chosen aggregation windows rather than asserting a universal flow cutoff (Fathollahi et al., 2020, *AAPS PharmSciTech*, [doi:10.1208/s12249-020-01835-5](https://doi.org/10.1208/s12249-020-01835-5)).

**Correction.** Replace C4’s fixed 0.01 g/s rule with an empirically estimated event model by powder, tilt, rpm, and lip load. Handover when the upper quantile of mass delivered over the stop horizon exceeds the remaining one-sided error budget, or when $N_T$ is too small for the validated rate model. That may or may not occur near 0.02 g/s.

## C5. Ablation inference: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, materially confounded**

### (a) Fixed margin

Supported only as: “the fixed margin is necessary in the current twin/controller combination.” Removing it gives 94% overshoot, but this does not prove it is the sole safety mechanism or that 35 mg is appropriate on hardware.

### (b) Mass lag correction

Plausible but not isolated. The ~29 mg handoff difference comes from comparing −12.8 mg with +16.4 mg across controllers that differ in mass, rate, covariance, cutoff timing, and feedback trajectory. It cannot be assigned mainly to mass lag from this table.

### (c) Filtered rate buys speed, not safety

Not supported. `_rawrate_update()` (`diag_trickle_stages.py`, lines 226–233) returns the crude rate to both:

* the cutoff statistic at `bangbang.py`, line 263; and
* the PI error at lines 269–276.

It therefore changes rpm, lip inventory, vibration, trajectory duration, and stop statistic simultaneously. Calling the raw-rate controller “safer” because it has 21% versus 31% overshoot is a confounded policy comparison, not an estimator-channel ablation.

**Correction and clean design.**

1. Refactor `trickle_tap()` to accept separate `r_feedback` and `r_stop` signals.
2. Run a 2×2 factorial: filtered/raw feedback × filtered/raw stop statistic, leaving mass estimation fixed. Add lag-corrected versus raw mass as a separate factor.
3. For a pure stopping-rule comparison, record each nominal pre-stop trajectory and evaluate all stop statistics at every frame. Because alternative stopping changes future physics, replay the simulator from a saved state at each counterfactual stop using the same exogenous random streams.
4. Use common random numbers within powder × target × context × seed. The simulator already separates mechanism-specific random streams (`powder_sim.py`, lines 200–213), which helps, but different actuation trajectories still require state snapshot/replay.
5. Save paired outcomes. For overshoot incidence use stratified McNemar differences or a paired cluster bootstrap; for signed error, positive excess, and time use paired differences with bootstrap confidence intervals. Treat powder/target/context as strata or hierarchical effects. Correct the family of planned comparisons, for example with Holm’s method.

Fifteen seeds per cell are adequate for detecting gross failures such as 94% versus 31%, but not for a stable claim about 21% versus 31% or tails. Pooling to 90 does not erase the six-cell structure. Determine seed count from the precision required for the paired overshoot-risk difference and upper quantile; hundreds per cell may be needed for a hard safety claim.

## C6. Dominant post-cutoff uncertainty: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED in the twin only**

**Reason.** The reported median +26.0 mg and p95 +52.4 mg are correctly computed for this twin. Their attribution to passive lip drain depends directly on provisional `powder_sim.py::_substep`, lines 457–470, especially `smooth = 1.2*s*s*...` at line 462 and the avalanche hazard. Therefore “dominant real uncertainty” and “the margin is sized against drain” are unsupported hardware claims.

The reported bench observation of no creep after ~2 s does not by itself contradict 26 mg released during the first 1.2 s; a reservoir can drain early and then stop. It does make a long continuing tail unlikely. If real passive drain is negligible, the causal story about the margin collapses. The need to account for in-flight material, balance error, discrete slugs, and the tap endgame remains.

**Minimal discriminating experiment.** At a fixed 20° tilt, establish several controlled lip loads, run steady trickle at several rates, command an abrupt auger halt, and record at least 10 s. Randomize and replicate three post-stop actions: hold 20°, move to 0°, and a sham servo move. Use an independent downstream collector or rapid cup swap to separate material already in flight from later outlet discharge; synchronize command, servo position, and 10.4 Hz balance data. Primary outputs are cumulative mass in 0–0.3, 0.3–1.2, 1.2–2, and 2–10 s, plus probability and mass of discrete releases. The proposed “halt at 20° and log 10 s” is the minimum first test, but without separating balance dynamics and in-flight mass it does not identify passive lip drain cleanly.

I found published work on cohesion-dependent screw-feeder flow regimes and complex retained-flow patterns, but **no directly applicable peer-reviewed measurement of post-halt discharge from an inclined auger lip/tube outlet**. Hou et al. show that cohesion creates distinct screw-feeder flow regimes, but do not validate this twin’s shutdown drain law (2014, *Powder Technology*, [doi:10.1016/j.powtec.2014.01.062](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.powtec.2014.01.062)). The claimed post-halt behavior therefore needs the bench experiment rather than an analogy to the literature.

## C7. Overshoot taxonomy and metrics: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

**Reason.** The split is useful descriptively: 11% entered the tap phase already over and all finished over, while ~11% of all doses were handed off short but crossed during tapping, with median final +1.4 mg. Roughly half the strict overshoots are therefore small tap crossings.

But “all consequential overshoots trace to late cutoff, not rate” is not established. `late` in `report_stages()` lines 168–169 is defined by truth at cutoff **or tap entry** exceeding target. It identifies stage, not root cause. Rate error, mass error, afterflow, and the provisional drain can all produce a late handoff.

Strict overshoot and ±5 mg yield are not a sufficient pair. A symmetric tolerance rate can conceal the hard asymmetry. Report, by powder/target/context:

* $P(E>0)$, the hard-constraint violation rate;
* $P(E>5\,\mathrm{mg})$ and $P(E<-5\,\mathrm{mg})$ separately;
* median, p95/p99, and maximum positive excess;
* expected positive excess $E[\max(E,0)]$;
* probability of landing in the actually acceptable one-sided interval, apparently $[-5,0]$ mg if *any* overshoot is forbidden;
* completion/stall rate, number of taps, and cycle time;
* confidence bounds for all safety probabilities and upper quantiles.

Continuous loss-in-weight feeder studies commonly report mean relative deviation from setpoint and relative standard deviation over a specified time window (Fathollahi et al., 2021, *AAPS PharmSciTech*, [doi:10.1208/s12249-021-02104-9](https://doi.org/10.1208/s12249-021-02104-9)). Those are useful for continuous feed-rate regulation but are insufficient for this one-sided batch endpoint.

**Correction.** Rename the two groups “over before tap control” and “crossed during tap control,” and remove the causal phrase “not the rate estimate” unless a mediation/counterfactual analysis supports it.

## C8. Recommendations: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED; priorities need reordering**

### 1. Add `AF0 ≈ 15 mg`

Conceptually sound only if the reported stop-response regression is replicated and its residual distribution is characterized. The raw regression data, uncertainty, powder generalization, and validation are absent. Adding 15 mg while retaining a 35 mg margin may double-count the same afterflow.

Replace the cutoff with a calibrated conditional upper-quantile model such as

$$
\widehat M_{\text{settled}}=\n\hat m + \widehat{AF}_0 + \widehat\tau r,
$$

plus a residual one-sided safety quantile. Do not label both a fitted afterflow intercept and an unexplained fixed margin as independent physics.

### 2. Tilt horizontal immediately

Promising but **not yet safe**. Servo motion causes vibration and changes outlet geometry/capacity; the observed 25.4 mg release on a tilt move is exactly the failure mode that must be measured. Also, `trickle_tap()` currently waits 1.2 s at 20° (`bangbang.py`, lines 278–283); horizontal motion happens only inside `tap_finish()` (`controllers.py`, lines 62–65).

Test paired randomized post-stop actions with matched initial lip load before changing the order. Include movement direction, speed, and acceleration.

### 3. Powder-specific margin from drain

Do not calibrate only “drain.” Calibrate total post-command mass conditional on powder, flow, tilt, lip state, and movement policy, then use its upper conditional quantile. Drain, free fall, screw discharge, and estimator error need not be separately identifiable for a robust cutoff predictor.

### 4. Increment-and-measure below ~0.02 g/s

The architecture is appropriate for a hard one-sided constraint and the reported highly variable slugs, but 0.02 g/s is not yet justified. Select handover from measured increment-yield and afterflow distributions. An increment must itself have a sufficiently small upper quantile; the reported 15.9 mg SD for 45° salt increments suggests 45° is much too coarse for a 5 mg endpoint.

### Missing standard practices

* Explicit coarse-to-fine/dribble handover, with a final phase whose maximum credible slug fits the remaining one-sided budget.
* Learned in-flight/free-fall or settled-mass cutoff compensation. Predicting mass already committed after shutoff is standard in dynamic filling; the key is bench identification and ongoing cutoff-weight correction, not necessarily a Kalman filter.
* Batch-to-batch adaptation based on **settled cutoff error**, with bounded updates and holdout validation.
* A physical cutoff/gate or outlet geometry that arrests discharge, if tilt/auger stop cannot bound the final slug.
* Separate safety qualification from mean-cycle-time tuning.

Automatic weight-filler research confirms that actuator/load-cell vibration requires identified dynamic models rather than a single static-noise number (Tiboni et al., 2020, *Electronics*, [doi:10.3390/electronics9060995](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics9060995)).

## C9. Dead feed-factor margin: **SUPPORTED**

**Reason.** The term at `bangbang.py::trickle_tap`, line 260, is identically zero for the reported range: median `ff=0.113`, maximum 0.198, threshold 0.30 g/rev. The diagnostic confirms 0% activation in 360 twin doses. The comments at lines 256–259 therefore falsely describe current adaptation.

**Correction.** Delete the term and its adaptation claim for now, leaving `margin = cutoff_margin_g`. Reintroduce a feed-factor or flow-dependent term only after fitting its relationship to settled post-stop mass on training data and validating the one-sided residual quantile on held-out powders/runs. Merely lowering the threshold to make the term fire would be tuning without evidence.

## C10. Noise model: **WRONG attribution; model concern is valid**

**Reason.** `NOISY_SD=8 mg` is selected whenever the controller reports actuation (`bangbang.py`, lines 87–100; `controllers.py`, lines 36–37). This coarse switching does not prove that under-trusting a quiet scale causes the 9 mg mass error. Competing causes include:

* colored vibration, whereas the filter assumes white measurement noise;
* wrong `Q` and rate time constant;
* wrong feed-factor input model at `bangbang.py`, lines 95–97;
* wrong balance time constant;
* discrete avalanches absent from the Gaussian state model;
* bias/drift in `powder_sim.py::_update_balance`, lines 565–605.

In the twin, vibration during any auger operation is already set to at least 6 mg and is colored (`powder_sim.py`, lines 569–596), so 8 mg is not obviously excessive at 18 rpm.

**Correction.** Add innovation, normalized innovation squared, and autocorrelation logging to `MassRateLagKF.update()`. In twin validation also log normalized estimation error squared against true `[m,r,b]`. An empty-hopper rpm sweep is useful for mechanical/electrical vibration, but insufficient: repeat with representative powder flow, tilt changes, taps, and different lip impacts. Fit `R(rpm, actuator, tilt-motion)` or a colored disturbance state only if those data support it.

## C11. Estimator/architecture: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

The augmented state is a sensible observer structure, but the implementation is not a fully consistent constrained estimator and “lag-free” is conditional on a correct balance model.

### (i) Rate clamp

Clamping `x[1]` after the update at `bangbang.py`, line 100, while leaving `P` unchanged makes the reported Gaussian mean/covariance pair inconsistent. After an active clamp, `pred_sigma()` may be miscalibrated because it still includes covariance from the unconstrained posterior. This matters directly to C3.

Use a constrained Kalman projection with covariance adjustment, a truncated-normal moment update, or a state parameterization/model that respects nonnegative flow. At minimum, log clamp frequency and exclude `k*sigma` safety claims until coverage is tested.

### (ii) Near-zero balance-state process noise

`Q[2,2]=1e-8` at lines 77–80 is not inherently numerically unsafe: the balance state receives uncertainty through coupling from mass. The concern is model rigidity and conditioning over long runs if the sensor dynamics are imperfect. Use exact discrete-time `F,Q`, Joseph-form covariance updates or a square-root filter, enforce covariance symmetry/positive semidefiniteness, and run condition-number/coverage tests. Increase balance-state model error only from residual evidence, not as a numerical patch.

### (iii) Lag deconvolution

Augmenting the plant/observer with a known sensor-dynamics state and observing that state is standard state-space practice. It is preferable to direct inverse filtering, which amplifies noise. The exact three-state arrangement is not uniquely standard, and it should be called a **model-based estimate of current mass**, not “lag-free true mass.” Better-conditioned options include an exactly discretized augmented continuous-time model, a steady-state or square-root Kalman observer, or joint offline identification of sensor poles followed by a fixed augmented observer. A two-state direct inverse $m=b+\tau\dot b$ is simpler but generally noisier.

Dynamic load-cell compensation literature likewise treats the sensor response as an identified dynamic system rather than assuming an exact algebraic inverse (Piskorowski and Barcinski, 2008, *Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing*, [doi:10.1016/j.ymssp.2008.01.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymssp.2008.01.001)).

### (iv) `tau_bal`

This is critical. The twin and filter both use 0.7 s, so the simulation grants the estimator a correctly specified sensor. If hardware is ~0.16 s, conclusions about lag correction, raw-balance failure, `m_hat` error, threshold composition, and speed/safety all require rerunning. The potential correction mismatch is ~22.7 mg at the reported cutoff flow, larger than the 5 mg tolerance and the 12.7 mg rate term.

**Correction.** Replace the hard-coded `BAL_TAU_S=0.7` (`bangbang.py`, line 48) with a configuration identified for the actual HR-100A mode. Fit step/drop responses across load, filter setting, serial mode, and vibration condition; inspect whether a first-order model is adequate; report confidence intervals. Then run a sensitivity grid over plausible time constants and mismatches between plant and filter.

## C12. Headline: **PARTIALLY CORRECT, but misleading**

The accurate part is that the filter supports rate feedback and provides a model-based current-mass estimate, while an intentional undershoot and at-rest incremental finish are sensible for an irreversible endpoint.

The misleading part is “the KF buys speed, not safety.” The current evidence does not isolate that claim. The filter’s mass estimate is inside the safety-critical stop rule, the rate ablation changes the feedback trajectory, and both the filter and twin share an unverified 0.7 s balance constant. The tap phase also causes some overshoots rather than preventing them.

**Single most important correction:**

> Say that the twin demonstrates the necessity of an early-stop allowance, but does not identify the filter as speed-only. The filter’s separate mass, rate-feedback, and rate-lookahead contributions must be tested with paired, unconfounded counterfactuals after the hardware balance and post-stop response are identified.

## Ranked changes

### (a) Justified by the uploaded evidence

1. **Refactor and rerun an unconfounded paired factorial ablation.** Separate mass estimate, feedback rate, stop-rule rate, and covariance cushion; save paired outcomes and report confidence intervals for one-sided metrics.
2. **Replace the current scorecard.** Make hard-overshoot probability, >5 mg overshoot probability, upper excess quantiles, expected positive excess, one-sided acceptable yield, and cycle time the primary outputs. Preserve powder/target/context strata.
3. **Remove false adaptation claims.** Delete the inactive feed-factor margin and describe `k*sigma` as model-scheduled, not innovation-adaptive. Add innovation/NIS/whiteness diagnostics and log rate-clamp activity.
4. **Treat the low-flow tail as discrete until shown otherwise.** Add a configurable increment-and-settle mode and determine its handover from measured event/slug upper quantiles, not the unsupported 0.01 or 0.02 g/s cutoffs.

### (b) Changes requiring a bench measurement first

1. **Identify the HR-100A dynamics.** Measurement: replicated step/drop tests in the exact operating/filter/serial configuration, under quiet and representative vibration, fitting first- and higher-order models. Then retune and sensitivity-test the observer.
2. **Identify total post-stop mass and its components.** Measurement: randomized abrupt stops over powder × rpm × tilt × lip load, with 10 s logging and an independent collector/cup-swap to separate in-flight mass from outlet discharge. Fit an upper-quantile settled-afterflow model.
3. **Test post-stop tilt motion before changing sequencing.** Measurement: paired hold-20° versus 20°→0° versus sham moves, varying servo speed/acceleration and matched lip load; measure release probability and p95/p99 mass.
4. **Characterize endpoint slug distributions.** Measurement: many small auger increments and taps at candidate angles/tilts for each powder, including zero yields and dependence on lip load. Choose the largest action whose upper mass quantile fits the remaining one-sided budget.
5. **Only then replace the 35 mg constant.** Use the validated upper conditional quantile of total committed mass plus estimator residual, with a documented target exceedance probability. Do not add the reported 15 mg intercept on top of 35 mg without checking double counting.

### Discretionary analytical decisions

* Treated simulator results as evidence about the implemented twin, not as validated hardware performance.
* Treated the three bench summaries in `spotcheck_claims.md` as reported summaries because their raw observations were not uploaded.
* Used the 0.30 s controller lookahead as the relevant horizon for the marked-point-process calculation.
* Used a compound-Poisson approximation only as a diagnostic; did not assume the 45° increment experiment exactly represents the 20° trickle condition.
* Used Jeffreys-style approximate 95% intervals to illustrate the precision of unpaired ablation proportions; recommended paired stratified inference for the actual rerun.
* Recommended upper-quantile cutoff calibration rather than a mean-afterflow correction because overshoot is a hard asymmetric constraint.
* Did not claim published support for passive post-halt inclined-lip drainage because no directly applicable peer-reviewed experiment was found.

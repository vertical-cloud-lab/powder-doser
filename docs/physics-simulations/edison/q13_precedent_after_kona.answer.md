We have dosed 13 powders (NaCl table salt, gas-atomized AlSi10Mg and Al 4047, two silicon grades, rice flour, xanthan gum, carboxymethyl cellulose, BaCl2 and others) and record a 'dosing fingerprint': mass per auger revolution versus tilt and speed, the mass freed by one tap, the afterflow time constant after the auger stops, and the fraction of increments with no flow. We want to (i) infer powder properties (cohesion, friction, moisture sensitivity, packing) from dosing data by simulation-based Bayesian calibration, (ii) check the inferred values against standard tests (ASTM D7891 FT4 shear cell, D6773 Schulze ring shear, B213 Hall and B964 Carney flow, B212/B527 apparent and tap density, angle of repose, B214 sieving, B822 laser diffraction, loss on drying), and (iii) anticipate spreadability in powder-bed additive manufacturing. No. ## Literature assessment

**I found no study that demonstrates the complete workflow described:** infer DEM or continuum contact parameters *primarily from screw/auger dosing responses for several distinct powders*, quantify parameter uncertainty with Bayesian calibration, and then test predictions against previously unused shear, funnel-flow, density, and powder-spreading measurements. This is a conclusion about the literature located and examined, not proof that no such study exists. Crucially, however, **doser-output-based contact-parameter calibration itself is not new**: two independent precedents beyond El Kassem and colleagues directly fit DEM parameters to measured screw-dosing output. (imole2016reprintof"experiments pages 1-5, imole2016reprintof"experiments pages 34-37, shen2021calibrationofdiscrete pages 1-2, shen2021calibrationofdiscrete pages 8-10)

*Table: Comparison of feeder-based DEM calibration studies and adjacent Bayesian or powder-spreading work. The matrix distinguishes calibration data from validation data and shows why none implements the complete proposed workflow.*

**Closest feeder-first precedents.** Imole *et al.* (2016, *Powder Technology*, doi:10.1016/j.powtec.2015.07.052) measured cocoa-powder mass per screw dose and calibrated interparticle cohesion and friction against it; their published calibration plot places experimental mass per dose directly against simulated responses over those contact parameters. They also investigated screw speed, dose duration, pitch and initial powder mass, and observed arching/blockage. This is particularly close to a dosing fingerprint, but concerns one powder, does not report Bayesian posteriors, and does not establish agreement with held-out standardized flow tests or recoating. (imole2016reprintof"experiments pages 1-5, imole2016reprintof"experiments pages 5-11, imole2016reprintof"experiments pages 34-37, imole2016reprintof"experiments media 1771b355)

Shen, Li and Xu (2021, *Journal of Engineering*, doi:10.1155/2021/6671511) **explicitly calibrated directly to wheat-flour screw-conveyor mass flow**, using experimental-design screening and response-surface optimization to select JKR surface energy and flour–flour and flour–steel static-friction coefficients. The fitted-flow comparison differed by 1.37%; comparisons at other screw speeds, 60–140 rpm, differed by less than 3%. Those are valuable operating-condition checks, not independent shear-cell, Hall/Carney, tapped-density or spreading validations; the study treats one powder and reports an optimum rather than a Bayesian parameter posterior. (shen2021calibrationofdiscrete pages 1-2, shen2021calibrationofdiscrete pages 5-7, shen2021calibrationofdiscrete pages 8-10)

**Why other apparently close studies do not answer the question.** El Kassem *et al.* fitted auger-derived angle of repose, bulk density and flow rate for a free-flowing material using design of experiments and regression; their additional 400–800-rpm checks remained within the auger-response family. In the opposite calibration direction, Tekeste *et al.* (2018) fitted corn friction parameters to two *angle-of-repose* experiments, then used commercial-auger flow as validation. Gómez *et al.* (2024) used FT4 flow-energy and rotating-drum responses to calibrate powder DEM models, then tested lactose-based and paracetamol feeder behavior. Thus, multiple materials appearing in a feeder paper does not mean multiple materials were **inferred from feeder data**. (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 1-2, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 12-13, tekeste2018discreteelementmodel pages 1-3, tekeste2018discreteelementmodel pages 8-9, gomez2024investigatingscrewagitatorspeed pages 6-7)

**Bayesian inference and spreading have been demonstrated separately, not in that combination.** Cheng *et al.* (2019, doi:10.1016/j.cma.2019.01.027) inferred distributions and correlations of DEM stiffness and friction parameters using cyclic-compression stress–strain measurements, **not dosing data**. Katagiri *et al.* (2025, doi:10.3390/jmmp9120383) used *Bayesian optimization* to select cohesion and rolling-resistance parameters from metal-powder consolidation void ratio; this optimizes an objective rather than reporting the requested posterior over contact parameters. Applying that parameter set to spreading produced excessively cohesive, nonuniform beds, illustrating that a fit in one deformation regime need not transfer to another. Independent multi-powder studies measure actual recoating quality—for example, Brocksieper *et al.* (2026) tested three metal powders—but do not infer their contacts from screw-doser output. (cheng2019aniterativebayesian pages 1-2, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 2-4, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 1-2, brocksieper2026investigationofflowability pages 1-2)

**Defensible novelty claim.** The advance would be *multi-powder, feeder-first, uncertainty-quantified inverse calibration of effective contact parameters from a multivariate dosing fingerprint, prospectively validated against tests withheld from fitting and against measured recoated-layer quality*—not the first use of a screw feeder to calibrate DEM. The examined precedents do not establish that tilt response, solenoid-tap release, post-stop afterflow, and zero-flow increment frequency have jointly been used as inverse-calibration observables. In reporting results, distinguish an inferred, model- and wall-material-dependent **effective** friction/cohesion parameter from an independently measured intrinsic powder property; likewise, moisture sensitivity requires controlled moisture variation, and a claim about spreadability requires an actual spreading measurement rather than funnel flow alone. The observed failure of consolidation-calibrated parameters to transfer to spreading makes that last independent test especially consequential. (imole2016reprintof"experiments pages 1-5, shen2021calibrationofdiscrete pages 1-2, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 12-13, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 11-14, brocksieper2026investigationofflowability pages 1-2)

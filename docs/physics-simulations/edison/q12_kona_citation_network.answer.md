## 1. What El Kassem et al. KONA 2021 Did

**Dosing device and geometry.** The apparatus was a vertical, motor-driven micro-auger doser designed for pharmaceutical powder filling. A cylindrical container (64 mm diameter, 224 mm height) fed powder through a stainless-steel funnel (63 mm upper diameter, 6 mm lower opening, 45° inclination) onto a borosilicate-glass catching container placed on a balance that recorded discharged mass every 0.2 s (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 3-5).

**Powder.** A single powder was tested: SpheroLac 100, a dry, non-cohesive, free-flowing crystalline lactose monohydrate (MEGGLE), with volume-weighted mean diameter D[4,3] = 115 μm, aspect ratio 0.746, roundness 0.891, and particle density 1.5361 g/cm³ (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 3-5, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 8-10).

**Measured bulk responses.** Three experimentally accessible responses were used for calibration: angle of repose (AoR), bulk density (BD, measured in a glass tube filled with 6 g of powder), and steady-state mass flow rate (ṁ), measured at 400, 600, and 800 rpm. A fourth response, normalized average translational kinetic energy (NA-TKE), was studied in the DEM but could not be measured experimentally (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 6-8, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 8-10). Experimental results were: BD = 0.758 g/cm³; ṁ = 0.2568, 0.3744, and 0.5074 g/s; AoR ≈ 33.2–33.4° (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 8-10).

**DEM code and contact models.** Simulations used LIGGGHTS-Premium 4.X with Hertz–Mindlin normal/tangential contact mechanics and Coulomb friction. Rolling resistance was handled by the EPSD2 (elastic–plastic spring–dashpot) model with damping torque disabled. No cohesion model was applied because the powder was treated as non-cohesive. Particle size was coarse-grained sixfold following Bierwisch et al. (2009), and the timestep was kept below 20% of the Rayleigh critical timestep. CAD was done in SolidWorks, meshing in Gmsh, and post-processing in ParaView (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 5-6, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 5-6).

**Parameters varied and fixed.** Four factors were varied in a DoE: auger speed ω (400, 600, 800 rpm), particle–particle static friction μ_s,pp (0.3, 0.55, 0.8), particle–wall static friction μ_s,pw (0.1, 0.25, 0.4), and particle–particle rolling friction μ_r,pp (0.1, 0.4, 0.7). Fixed parameters included Young's moduli, Poisson ratios, restitution coefficients, particle density and scaled size, filling mass, gravity, and the numerical timestep (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 6-8).

**Design of experiments.** A quadratic D-optimal design required only 20 simulation runs (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 12-13, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 6-8).

**Regression models.** Separate quadratic multivariate response surfaces were fitted for AoR, BD, ṁ, and NA-TKE, with adjusted R² values of 0.975, 0.983, 0.998, and 0.986, respectively. Pareto charts and adjusted-response graphs ranked parameter effects (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 8-10).

**Contour-overlay calibration.** For each pair of calibration parameters (μ_r,pp and μ_s,pp), predicted contour plots of the three responses were superimposed. The experimental target values defined contour lines on each surface; their intersection dramatically narrowed the admissible region. Cornerstone optimization software then selected the minimum-error parameter combination. At 800 rpm the optimized combination used μ_s,pw = 0.347 as an optimized wall-friction value (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 10-12).

**Number of simulations.** Twenty DoE runs plus three confirmation runs (one at each speed) totaling 23 simulations (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 6-8, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 10-12).

**Validation.** Three confirmation DEM runs at 400, 600, and 800 rpm compared simulated AoR, BD, ṁ, and dosing time with experiments. Across 12 comparisons, the overall mean deviation was 0.87% and the maximum was 2.86% (for dosing time at 600 rpm) (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 12-13, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 10-12).

**Stated limitations.** The method was demonstrated only for one non-cohesive, free-flowing powder. The authors stated that future work should study more material properties and bulk behaviors and extend the method to cohesive powders (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 12-13). No held-out independent test types (shear cell, Hall flow, etc.) were used for validation; confirmation reused the same response types (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 10-12).

## 4. Follow-Up by the Same Authors

Bilal El Kassem was a PhD candidate at Robert Bosch Packaging Technology GmbH in Stuttgart, supervised by Prof. Bernd Markert at the Institute of General Mechanics, RWTH Aachen University (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 14-16). The doctoral thesis is titled *"Machine design for auger dosing of powders by utilizing DEM simulations and analysis"* (RWTH Aachen University, 2021) and is explicitly cited by Kruppa et al. (2022) (kruppa2022increasingthedosing pages 1-11).

The publication sequence progressed as follows: (1) PAMM 2018 introduced auger-dosing DEM with DoE for AoR calibration; (2) JDDST 2020 (doi:10.1016/j.jddst.2020.101950) applied multivariate statistics to auger-dosing quality; (3) CPM 2020 (doi:10.1007/s40571-020-00315-8) extended the MVRA framework to cohesive powders using the SJKR cohesion model, examining six DEM inputs and five responses including porosity (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 21-23, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 5-6, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 1-2); (4) KONA 2021 narrowed the method to a single-powder, multi-response auger calibration; and (5) the vertical loss-in-weight feeder prototype (doi:10.1080/10837450.2021.1901915) extended the device-design strand to gravimetric feeding.

The CPM 2020 paper is the most relevant extension to cohesive powders: it used SJKR cohesion energy density as a varied input alongside static and rolling friction, restitution, Young's modulus, and particle size, applied to angle-of-repose tests rather than to the auger-dosing setup itself (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 21-23, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 5-6). The doctoral thesis presumably consolidated these strands but was not accessible for full review.

No further publications by El Kassem, Salloum, Brinz, Heider, or Markert extending the auger-based DEM calibration to inverse identification, Bayesian inference, AM powders, or additional dosing devices were found in the retrieved literature.

### Novelty Claims to Make

The user's work can defensibly claim the following novelties relative to this citation network:

1. **A richer dosing-fingerprint excitation/response protocol** comprising speed-dependent mass-per-revolution at multiple tilt angles (0–30°), solenoid-tapped mass release, afterflow time constant, and zero-flow increment fraction — none of which appear in El Kassem or any retrieved citing work.

While Bayesian DEM calibration exists (Cheng et al., GrainLearning), it has not been applied to an auger or screw-feeder response in the retrieved literature.

6. **Integration into a self-driving laboratory** for alloy discovery, using an open-source, 3D-printed doser under closed-loop balance control with multi-task Bayesian optimization across powders.

### Claims to Drop or Soften

- **Do not claim** to be the first to use an auger doser as a DEM calibration device — El Kassem et al. (KONA 2021) explicitly proposed this (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 2-3).
- **Soften** any claim of first use of multiple bulk responses for DEM calibration — Roessler et al. (2018) and El Kassem et al. (2021) both demonstrated multi-response contour overlays (kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 10-12, kassem2021asemiautomateddem pages 13-14).
- **Soften** any claim of first Bayesian DEM calibration — Cheng et al. (2018, 2024) established Bayesian DEM inference; the user's novelty is applying it to auger-dosing fingerprints for property inference.
- **Soften** any claim of first closed-loop powder dosing — Kruppa et al. (2022) demonstrated inline density-regulated screw dosing (kruppa2022increasingthedosing pages 1-11); the novelty is the specific open-source platform and the use of dosing dynamics as a mechanistic probe.
- **Do not claim** first DEM calibration for AM powders — Girnth et al. (2024), Sani et al. (2026), Meier et al. (2019), and others have parameterized DEM for AM feedstocks. The novelty is inferring AM-relevant properties from auger-dosing fingerprints and predicting spreadability from those inferred properties.
- **Soften** the claim of a parameter-free DEM prediction (105 mg/rev for salt) — specify all independently sourced inputs and clarify that this applies to one specific non-cohesive powder configuration; do not imply general parameter-free predictability.

In summary, the user's strongest novelty claim lies in the *combination* of (a) the enriched dosing-fingerprint protocol with tilt, tapping, afterflow, and intermittency responses, (b) Bayesian inverse inference with uncertainty quantification applied to that fingerprint, (c) systematic fingerprinting across a broad set of powders including metal AM feedstocks, (d) held-out validation against standard powder-characterization tests, and (e) downstream prediction of AM-relevant spreadability — none of which has been demonstrated as an integrated package in the existing auger-dosing DEM literature.

## DEM Contact Parameter Calibration for Cohesive Powders: Tests, Sensitivity, Identifiability, and Surrogate/Bayesian Methods

### 1. Overview of Calibration Strategy

DEM calibration for cohesive powders typically follows a staged approach. First, non-cohesive parameters (sliding friction, rolling friction, restitution, Young's modulus) are calibrated using flow-property and bench-scale tests; then cohesive parameters (surface energy, cohesion energy density) are introduced while keeping the dry parameters fixed (coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34). Restitution is often obtained from particle drop tests, particle–wall sliding friction from Jenike direct-shear or inclined-wall tests, and Young's modulus from literature or nano-indentation rather than from bulk flow tests (coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34). This staged strategy is necessary because the parameter space is high-dimensional and individual tests cannot uniquely resolve all parameters simultaneously.

**Key findings by test type:**

**Shear cells (Schulze ring shear, FT4 rotational shear).** In a 32-run, five-factor full-factorial DEM screening of the Schulze ring shear test, particle sliding friction was the dominant factor for pre-shear stress, while surface energy was the most important factor for incipient-flow (yield) stress (chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 3-5, chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 5-6). Young's modulus became statistically significant for incipient flow at high cohesion, but restitution and Poisson's ratio were non-significant for mean stress (chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 5-6). Rolling friction increased critical-state stress from 1008 to 1183 Pa in a parallel-plate shear-cell study, while sliding friction effects saturated above approximately 0.4 (karkala2021analysisofpowder pages 33-37).

**FT4 dynamic flow / dynamic yield strength (DYS).** Surface energy (cohesion) is overwhelmingly dominant: increasing it from baseline raised DYS by 274.3% (495 → 1853 Pa), followed by rolling friction at 37.0%, shear modulus at −19.2%, sliding friction at −18.8%, and restitution at only −4.6% (karkala2021analysisofpowder pages 33-37).

**Angle of repose (fixed funnel, lifting cylinder, rotating drum).** For free-flowing powders, static friction is the critical parameter with a positive power-law dependence on AoR (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 1-2). For cohesive powders, rolling friction has a substantial impact while static friction and particle size play minor roles (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 1-2). Cohesion controls heap curvature, clumping, and collapse onset, but a scalar AoR cannot uniquely separate cohesion from friction (roessler2019demparametercalibration pages 2-3, roessler2019demparametercalibration pages 1-2, gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 1-3). Different AoR measurement methods (height/radius ratio, full-slope regression, half-slope regression) yield different "best" parameter sets—for Ti6Al4V powder, the image-based SAD metric favored low μs, low μr, high γ, while the height/radius AoR method favored high μs, high μr, low γ (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 12-15).

**Oedometer / uniaxial compression / bulk density.** Young's modulus is the most significant parameter for the oedometer force–displacement response, as confirmed by Sobol sensitivity analysis, while rolling friction dominates the pile experiment (jahn2023onthesensitivity pages 1-2, jahn2023onthesensitivity pages 3-5). For loose packing porosity, both static and rolling friction coefficients are highly influential (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 1-2).

**Powder spreading in AM.** For fine metal powders (~30 μm), adhesive forces exceed gravity by roughly an order of magnitude, making cohesion/surface energy the dominant parameter controlling mean packing fraction and surface roughness (meier2021physics‐basedmodelingand pages 2-4). Sliding friction controls particle–substrate and particle–recoater interactions (zhang2017machinelearningenabled pages 4-7).

### 3. Parameter Degeneracy and Identifiability

A central finding across the literature is that **no single test can uniquely identify all DEM contact parameters**. Specific non-identifiability issues include:

- **Angle of repose alone** produces a large solution manifold: different combinations of μs, μr, and γ yield the same scalar AoR (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 1-3, rackl2017amethodicalcalibration pages 1-2). Rackl and Hanley showed that substantially different parameter sets produce similar AoR and bulk density simultaneously, and found no correlation between static and rolling friction despite this sometimes being assumed (rackl2017amethodicalcalibration pages 1-2). Gaboriault et al. demonstrated that four different AoR measurement methods selected four distinct parameter clusters for the same Ti6Al4V powder (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 12-15).

- **Sliding and rolling friction** are confounded in most flow tests because both resist relative particle motion. In the El-Kassem study, the same AoR could be achieved with various μs–μr combinations (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 1-2). Coetzee and Scheffler found that slump and hopper tests required different rolling-friction values for the same sliding friction, indicating poor transferability (coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34).

- **Cohesion and friction** are degenerate in heap tests: a more cohesive powder with lower friction can produce the same pile geometry as a less cohesive powder with higher friction (roessler2019demparametercalibration pages 2-3, gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 1-3).

- **Young's modulus** is poorly constrained by flow tests (AoR, funnel discharge) since reducing it over several orders of magnitude has negligible effect on flowability for free-flowing materials (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 2-3), but it dominates the oedometer response (jahn2023onthesensitivity pages 1-2, jahn2023onthesensitivity pages 3-5).

**Breaking the degeneracy** requires combining tests that activate different physical mechanisms. The recommended multi-test strategies from the literature include:

- **Shear cell + angle of repose + bulk density**: The shear cell constrains friction and surface energy under controlled consolidation stress; AoR constrains friction and cohesion under gravity flow; bulk density constrains packing and indirectly Young's modulus (coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 28-30, coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34).

- **DYS or drop test for cohesion + shear cell for friction**: Karkala's approach uses dynamic yield strength simulations to calibrate JKR surface energy independently, then parallel-plate shear simulations to calibrate sliding and rolling friction (karkala2021analysisofpowder pages 24-28).

- **Full-field heap morphology instead of scalar AoR**: Gaboriault et al.'s pixel-based SAD metric better constrains the parameter space than any single AoR definition by capturing slope curvature, peak sharpness, and concavity (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 1-3, gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 12-15).

- **Oedometer for stiffness + pile for friction**: Since Young's modulus dominates the oedometer while rolling friction dominates the pile, combining these two tests separates these otherwise-confounded parameters (jahn2023onthesensitivity pages 3-5).

**GrainLearning (Sequential Monte Carlo / SQMC).** This Bayesian toolbox uses iterative importance sampling: an ensemble of DEM parameter samples is weighted by agreement with time-series observations (e.g., stress–strain curves), the posterior is recursively updated via Bayes' rule, and a Gaussian-mixture model is fitted to resample for the next iteration (cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 1-3, cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 3-4). In Cheng et al.'s triaxial-compression calibration of granular soils in a five-dimensional parameter space, posterior distributions stabilized after approximately 60 data-assimilation steps (cheng2018probabilisticcalibrationof pages 8-11). GrainLearning can substitute recurrent neural networks for the DEM simulator, reducing cost by "several orders of magnitude" (cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 3-4). The method produces posterior distributions and parameter correlations rather than single optima (cheng2018probabilisticcalibrationof pages 16-18).

**Kriging / Gaussian-process emulators.** Rackl and Hanley used Latin-hypercube sampling to generate DEM training data for a seven-parameter calibration (particle density, Young's modulus, μs,pp, μs,pw, μr,pp, μr,pw, time-step weighting) against AoR and bulk density for glass beads. The Kriging meta-models showed "excellent correlation" with DEM responses (rackl2017amethodicalcalibration pages 1-2, rackl2017amethodicalcalibration pages 2-3). Richter and Will also list Kriging and Gaussian-process models among effective surrogate types, alongside neural networks (richter2021introducingmetamodelbasedglobal pages 3-5).

**Artificial neural networks.** Benvenuti et al. trained a feed-forward ANN on 546 shear-cell and 81 AoR DEM simulations (627 total) for iron-ore sinter fines with four varied parameters (μs, μr, COR, ρp). The trained ANN then screened 6,250,000 parameter combinations to identify sets matching experimental bulk density, internal friction, and AoR within 5% (benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 1-2, benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 2-3, benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 3-4). The ANN achieved R² = 0.96 with 15 hidden neurons (benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 3-4).

**Metamodel-Based Global Calibration (MBGC).** Richter and Will trained a reusable symbolic-regression surrogate across a 12-dimensional parameter space (including parameterized PSD) using "several hundred" DEM simulations. Once trained, the metamodel enables instant calibration for new materials within the same domain without additional DEM runs (richter2021introducingmetamodelbasedglobal pages 5-7, richter2021introducingmetamodelbasedglobal pages 3-5, richter2021introducingmetamodelbasedglobal pages 1-3).

**Response Surface Methodology / D-optimal DoE.** El-Kassem et al. used quadratic D-optimal designs with 40 runs (free-flowing lactose, 5 parameters) and 53 runs (cohesive lactose, 6 parameters including SJKR cohesion energy density) to build multivariate regression models with adjusted R² = 0.954 for AoR (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 10-11, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 9-10). Chen et al. used 32 simulations for a five-factor screening of the ring shear cell, followed by response-surface augmentation (chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 3-5, chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 5-6).

**Evolutionary algorithms (ACCES).** Nicuşan et al. describe population-based evolutionary optimization for DEM calibration, noting that brute-force grid search requires ~100 simulations for 2 parameters but ~10⁶ for 6 parameters, motivating surrogate-assisted approaches (nicusan2025accesnoninvasivesimulation pages 2-4).

**Metal AM powders.** For Ti6Al4V (D5–D95 = 45–111 μm), full-field heap calibration using 670 DEM simulations yielded μs = 0.25, μr = 0.001, γ = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ J/m², e = 0.9, Y = 26.25 MPa (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 9-12, gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 12-15). For Al6061 (D5–D95 = 18–50 μm), 100 LHS simulations yielded μs = 0.68, μr = 0.093, γ = 5.8 × 10⁻⁵ J/m² (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 9-12, gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 12-15). These results highlight that even within metal AM powders, calibrated parameters vary substantially between alloys and particle sizes. Meier et al. report an effective surface energy of γ = 0.1 mJ/m² for fine metal AM powder calibrated via static AoR, noting that for ~30 μm particles, adhesive forces already exceed gravity by an order of magnitude (meier2021physics‐basedmodelingand pages 2-4). Zhang et al. report μpp = 0.185 and e = 0.8 for Ti-6Al-4V DEM spreading simulations (zhang2017machinelearningenabled pages 4-7).

**Pharmaceutical powders.** For free-flowing lactose (SpheroLac 100, D[3,4] = 115 μm, AoR = 33°), the D-optimal DoE varied μs over 0.10–0.85, μr over 0.05–0.75, e over 0.051–0.60, and Y over 0.026–2.6 GPa across 40 simulations (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 10-11, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 9-10). For cohesive lactose (InhaLac 251, D[3,4] = 53.4 μm, AoR = 40°), SJKR cohesion energy density was varied over 30,000–70,000 J/m³ across 53 simulations, with rolling friction showing the strongest AoR effect in the cohesive regime (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 9-10, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 11-13). In ring-shear DEM of cohesive lactose, Chen et al. found that particle friction (0.1–0.8) and surface energy (10⁻⁴–0.1 J/m²) were highly significant for shear stress, while restitution and Poisson's ratio were non-significant (chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 3-5, chen2022a“sequentialdesign pages 5-6).

**Iron ore and industrial powders.** Benvenuti et al. calibrated iron-ore sinter fines using combined shear-cell and AoR data, training ANNs on 627 DEM simulations with μs ∈ [0.4, 0.8], μr ∈ [0.4, 0.8], COR ∈ [0.5, 0.9], and ρp ∈ [2500, 3500] kg/m³. Valid parameter combinations were identified by requiring all four bulk responses (pre-shear friction, shear friction, bulk density, and AoR) to match experiments within 5% (benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 1-2, benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 2-3, benvenuti2015demparameteridentification pages 3-4).

### 6. Implications for Auger Dosing and Powder-Bed AM Spreadability

For the context of an auger doser calibration targeting simulation-based Bayesian inference, several practical conclusions emerge. The "dosing fingerprint" (mass/revolution, tap-freed mass, afterflow time constant, no-flow fraction) provides multi-modal calibration targets analogous to combining AoR, shear, and dynamic tests. Since angle of repose alone is demonstrably non-unique for cohesive powders (gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 1-3, gaboriault2026fromangleof pages 12-15), using multiple dosing observables simultaneously should help break the μs–μr–γ degeneracy. The LIGGGHTS Hertz-Mindlin + SJKR + constant-directional-torque rolling friction model used in the user's 105 mg/rev salt simulation is consistent with the contact models employed in many of the cited calibration studies (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 5-6, roessler2019demparametercalibration pages 2-3). For computationally expensive simulations (26–30 CPU-minutes per revolution), surrogate-assisted Bayesian methods such as GrainLearning SMC or GP-emulator-based calibration are strongly recommended over brute-force parameter sweeps, as they can achieve convergence with O(10²) rather than O(10⁴–10⁶) simulations (cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 1-3, nicusan2025accesnoninvasivesimulation pages 2-4, rackl2017amethodicalcalibration pages 1-2).

For anticipating powder-bed spreadability from calibrated DEM parameters, cohesion (surface energy) is the most critical parameter: for fine metal powders, adhesion forces dominate gravity and control both packing fraction and surface roughness of spread layers (meier2021physics‐basedmodelingand pages 2-4). Parameters calibrated against dosing or heap tests should be validated against independent spreading experiments before predictive use in AM process simulation, since parameter transferability between different test geometries and stress states is not guaranteed (coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34, coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 28-30).

## Query

Context: we run an open-source, 3D-printed (PLA/PETG) auger powder doser for self-driving labs in alloy discovery for additive manufacturing. A small helical auger (1 mL and 10 mL reservoirs, about 3 mm outlet) is turned by a stepper motor at 1-30 rpm, the doser can be tilted 0-30 degrees from horizontal, and a solenoid taps the housing; doses of 0.05-8 g fall onto an analytical balance under closed-loop control. We have dosed 13 powders (NaCl table salt, gas-atomized AlSi10Mg and Al 4047, two silicon grades, rice flour, xanthan gum, carboxymethyl cellulose, BaCl2 and others) and record a 'dosing fingerprint': mass per auger revolution versus tilt and speed, the mass freed by one tap, the afterflow time constant after the auger stops, and the fraction of increments with no flow. What machine-learned surrogate simulators exist for granular flow (graph-network simulators such as GNS and 'learning to simulate', neural operators, Gaussian-process or neural-network emulators of DEM outputs), how are they trained from DEM data, how well do they generalize to new geometries and contact parameters, and have they been used for inverse problems such as estimating friction or cohesion from observed flow? Which open-source implementations and pretrained models exist?

# Machine-learned surrogates for auger-powder calibration

**Bottom line.** For the described 3-mm-outlet doser, start with a **probabilistic emulator of measured DEM outputs**, not a pretrained particle simulator. A Gaussian process (GP), small neural network, or fitted response surface can map contact parameters and operating conditions directly to mass per revolution, tap release, afterflow, and no-flow probability. This is much cheaper to train than a full trajectory model and permits posterior uncertainty and targeted DEM runs. Particle graph simulators are promising if many related geometries or operating trajectories must eventually be simulated, but published speed and generalization results do **not** establish that an existing checkpoint predicts a tapped, intermittently flowing, SJKR-cohesive auger. Published granular examples also distinguish DEM-trained models from models trained on the material point method (MPM). (cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 1-3, cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 3-4, lupo2021calibrationofdem pages 1-2, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 23-24)

## What has been demonstrated

| Approach | Training target and demonstrated scope | Generalization and relevance to the doser |
|---|---|---|
| **Particle graph simulators (GNS; “learning to simulate”)** | Construct nearby-particle edges; an encoder–message-passing processor–decoder predicts acceleration, followed by numerical position/velocity updates. Choi and Kumar trained a granular GNS on **MPM**, not DEM: 385 two-dimensional trajectories at five friction angles, 15°–45°, and 1,000 three-dimensional barrier trajectories. | Demonstrated different column configurations and barriers, and friction-angle-conditioned prediction within the studied setting. Their 2D training material had **fixed cohesion** of 0.1 kPa; this is not evidence for extrapolation over SJKR surface energy, rolling friction, moisture, or an auger outlet. (choi2024forwardandinverse pages 70-75, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 20-21, choi2024inverseanalysisof pages 8-12) |
| **Mayr et al., Learning 3D Granular Flow / Boundary Graph Neural Networks (BGNN)** | **LIGGGHTS DEM** trajectories train a particle graph. Virtual nodes at nearby triangular-wall surfaces convey particle–wall distances and surface normals. Evaluations include hoppers, rotating drums, and moving-blade mixers; the earlier hopper study used 30 trajectories with varied wall angles, openings, and initial fills. Cohesive as well as noncohesive examples appear in the BGNN evaluation. | The authors tested **held-out changes to hopper outlet/wall angles and drum length**; Figure 5 displays predictions beside DEM ground truth for these out-of-distribution geometries. This is more pertinent to an auger than column collapse, but is **not** a test of a new contact law or reliable prediction of rare clogs. For one drum benchmark, 250,000 LIGGGHTS steps took **356 s on a CPU**, versus **158 s for 2,500 BGNN predictions on an A100**—different hardware and time resolution. (mayr2021learning3dgranular pages 1-2, mayr2021learning3dgranular pages 2-4, mayr2021boundarygraphneural pages 6-7, mayr2021boundarygraphneural media 5e47ce6e) |
| **NeuralDEM, Alkin et al. (2025)** | A **DEM/CFD–DEM-trained field-based neural operator**: compress particle information into continuous fields and use interacting transformer-style branches for particle and, where applicable, fluid dynamics. Hopper data vary geometry and friction; a separate fluidized-bed model uses CFD–DEM. It predicts macroscopic drainage, transport, and mixing rather than requiring accurate individual-particle paths. | It handled withheld **combinations** of hopper angle and shear-cell friction angle within the studied range; this is parameter interpolation, not a verified new auger geometry or new SJKR physics. In its reported benchmarks, a 250,000-particle, 40-s hopper DEM run took **3 h on 16 CPU cores**, versus **1.4 s** for the fastest neural rollout on one GPU (**8 s** including all output points); a 500,000-particle fluidized-bed case took **6 h on 64 CPU cores**, versus **11 s** on one GPU. Training the fluidized-bed model used approximately **320 H100 GPU-hours**. These figures describe different hardware and exclude amortization of dataset generation. (alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 1-2, alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 7-8, alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 4-5, alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 5-7) |
| **Contact-memory graph networks (TRACE, 2026 preprint)** | Store evolving **contact-edge memory**, predict constrained normal/tangential forces, and train with single-step then rollout losses. Tested on 2D/3D column collapse against **MPM**, not an SJKR auger DEM. | Reports **8.9×–12.2×** speed relative to its MPM reference and improved deposit errors over graph baselines. Contact history is attractive for stop/start flow, but published tests do not demonstrate moisture, adhesive bonds, tapped walls, or inference of physical cohesion. (zhou2026traceaspatiotemporal pages 1-3) |
| **Low-dimensional DEM-output emulators** | Train directly on responses at deliberately chosen contact-parameter settings. Demonstrations include a quadratic D-optimal regression of repose angle, porosity, and flow rate for cohesive/free-flowing powders; a **symbolic-regression** metamodel fitted to **563 DEM parameter sets**, checked against 68 independent sets; and polynomial response surfaces for rotating-drum avalanche energies of AM powders. | Best matched to a small number of expensive auger outputs and Bayesian inversion. A fitted response surface is reliable only inside its sampled geometry, contact-model, environmental, and operating-condition domain; it does not automatically simulate particles or transfer to recoating. In the rotating-drum study, factor screening also eliminated weakly influential wall-contact parameters before fitting, illustrating why parameter identifiability should be assessed before expanding a design. (richter2021introducingmetamodelbasedglobal pages 12-15, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 21-23, sani2026efficientdemcalibration pages 6-7) |

A **neural operator** learns a map between discretized fields, rather than rebuilding and stepping every grain-contact graph. NeuralDEM is a concrete granular example; it is not evidence that a generic Fourier neural operator, trained on unrelated fields, can resolve a 3-mm bridge or predict a zero-flow event. The granular-ML review specifically notes difficulties with changing particle contacts, solid-to-flowing transitions, and reconstructing physically admissible particle states from field representations. Thus, use a field operator if macroscopic fields over many operating conditions justify its training cost; retain contact-resolved DEM when clogging and tap-induced release determine the likelihood. (alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 1-2, alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 7-8, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 23-24)

## Inverse problems: what is known, and what is not

**Friction has been inverted with a differentiable graph simulator.** Choi and Kumar differentiated an MPM-trained GNS through a granular-column rollout and optimized friction angle against a target runout distance. Four held-out collapse cases gave inferred-angle errors **below 8.94%**; the authors report approximately **151×** faster optimization iterations than their MPM/finite-difference baseline. This estimates a continuum-model friction angle—not particle–particle sliding friction, wall friction, SJKR cohesion, or moisture from auger mass data. Differentiating long particle rollouts was memory-intensive and required checkpointing. (choi2024inverseanalysisof pages 16-20, choi2024inverseanalysisof pages 30-34)

**Cohesion-related and rolling parameters have been estimated with DEM response surrogates**, but an optimizer is not a Bayesian posterior. Katagiri and colleagues used GP-guided Bayesian optimization of a cohesion-related coefficient and rolling resistance against consolidation void ratio. A near-optimal result appeared within **12 optimization trials**; nevertheless, those calibrated parameters produced an **excessively cohesive spreading layer**. The authors attribute this to changed loading mechanics and stress scaling. Their GP guides evaluations toward an optimum; it does not by itself report a joint probability distribution of plausible physical parameters. For an actual parameter posterior, **GrainLearning** provides sequential Bayesian filtering of experimental time series against DEM runs and supports surrogate modeling as an alternative to repeatedly evaluating DEM. (katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 1-2, cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 3-4, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 6-9)

**Non-uniqueness is a substantive risk.** In a cohesive-powder study, different adhesion-energy/rolling-friction combinations reproduced the same repose angle, while matching unconfined yield strength called for a substantially different rolling-friction value. No single tested set reproduced both measurements consistently. A salt mass-per-revolution match without fitting is an excellent **forward check for that case**, but it is not evidence that the remaining powders’ contact parameters are identifiable from mean dose alone. (lupo2021calibrationofdem pages 1-2, lupo2021calibrationofdem pages 17-19, lupo2021calibrationofdem pages 15-17)

## Recommended calibration and validation design

1. **Define the estimand as effective DEM parameters.** For each powder and batch, condition particle-size distribution, density, and measured moisture history; infer a *small, sensitivity-screened* subset of particle–particle sliding/rolling resistance, particle–PLA/PETG wall interaction, and SJKR cohesion. Treat fill level/packing history, powder shape, ambient humidity, and the tap impulse as recorded inputs or nuisance variables. Adhesive-energy and rolling coefficients depend on the chosen contact law and representation of particle shape; do not report them as uniquely measured intrinsic material constants. The finding that rolling friction can compensate for spherical representation of nonspherical grains reinforces this distinction. (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 3-5, lupo2021calibrationofdem pages 1-2, sani2026efficientdemcalibration pages 4-6)
2. **Generate an auger-specific DEM design.** Sample physically plausible parameters and deliberately include both flowing and jammed regimes, each reservoir, low/high fill, tilt, 1–30 rpm, stop/start states, and housing taps. Export the *same balance-observable summaries* as in the laboratory, with replicate initial packings; reserve entire powder batches, operating conditions, and, if relevant, outlet geometries as held-out tests. A predictor of average mass per turn alone cannot be expected to learn afterflow or the no-flow fraction. The varied-parameter DEM datasets and out-of-distribution geometry tests in published work illustrate the need to include each intended axis of generalization in training and evaluation. (choi2024inverseanalysisof pages 8-12, mayr2021boundarygraphneural pages 6-7, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 23-24)
3. **Fit a multi-output or linked likelihood.** A GP or small ensemble can emulate continuous mass-per-revolution, tap mass, and afterflow parameters; fit no-flow events with a Bernoulli/binomial likelihood and account for replicate variation. Compare posterior predictions to the **full incremental-mass traces**, not just their means. Include measurement noise, DEM-to-experiment discrepancy, and emulator uncertainty; acquire new DEM runs where posterior probability or uncertainty is high, especially near flow/no-flow transitions. These are proposed design choices for this doser, rather than published validation of an auger-specific surrogate. Bayesian filtering and documented cross-test failures motivate explicit uncertainty and independent checks. (cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 1-3, cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 3-4, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 1-2, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 23-24)
4. **Cross-check, do not force a universal flowability score.** Use **D7891 FT4 shear testing** and **D6773 ring shear** for constrained-strength information; **B213 Hall** and **B964 Carney** for funnel flow (including non-flow); **B212** apparent density and **B527** tap density for packing; **B214** sieving and **B822** light-scattering sizing for feed distribution. Include repose angle, documented humidity/conditioning and loss on drying. Report the actual apparatus, consolidation stress, sample preparation, and conditioning: these methods probe different stress and flow regimes. The source review’s summary table inconsistently groups some flow and density methods; its bibliography explicitly names **B964 as Carney *flow rate*** and **D7891 as Freeman powder *shear testing***, so those should not be silently relabeled as density or universal spreadability tests. (groarke2021powdercharacterization—methodsstandards pages 9-12, groarke2021powdercharacterization—methodsstandards pages 12-14, groarke2021powdercharacterization—methodsstandards pages 33-38)
5. **Measure spreadability separately.** For the AM-relevant metal powders, compare posterior-predictive rankings with a small recoater experiment at relevant blade/roller type, speeds and layer heights: image-based coverage and bare-patch frequency, layer mass/packing, thickness variability, and surface-height variation. Hulme-Smith and colleagues demonstrated inexpensive image-derived layer coverage over **50–200 mm/s** and **50–100 µm**; Yuasa and colleagues found AlSi10Mg surface-height variation increased markedly above **50 mm/s**, and powder-shape rankings changed at high speed. Angle of repose and FT4 basic flow energy can be informative correlates, but dosability through an auger, funnel discharge, and thin-layer recoating are *different operations*. The consolidation-to-spreading failure is direct evidence against declaring spreadability from a calibrated dosing fingerprint alone. (hulmesmith2021spreadabilitytestingof pages 1-2, yuasa2021influencesofpowder pages 1-2, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 1-2)

At **26–30 CPU-min per revolution**, even 100 single-revolution DEM cases would require roughly **43–50 CPU-hours**, before initialization, extra turns, replicates, and tap/afterflow sequences. This is a planning calculation from the runtime supplied in the question, **not** a published benchmark. It favors factor screening, space-filling initial runs, adaptive acquisition, and a cheap summary-output emulator first. Use a wall-aware particle GNN later if those runs already produce sufficient resolved trajectories and the research question genuinely requires transfer between screw/outlet designs. The reported NeuralDEM inference speed should not be read as a prediction of its training or validation cost for this doser. (alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 5-7, sani2026efficientdemcalibration pages 6-7, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 23-24)

## Public software and pretrained artifacts

- **NeuralDEM:** [github.com/Emmi-AI/NeuralDEM](https://github.com/Emmi-AI/NeuralDEM). The authors state that inference code, **pretrained model weights**, and one example trajectory for each hopper and fluidized-bed case are released; the complete approximately **3.2-TB** datasets are available on request. Those weights were trained for the published cases, not an auger. (alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 9-10)
- **Choi–Kumar GNS and inverse examples:** [github.com/geoelements/gns](https://github.com/geoelements/gns) and [github.com/geoelements/gns-inverse-examples](https://github.com/geoelements/gns-inverse-examples), reported under MIT license. Their **training data and trained models** are deposited on DesignSafe; the paper lists dataset DOI **10.17603/DS2-4NQZ-S548** and inverse-example DOI **10.17603/DS2-0WJQ-0J84**. These are MPM-trained granular examples. (choi2024inverseanalysisof pages 34-37, choi2024inverseanalysisof pages 30-34)
- **Mayr BGNN:** [ml-jku.github.io/bgnn](https://ml-jku.github.io/bgnn/) is the documented project page with visualizations. The retrieved paper establishes this page, **not a verified downloadable pretrained checkpoint**; verify repository and license before planning reuse. (mayr2021boundarygraphneural pages 7-8, mayr2021boundarygraphneural pages 6-7)
- **TRACE:** [github.com/Data-Driven-Computational-Geotechnics/TRACE](https://github.com/Data-Driven-Computational-Geotechnics/TRACE) is documented as available code; a pretrained auger or DEM-contact-law checkpoint is **not documented**. (zhou2026traceaspatiotemporal pages 1-3)
- **GrainLearning:** an open-source Bayesian calibration/surrogate toolbox with published [JOSS software article](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06338) and [archived release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8352544). It is infrastructure for calibration, **not a pretrained powder-flow predictor**. (cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 1-3, cheng2024grainlearningabayesian pages 3-4)

**Assessment:** Existing open implementations provide useful architectures, calibration software, and some pretrained *benchmark* weights. None of the cited evidence establishes a transferable pretrained model for this doser’s PLA/PETG walls, rotating helix, SJKR adhesion, taps, and intermittent milligram-scale discharge. The experimentally defensible route is an auger-specific, uncertainty-aware inverse emulator, independently challenged by shear, packing, size/moisture, and actual recoating observations. (lupo2021calibrationofdem pages 1-2, alkin2025neuraldemforreal pages 9-10, fransen2026towardsscientificmachine pages 23-24)

# Triboelectric modeling for a 3D-printed auger doser

**Recommendation.** Retain the validated mechanical LIGGGHTS auger model, but treat electrical charging as a separate, experimentally calibrated mechanism. A simulation that predicts salt mass per revolution without fitted parameters has not thereby established the charge transferred at salt–PLA/PETG contacts, the charge retained by an insulating wall, or electrostatic adhesion. Begin with measured outlet charge-to-mass ratio and wall deposition under controlled atmosphere; add charging and electrical forces only where those measurements show that they matter. Published particle–polymer charging models require contact-pair-specific parameters, and humidity can affect charge generation and decay in opposite directions. (pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 31-35, mirkowska2016principalfactorsof pages 9-11, phillips2021implementationofcharged pages 1-4, peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 5-7)

## Physics and required parameters

For repeated particle–wall contacts, a useful *condenser* model is `Δq = k_s A_c(ΔV − k_0q)`: transferred charge depends on maximum contact area, an effective contact-potential difference and the opposing potential created by charge already on the particle. Repetition produces an approximately exponential approach to saturation. Hertzian contact mechanics supplies a first estimate of `A_c`, but its dependence on impact speed, stiffness and real surface roughness matters. Pei’s implementation matched published repeated-impact measurements using this approach; its example `k_s = 10⁻⁴ C m⁻² V⁻¹` is a model parameter, **not** a measured universal constant for the present powders or printed walls. (pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 31-35, pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 102-107, pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 87-92)

For conducting metal–metal contacts, a contact-potential/work-function and capacitance description is a reasonable starting point. An oxidized AlSi10Mg or Al 4047 particle against printed PLA or PETG is instead a metal/oxide–insulator contact: the exposed oxide, adsorbates and polymer surface can govern transfer. Salt, silicon and the organic powders likewise cannot be assigned a reliable charge sign merely from a generic triboelectric series. For two insulating surfaces, occupied and empty surface-state models, mobile-ion transfer and effective contact potentials are alternatives; use measured *effective pair parameters*, rather than assuming that a tabulated metal work function specifies a PLA-, PETG- or nylon-wall collision. Contact charging and electrical conduction during contact should also be distinguished. (pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 35-40, phillips2021implementationofcharged pages 4-7, mirkowska2016principalfactorsof pages 9-11, alchikhsulaiman2020powderspreadingand pages 177-178)

Between contacts, evolve particle charge with an experimentally determined relaxation law, for example `dq/dt = −q/τ_q`; retain charge on insulating wall patches rather than automatically resetting the wall to zero. In contrast, an intentionally grounded conducting insert has a conducting electrical boundary condition, although it still attracts an already charged particle through polarization. Add pairwise Coulomb forces and wall-field forces to the existing Hertz–Mindlin/SJKR/rolling-resistance mechanics; do **not** fold electrostatic adhesion into an increased SJKR surface energy and then count it again as an electric force. For larger particle populations, map charges onto a mesh, solve a Poisson electrostatic problem with appropriate wall boundary conditions, and apply `F = qE`; direct all-pairs summation scales poorly, while a short force cutoff may discard consequential long-range fields. (pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 74-77, goniva2015opensourcecfddem pages 2-3, pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 113-118, walton2008reviewofadhesion pages 1-2)

**Adhesion at the approximately 3 mm outlet.** Compare the local electrostatic force with particle weight and the *separately calibrated* short-range adhesive force. For a charged particle of charge `Q` near an ideal grounded conductor, the simple image-force estimate at contact scales as `Q²/R²`; particle polarization and, especially, charge concentrated on a small contact patch can increase attraction. In one toner comparison, a 10 µm particle carrying about 8 fC gave an approximately 10 nN uniform-charge image-force estimate, yet measured adhesion exceeded such estimates by factors of 5–50. A printed dielectric wall is **not** an ideal grounded image plane: its permittivity contrast, trapped surface charge, conductivity and grounding arrangement determine the field. Roughness can greatly reduce ideal van der Waals pull-off, whereas moisture bridges can increase adhesion even while moisture accelerates electrical leakage. Thus a stopped-auger afterflow or no-flow increment alone cannot identify electrostatic versus capillary or mechanical sticking. (walton2008reviewofadhesion pages 1-2, walton2007adhesionoflunara pages 14-16, walton2008reviewofadhesion pages 5-7)

## Humidity and argon/nitrogen

Model relative humidity and water content as controlled inputs to **both** charge-transfer and charge-decay parameters, and to capillary cohesion. Thin adsorbed-water layers can facilitate mobile-ion transfer; thicker, more conductive layers can discharge surfaces. The resulting trend is material- and timescale-dependent: dry aerosol experiments found that silica surface charge increased almost tenfold near 50% RH, whereas a separate volcanic-ash study found that small amounts of water reduced collected charge by up to an order of magnitude during low-frequency, minute-scale collisions. Neither result supplies a transferable PLA/PETG or salt correction. Record equilibration time, RH, temperature and loss on drying for each powder/wall experiment. (mirkowska2016principalfactorsof pages 9-11, peltonen2019electrostaticinstrumentationand pages 35-38, alois2019contactelectrificationin pages 134-137)

**An inert atmosphere is not an antistatic treatment.** Dry argon and nitrogen still permit contact charging; changing gas also changes adsorbates, gas transport and discharge behavior. In a powder-jet experiment, both Ar and N₂ produced increasing Faraday-cage charge, although visible discharges were less frequent in N₂. Aerosol tests with air, Ar and CO₂ found gas-dependent charging that did not track breakdown voltage linearly. Gas breakdown is a *separate* limit: Paschen/Townsend descriptions depend on gas, pressure, gap and electrode properties. Measurements on 10–500 µm planar gaps found the qualitative breakdown-voltage ordering N₂ above Ar above He, but that geometry cannot provide a numerical breakdown threshold for this doser’s 3 mm outlet or nanometre contact gaps; small gaps and rough surfaces require particular caution. Specify the gas composition, pressure, water content and electrical boundaries rather than assuming that Ar or N₂ neutralizes charge. Likewise, do not invoke plasma Debye screening for an ordinary non-ionized inert atmosphere without evidence of an ionized gas. (alois2019contactelectrificationin pages 134-137, springsklee2023experimentalvolcaniclightning pages 63-67, carazzetti2009electricalbreakdownat pages 3-4, carazzetti2009electricalbreakdownat pages 2-3)

## Open-source implementation and validation status

* **LIGGGHTS-PUBLIC:** the closest documented starting point for the existing auger simulation. Phillips and colleagues modified this open-source DEM code to add contact conduction, work-function/contact-area tribocharging and pairwise standard or screened Coulomb forces, adapting Coulomb source code from **LAMMPS**. Their initial two-particle tests checked charge-transfer and force trends; they were not a Faraday-cup validation for an auger. These features were research modifications, not established default features of a general LIGGGHTS installation. The retrieved papers do not establish a downloadable, version-compatible release of those modifications. (phillips2021implementationofcharged pages 4-7, phillips2021implementationofcharged pages 7-9, phillips2021implementationofcharged pages 1-4)
* **Modified LIGGGHTS with stronger experimental validation:** Wang and colleagues used contact-point electric fields evaluated each timestep, particle–particle and particle–grounded-wall transfer, and electrostatic forces. They compared simulated PA/PC charge-versus-time with both earlier simulations and **Faraday-cage measurements** after 90 s in a grounded, vibrating aluminium box; agreement improved toward the end of the run. Their Figure 3 shows that comparison. The demonstration used millimetre-scale polymer particles, specified charge caps and adjusted contact/shape parameters—not Al alloy or salt fines against printed polymer, and not a parameter-free validation for this feeder. (wang2022investigatingparticleparticleelectrostatic pages 5-7, wang2022investigatingparticleparticleelectrostatic pages 7-8, wang2022investigatingparticleparticleelectrostatic media f4e11fdb)
* **CFDEMcoupling with LIGGGHTS and OpenFOAM:** an open-source framework for particle trajectories and gas momentum/drag exchange. Its documented coupling workflow does **not**, by itself, establish a packaged, validated contact-charging, Poisson-field or polymer-wall image-force solver. Pei’s published condenser-model **DEM–CFD** results and Coulomb/image-force analysis demonstrate modeling methods, but the retrieved account identifies a separately developed DEM–CFD code, not a ready-to-enable CFDEMcoupling module. Add CFD coupling when gas drag, fines entrainment or outlet flight affects the observation; for quasi-static auger contacts, charge-enabled DEM is the less costly first test. (pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 107-113, pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 87-92, goniva2015opensourcecfddem pages 2-3, goniva2015opensourcecfddem pages 1-2)

## Measurement and calibration program

1. **Measure electrical outputs independently of dose mass.** Collect a defined discharged mass in an electrically insulated inner Faraday pail shielded by a grounded outer enclosure; report signed `Q/m` and mass, with a consistent delay after exit. Compare initial and dispensed charge, then recover and weigh powder adhering to the outlet and wall. Vary wall coupons or liners—actual printed PLA, PETG and nylon surfaces versus a specified grounded metal—while controlling gas, RH, powder conditioning, reservoir fill, auger speed, tilt and tap history. Take replicates and measure charge decay after stopping. A pail measures *net* charge: positive and negative grains can cancel, so size-fractionated or single-particle charge measurements are valuable when segregation or bipolar charging is suspected. Published powder measurements demonstrate that changing plastic versus metal contact can change `Q/m` substantially and in a powder-dependent direction. (vyas2021amethodologyto pages 16-24, peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 5-7, peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 4-5, beleca2012investigationofbipolar pages 93-96)
2. **Fit mechanical parameters first, with electrical parameters conditional on those fits.** Use B214/B822 size distributions, particle morphology, B212/B527 packing, D7891/D6773 shear and wall-sensitive measurements, B213/B964 flow, angle of repose and loss on drying as distinct checks on size, packing, friction, cohesion and water sensitivity. Fit the dosing fingerprint—including no-flow frequency, tap-released mass and afterflow—across speeds, tilts and fills. Then use measured `Q/m`, charge decay, wall deposition and charge-versus-contact history to infer separate, pair-specific charging, wall-retention and leakage parameters. Bayesian simulation-based inference should include measurement noise, run-to-run variability and an explicit model-discrepancy term: several combinations of cohesion, wall friction and charge can otherwise explain the same dose. This is a proposed workflow, not an assertion that the cited charging studies recovered unique microscopic properties from auger mass data. (pei2014demcfdanalysisof pages 31-35, mirkowska2016principalfactorsof pages 9-11, vyas2021amethodologyto pages 16-24, peart2001powderelectrostaticstheory pages 5-7)
3. **Control computation and test transferability.** At 26–30 CPU-minutes per simulated revolution, reserve full auger evaluations for an informative design of experiments and posterior checks; use cheaper isolated collision/wall-deposition cases and a surrogate for repeated inference. Keep the salt 105 mg/revolution result as a *mechanical baseline*, then compare charge-enabled and charge-disabled predictions on withheld humidity/wall/gas conditions. Finally simulate a representative blade or roller spreading test with the inferred powder parameters and independently measure layer coverage, height variation, density/packing, roughness, streaks and segregation. This last test is essential: a published Bayesian-optimized DEM fit reproduced consolidation void ratio yet produced an excessively cohesive, nonuniform spread layer because consolidation and recoating probe different mechanics. A dosing fingerprint can prioritize powders for AM testing, but cannot on its own certify spreadability. (katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 1-2, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 11-14, lee2019quantificationofpowder pages 10-14, alchikhsulaiman2020powderspreadingand pages 66-70)

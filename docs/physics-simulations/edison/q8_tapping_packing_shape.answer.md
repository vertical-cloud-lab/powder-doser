# Simulating and interpreting tapping in an auger powder doser

**Bottom line.** Use the dosing fingerprint to infer *effective, device- and condition-dependent DEM parameters*, not unique intrinsic values of cohesion or friction. A useful model must reproduce several independent responses—mass per revolution, zero-flow events, release after one tap, afterflow, and packing under a specified preparation protocol. Matching mean flow alone, even without fitted parameters for salt, does not validate the contact law for cohesive powders or predict spreadability. In particular, published consolidation-calibrated cohesion parameters have produced an unrealistically cohesive simulated spread layer. (katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 9-11, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 11-14)

## 1. Tapping, vibration, and compaction

**Tapped-density DEM.** Generate particles from the measured size distribution, pour them into a model of the *actual density-test cylinder*, let them settle, and impose the measured displacement or acceleration history of each tap on the walls. After every impulse and relaxation, measure bed height and calculate bulk density. Report the full trajectory, ρ(n), as well as loose density, protocol-defined final tapped density, and **Hausner ratio = ρ_tapped/ρ_loose**. Keep fill mass, cylinder and wall material, initial pouring procedure, tap amplitude, frequency, number of taps, and relaxation criterion identical between experiment and simulation: tapping is history-dependent, not simply a request for the densest attainable packing. Classical granular-tapping experiments exhibit slow, approximately inverse-logarithmic compaction and dependence on excitation history; DEM of frictional *and* cohesive particles likewise shows unusually strong preparation-rate dependence. (ratsimba2021densificationbehaviourand pages 5-10, fernandezUnknownyeardevelopmentandevaluation pages 40-43, nan2023ultraslowsettlingkinetics pages 2-3, luding2000aminimalmodel pages 1-3)

A published GranuPack protocol illustrates what should be reproduced: a 26-mm-diameter metal cylinder was filled using a slowly withdrawn inner tube; each tap raised and dropped the cylinder **1 mm**, and bed height was recorded after each tap. For irregular, fine Y₂O₃, measured loose and tapped densities were **0.85 and 1.26 g cm⁻³**, giving **HR ≈ 1.49**. Its accompanying DEM, however, simulated *particle rain followed by roller recoating*, **not** that tap sequence; its 57% platelet-bed packing therefore cannot be claimed as a prediction of tapped density or HR. The paper also reports mutually difficult-to-reconcile relative-density figures—26.1% for its measured tapped density, 37 ± 5% for a geometrically measured recoated bed, and, later, 42.8 ± 0.5% described as tapped density. Check solid-density denominators and protocols before using any of those fractions as calibration targets. (ratsimba2021densificationbehaviourand pages 10-15, ratsimba2021densificationbehaviourand pages 5-10, ratsimba2021densificationbehaviourand pages 15-22)

**Tap-assisted dispensing is a different experiment.** In the 3-mm outlet, a solenoid impulse can both rearrange or densify the reservoir and break a local arch or adhered plug. Model the auger, outlet and PLA/PETG walls; stop the auger; equilibrate the observed fill state; apply the *measured housing acceleration waveform* at its measured location; then count particles crossing the outlet versus time. Sample independent initial configurations and report the probability of **no release**, the distribution of released mass conditional on release, latency, and post-tap afterflow. If housing flexure changes outlet clearance or wall motion, a rigid-body impulse is insufficient; measure or model that motion. For periodic vibration use a prescribed wall motion, for example z(t)=A sin(2πft), while keeping amplitude and acceleration distinct. Published vibrated-tube and capillary work reports onset thresholds and strong dependencies on frequency, amplitude and bore: a review describes several-hundred-Hz onset in one vibrating-capillary arrangement and milligram-per-second flows through approximately 0.6–1.3-mm ultrasonic capillaries. A separate experimental ultrasonic system used **28 kHz** and **0.30–0.35-mm** outlets for cohesive 10–50-µm powders. These are mechanistic analogues, **not** quantitative validation of a single solenoid tap on an auger housing. An ultrasonic gas-transport nozzle simulation located in this literature uses a dilute discrete-phase CFD model that neglects interparticle contacts; it likewise cannot validate cohesive arch breakup. (nikiforov2026numericalsimulationof pages 10-13, zhang2019laserbasedadditive pages 100-104, psarommatis2022systematicdevelopmentof pages 4-5)

The available numerical benchmarks show both promise and limitations, but are **not interchangeable validation tests**. (ratsimba2021densificationbehaviourand pages 15-22, ma2023acomparativestudy pages 13-15, ma2023acomparativestudy pages 15-18, habiba2023powderspreadingmechanism pages 15-17)

## 2. What a particle-size distribution can—and cannot—predict

A fast packing ensemble is valuable for initializing DEM and bounding the *geometric* contribution of size grading. Convert a volume-weighted diffraction distribution into numbers of particles in size bins, preserving their **solid volume fractions**, not assigning equal particle counts to each bin. Generate multiple finite-volume realizations and check wall effects, size segregation and the contribution of rare coarse particles near the outlet. Three distinct constructions are useful:

* **Lubachevsky–Stillinger:** move initially small hard spheres while increasing their radii in the prescribed size ratios; collisions and growth rate determine the final jammed structure. Polydisperse extensions and periodic boundaries have been described. (buryachenko2012modelingofrandom pages 4-6, buryachenko2012modelingofrandom pages 6-7)
* **Jodrey–Tory/collective rearrangement:** begin with candidate centers and iteratively resolve overlaps while compacting the configuration. This supplies dense sphere packs efficiently, but geometric packs may lack mechanically stable contacts and contact-force information. (bezrukov2006simulationandstatistical pages 2-4, ibrahim2019particulaasimulator pages 2-3)
* **Random sequential addition:** attempt nonoverlapping placements and reject unsuccessful insertions; optional later displacements or compression change the protocol and therefore the attainable packing. For irregular blocks, reported packing fractions changed appreciably with orientation and subsequent applied pressure. Thus a generated fraction is not, by itself, an ASTM tapped density. (sergi2012randompackingof pages 2-4)

Compute geometric ϕ=ΣV_particle/V_bed and, for a single fully dense constituent, ρ_bulk≈ϕρ_skeletal. For alloy mixtures, sum constituent masses and solid volumes explicitly. Then **pour and tap those generated particles with contact forces** if the desired output is loose or tapped density. A near-0.64 random-close-packed sphere baseline is not universal: an illustrative cohesive/frictional DEM study obtained approximately **0.646** for repulsive frictionless grains, **0.60** with friction alone, and **0.52** with cohesion alone under its particular preparation protocol. These are mechanism demonstrations, not predictive constants for salt, Si, or Al powders. (ibrahim2019particulaasimulator pages 2-3, nan2023ultraslowsettlingkinetics pages 2-3)

## 3. Image-informed irregular shapes

A practical image-to-DEM pipeline is **representative sampling → SEM projections or sufficiently resolved micro-CT → segmentation and separation of touching particles → particle-wise volume, equivalent diameter, aspect ratio, angularity and convexity distributions → simplified shapes → validation of shape *and* bulk responses**. SEM alone does not uniquely recover depth: one published workflow extruded binarized SEM silhouettes of PA12 powder into approximate 3D targets and used the open-source **CLUMP** generator to fit approximately **10 rigidly connected subspheres per particle**. Micro-CT provides genuine 3D geometry but introduces resolution, segmentation and contact-identification errors; a validated image-reconstruction study found that identifying nonspherical touching particles was substantially harder than identifying spheres. A newer micro-CT workflow segmented isolated regolith particles, reconstructed triangular meshes and coarsened them while preserving selected volume, area and aspect-ratio statistics. CT-resolved and sub-resolution surface roughness must be distinguished: unresolved roughness and chemistry still require effective contact parameters. (thompson2006quantitativecomputerreconstruction pages 6-8, roy2024combinedthermaland pages 3-5, thompson2006quantitativecomputerreconstruction pages 2-3, weber2026geometrypreservingmeshcoarsening pages 3-6)

For **gas-atomized Al alloys**, start with PSD-resolved spheres and measured satellites or an irregular-shape minority; for **salt crystals**, test cuboid-like superquadrics or low-face-count polyhedra; for **water-atomized, milled or visibly elongated particles**, use a small *distribution* of image-informed multispheres or polyhedra. Rigid multispheres reuse sphere-contact routines but can introduce artificial bumps and multiple contacts. Superquadrics efficiently capture rounded boxes and elongated or flattened forms but not arbitrary concavities. Polyhedra preserve facets and edges useful for crystalline/interlocking grains, at higher contact-detection cost. These are alternatives to benchmark, not interchangeable ways of assigning the same friction coefficient. In an experimental quartz-sand charging comparison, measured voidage was **0.386** versus **0.391** for a six-sphere representation, **0.398** for a 32-plane polyhedron, and **0.359** for the tested superellipsoid; increasing subsphere count did **not** monotonically improve voidage. More facets and subspheres increased computational cost, particularly above about 16 polyhedral planes or 12 subspheres in that study. (roy2024combinedthermaland pages 3-5, weber2026geometrypreservingmeshcoarsening pages 3-6, ma2023acomparativestudy pages 13-15, ma2023acomparativestudy pages 15-18, ma2023acomparativestudy pages 18-19)

**Shape versus cohesion is regime-dependent.** A comparison of chemically identical limestone powders found cohesion dominant for its finer samples, whereas for coarser samples—above roughly **150 µm** in that experiment—geometric interlocking increasingly controlled bulk friction. Removing fines reduced measured bulk cohesion while hardly changing bulk friction. DEM spreading studies likewise find that sufficiently strong cohesion can create clumps and sparse layers, while sliding/rolling resistance and shape also change coverage, alignment and heterogeneity; a roller may compact where a blade does not. Consequently, ranking shape against cohesion using one fitted angle of repose is not identifiable: evaluate a *shape × adhesion × moisture* sensitivity grid for each powder class. In particular, rolling friction used to compensate for missing angularity should be reconsidered after introducing faceted salt particles. (shaheen2020theinfluenceof pages 11-14, roy2024combinedthermaland pages 1-3, shi2018effectofparticle pages 15-17, shi2018effectofparticle pages 1-2, shi2018effectofparticle pages 17-18)

## 4. Feasible Bayesian calibration and independent checks

At **26–30 CPU-min per revolution**, a design of 100 full-revolution runs costs roughly **43–50 CPU-hours**, before replicated stochastic initial states or more expensive shapes. First fix externally measurable PSD, skeletal density, geometry, fill level and tap waveform; perform a screening design on particle–particle and particle–wall sliding friction, rolling resistance, restitution and effective adhesion. Use shorter outlet-region, gravity-pour, single-tap and shear simulations to refine parameter ranges. Fit a multi-output surrogate with predictive uncertainty and selectively add full-doser simulations where posterior uncertainty or predicted response is high. A reported Bayesian-*optimization* study required approximately eight hours per full DEM run and successfully found a **point estimate** using a surrogate, but its consolidation-only fit failed in powder spreading. Bayesian optimization of an error is **not** a posterior over material properties. (katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 9-11, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 4-6, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 2-4, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 11-14)

For actual simulation-based inference, define a powder-specific effective parameter vector θ and covariates x=(PSD/shape class, relative humidity or water content, reservoir and fill state, tilt, speed, wall material, tap impulse). Use a hierarchical likelihood combining continuous dose and density measurements with a Bernoulli or hurdle likelihood for **zero-flow increments**, a positive-mass distribution for nonzero increments and tap release, and censored/event-time treatment if afterflow ceases or is unobserved. Include balance noise, run-to-run initial-state variation, surrogate uncertainty and a model-discrepancy term; fit replicate powder preparations, and reserve complete humidity, fill, angle or powder conditions for posterior-predictive testing. Record whether mass per revolution and afterflow were measured with or without taps, rather than treating them as independent material properties. A salt prediction of 105 mg/rev is a useful held-out flow check, but does not by itself identify adhesion, wall friction or packing. Multiresponse sensitivity work shows that repose angle, porosity and mass flow respond differently to sliding friction, rolling resistance and cohesion—precisely why combining responses improves identifiability. (elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 16-18, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 4-6, elkassem2021amultivariateregression pages 20-21, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 11-14)

**Standards should test transferable predictions, not be assumed equivalent to DEM coefficients.** Pair the simulated preparation and stress path with ASTM **B212** apparent density, **B527** tapped density and HR; compare shear yield loci/flow functions over matched consolidation stresses with **D7891** FT4 and **D6773** Schulze ring shear; use **B213** Hall flow and **B964** Carney flow as explicitly geometry-dependent discharge checks; and use **B214** sieving plus **B822** laser diffraction for PSD. Report non-flow through a funnel as such, not as a missing flow time; obtain skeletal density independently when converting density to packing fraction. Include measured angle of repose, SEM shape/satellites, moisture by loss on drying and controlled-humidity repeat runs. Hall/Carney openings and operating conditions differ from a 3-mm printed auger outlet. FT4 and ring-shear yield loci can differ systematically because of preshear protocol; moreover, low-stress dispensing may lie below the most reliable FT4 shear regime. Do not equate a macroscopic shear-cell cohesion intercept with an SJKR surface-energy input. (fernandezUnknownyeardevelopmentandevaluation pages 40-43, shi2018effectofparticle pages 15-17, shi2018effectofparticle pages 1-2, butler2020standardsinadditive pages 8-11, groarke2021powdercharacterization—methodsstandards pages 33-38, pinotti2026additivemanufacturingof pages 204-206, groarke2021powdercharacterization—methodsstandards pages 1-5, shi2018effectofparticle pages 17-18)

Finally, validate **spreadability with an actual representative recoater and layer thickness**: measure deposited mass or relative bed density, area coverage, empty patches, surface-height variation and size segregation, and compare posterior-predictive DEM results across blade/roller speed and gap. Do not use tapped density as the spread-bed density. In one IN718 study, experimentally inferred layer packing was **61.2–62.4%**, whereas multilayer DEM tended toward **55–56%**; differing observation and simulation conditions limit that comparison. Another study found that fitting Hastelloy-X consolidation void ratio alone led to excessive modeled adhesion and an implausibly limited spread region. The doser fingerprint can therefore be a valuable *screen and prior* for alloy discovery, but its ability to forecast powder-bed quality must be established by held-out spreading experiments, particularly for irregular grains, fines and changing moisture. (shaheen2020theinfluenceof pages 11-14, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 9-11, mussatto2021influencesofpowder pages 7-10, habiba2023powderspreadingmechanism pages 15-17, katagiri2025bayesianoptimizationbasedparameter pages 11-14, gibbons2024metalpowderfeedstock pages 7-8)

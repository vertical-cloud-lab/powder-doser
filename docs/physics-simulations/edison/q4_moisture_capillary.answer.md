# Humidity-aware DEM for an auger powder doser

**The modeling distinction is between liquid already present at contacts and water supplied as vapor.** Pendular-bridge DEM can predict the forces exerted by an assigned small liquid volume. It does not, by itself, determine whether humid air produces that volume, how quickly water reaches powder inside a reservoir, or whether a dissolved salt subsequently recrystallizes into a persistent bond. Those require a sorption/transport and, for salts, a phase-change model. A dry SJKR-cohesion model that reproduces salt’s mass per revolution is therefore a valuable baseline, but not evidence that its cohesion parameter represents moisture or will transfer across humidity histories. (gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 5-7, samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 1-5, coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34)

## Contact physics and humidity

In the **pendular regime**, isolated menisci connect particle pairs. A DEM contact law combines surface-tension and Laplace-pressure attraction, with force depending on separation, particle radii, surface tension, contact angle and bridge volume; the force persists after particles lose mechanical contact and vanishes at bridge rupture. A commonly used small-separation pull-off scale is approximately \(2\pi R_*\gamma\cos\theta\), where \(R_*\) is an effective radius; rupture distance scales approximately as \(V_b^{1/3}\), with contact-angle corrections. The simplified Willett approximation supplies a separation-dependent force; classical Willett is a more detailed fit. Lian gives a useful rupture-distance approximation. Rabinovich’s minimum-energy expression is another choice, but Gabrieli and colleagues note Lambert’s objection that adding a separate adhesion term to that energy-derived force can double-count it. These alternatives should be benchmarked with the same radius and volume conventions before interpreting fitted parameters physically. Isolated-pair laws cease to be sufficient when liquid bridges merge or liquid redistributes substantially. (schwarze2013rheologyofweakly pages 5-6, coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 18-20, bagheri2025discreteelementsimulations pages 5-9, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 7-9)

**Relative humidity (RH) is not bridge volume.** For vapor-condensed water, the Kelvin relation links equilibrium meniscus curvature and capillary pressure to water activity, approximately \(p_c=-(RT/V_w)\ln(\mathrm{RH})\) when RH is expressed as a fraction, subject to curvature and sign conventions. One must additionally determine *which contacts contain water*, their accessible geometry and roughness, and their liquid volumes. At the micro-/millimetre scale, wet-granular simulations often prescribe bridge volume instead and calculate capillary pressure from it—an importantly different boundary condition. An empirical \(F_{\rm adh}(\mathrm{RH})\) or RH-dependent SJKR energy is a defensible **effective** model for rapid screening, but it cannot identify a unique liquid-bridge volume, sorption rate or salt phase from dosing data alone. Electrostatics may also change with RH, so an observed flow change need not be capillary-only. (gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 5-7, janewska2024simulatingcapillaryforce pages 5-9, lu2017theeffectsof pages 3-7, lu2017theeffectsof pages 7-9)

For **NaCl**, the approximate deliquescence RH is **75%** for sufficiently large crystals under the conditions discussed by Hird and Bolton. Above deliquescence, a salt-solution film or bridge—not pure-water wetting of an inert sphere—can form; upon drying, dissolution and recrystallization can leave *solid bridges*. Thus the before/after-drying force can increase even when the final water content falls. Samain and colleagues demonstrated this sequence explicitly in a different crystalline material, sucrose: crystals were humidified at 89.5% RH, 25 °C, then dried at 30% RH; shear-measured cake strength increased with water taken up before drying. Their sucrose threshold, 85% RH, must **not** be assigned to NaCl or BaCl₂. I did not establish a reliable BaCl₂ deliquescence threshold or hydrate-transition conditions from the retrieved evidence; measure or obtain phase-specific data for the supplied BaCl₂ grade rather than borrowing NaCl’s threshold. Hygroscopic polymers, flour and metal powders likewise need separate sorption and surface-chemistry characterization. (hird2016migrationofsodium pages 2-4, samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 1-5, samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 7-11)

To represent **exposure duration**, measure adsorption *and desorption* mass-versus-time curves at controlled temperature and RH, for both a thin sample and the actual filled reservoir. A practical reduced model uses an equilibrium sorption isotherm \(w_{\rm eq}(\mathrm{RH},T)\), an uptake law such as \(dw/dt=k[w_{\rm eq}-w]\), and, when needed, vapor diffusion through the bed or particle interiors. Map local \(w\) to bridge occupancy/volume and to independently evolving solid-bond fraction or strength. Treat adsorption, drying and repeated RH cycles separately; the first-order law is an approximation to test against measured kinetics, not a property supplied by a standard capillary contact law. DEM with shrinking bridge volumes has reproduced evaporation-driven granular-slope evolution, but that study prescribed an evaporation law and accelerated it heavily to make the mechanical simulation feasible—it did not solve arbitrary ambient-RH sorption in a doser. (gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 1-3, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 5-7, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 20-24, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 18-20)

## What has been validated?

- **Wet shear, with liquid deliberately added:** Badetti and colleagues obtained *quantitative* agreement between pendular-bridge DEM and rheometry of wet polystyrene beads after fixing interparticle friction from the dry material (approximately 0.09). Their controlled variable was added liquid content and applied stress, **not RH**. Their results also caution against treating bulk Mohr–Coulomb cohesion as a universal constant at low confining pressure. (badetti2018shearstrengthof pages 2-3, badetti2018shearstrengthof pages 1-2)
- **LIGGGHTS bridge force and bulk rheology:** Schwarze and colleagues checked their Willett implementation against two-sphere separation experiments using 13.6, 31.3 and 74.2 nL bridges. Their wet-granular rheometer simulation reproduced *trends* in apparent viscosity, but the authors did not claim a completed quantitative constitutive match to the bulk experiment. This is pair-force and added-liquid validation, not a humid-air dosing test. (schwarze2013rheologyofweakly pages 5-6, schwarze2013rheologyofweakly pages 6-8, schwarze2013rheologyofweakly pages 9-11)
- **Repose/slope during drying:** Gabrieli and colleagues compared an evaporating wet-glass-bead slope with DEM: the experimental initial wet slope was about 43°, versus about 28° for dry material; the simulated final upper and toe angles were about **35° and 21°**, comparable with experimental upper and toe regions of about **37° and 19°**. This supports the *mechanism* of weakening as bridges evaporate, but its initial water was added and evaporation was prescribed, not inferred from controlled-RH exposure. (gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 20-24, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 18-20)
- **Controlled-RH powder flow, without matching DEM validation:** Lu and colleagues conditioned lactose blends for **48 h at 0%, 30%, 58% and 85% RH**, then measured FT4 dynamic-flow, permeability and shear responses. Flow was relatively good at intermediate RH and deteriorated at 85% RH; the change depended on fines fraction and was not simply monotonic. Their measured FT4 dynamic-property panels provide useful experimental comparators, **not** a validation of a humidity-aware DEM contact law. Sucrose humidity-cycle caking and shear-strength experiments likewise validate the importance of drying history, not an auger simulation. I did not find an equally substantiated *controlled-RH, same-powder* demonstration jointly matching DEM shear cell, repose and dosing/discharge across all conditions. (lu2017theeffectsof pages 1-3, lu2017theeffectsof pages 7-9, lu2017theeffectsof media 903ed815, lu2017theeffectsof media d3ea6a04, samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 7-11)

## A tractable pre-/post-exposure dosing study

1. **Control and measure the material state.** Split each powder lot into dry/control, humid-exposed, and humid-then-redried aliquots. Log actual RH and temperature *inside or immediately at the reservoir*, exposure duration, initial fill/packing, sample mass gain or loss, and delay before each test. Include levels below and across NaCl’s approximate threshold, with suitable containment and material-specific limits; determine BaCl₂ transitions independently. Measure an uptake/desorption time course rather than assuming that the reservoir equilibrates in the same time as a thin test sample. (hird2016migrationofsodium pages 2-4, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 5-7, samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 7-11)
2. **Collect matched fingerprints and independent tests.** Randomize replicate runs across tilt, speed, fill level and tap setting; record the *distribution* of mass per revolution, tap-released mass, afterflow and zero-flow increments, including complete blockages. At the same conditioning state, measure the proposed ASTM D7891 FT4 and D6773 ring-shear responses, B213/B964 funnel flow or no-flow, B212/B527 bulk/tap densities, repose, B214/B822 size distributions, and loss on drying. Document sample preparation and consolidation: comparisons of FT4 and Schulze-family shear devices can differ systematically because pre-shearing differs. Hall/Carney no-flow is an informative outcome, not missing data. (shi2018effectofparticle pages 1-2, lu2017theeffectsof pages 1-3)
3. **Calibrate hierarchically.** First constrain dry size distribution, packing, particle–particle and PLA/PETG-wall sliding/rolling friction, restitution and geometry with dry tests; use the supplied 105 mg/revolution salt match as an *out-of-sample baseline check* where possible. Then infer an effective humidity-dependent force, or explicit bridge occupancy/volume and rupture law if independently measured sorption supports them. Add persistent solid bonds for humid-then-dried salt only if that history requires them. Fit a joint likelihood for continuous mass and time outputs, tap response, and binomial/sequence-aware no-flow events; include balance noise, run-to-run packing and simulator discrepancy. Repose alone cannot separate friction, rolling resistance and adhesion: published calibration examples produce different parameter sets across slump and hopper tests. Report posterior *effective parameters and correlations*, not an unjustified unique microscopic surface energy. (coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34, shi2018effectofparticle pages 1-2, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 18-20)
4. **Spend DEM runs on discrimination, not brute-force posterior sampling.** At the stated **26–30 CPU-min per simulated revolution**, screen sensitivity and fit an uncertainty-aware surrogate using selected tilt/speed/RH-history conditions, then run direct DEM checks at posterior extremes and held-out conditions. Advance the sorption/solid-bond state on the measured exposure-time scale and use DEM for the comparatively short dispensing/tapping intervals; explicitly model the 3 mm outlet and particle–printed-wall interactions. A repeatable failure of a liquid-only model to predict the redried sample’s no-flow rate or tap response would be evidence to test a caking/bond-history model, **not** proof of a particular salt bridge chemistry. (samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 1-5, gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 20-24, coetzee2022reviewthecalibration pages 32-34)
5. **Test transfer to additive-manufacturing spreading separately.** Carry the *posterior distribution* of contact and size parameters into a recoater/roller geometry and measure deposited-layer mass or density, coverage/voids and surface roughness at controlled RH and spreading speed. Dosing from an auger, gravity funnel flow and formation of a thin moving powder bed impose different stresses and boundaries; even a well-matched dosing fingerprint does not alone validate spreadability. Published spreading work assesses layer packing and nonuniformity directly rather than identifying layer quality from repose alone. (haydari2021thespreadingbehaviour pages 51-54, lee2019quantificationofpowder pages 21-28, alchikhsulaiman2020powderspreadingand pages 63-66)

**Bottom line:** A before/after humid-air dosing experiment is feasible now as an experimentally conditioned, state-dependent DEM comparison. Predicting its *time course* or irreversible salt caking requires measured sorption/phase history and new coupling; none of the wet-bridge implementations established here should be treated as a ready-made, validated RH-to-NaCl/BaCl₂-cake-to-auger-to-powder-bed pipeline. (gabrieli2012micromechanicalmodellingof pages 5-7, bagheri2025discreteelementsimulations pages 1-5, samain2019characterizationofcaking pages 1-5, lu2017theeffectsof pages 1-3)

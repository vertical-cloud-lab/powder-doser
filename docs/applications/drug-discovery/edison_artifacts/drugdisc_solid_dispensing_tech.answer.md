# Automated Milligram- and Gram-Scale Solid/Powder Dispensing in Pharmaceutical Research: A Technology Review

## 1. Introduction and Context

Automated powder dispensing remains a critical bottleneck in pharmaceutical high-throughput experimentation (HTE), self-driving laboratories (SDLs), and drug discovery workflows. The upstream step of weighing a solid into a vial—before dissolution and subsequent nanoliter-to-picoliter liquid dispensing onto microplates—demands milligram-to-gram accuracy, speed, and robustness across chemically and physically diverse powders. Solids present challenges that liquids do not: they may be light, fluffy, heavy, electrostatically charged, hygroscopic, or granular, and these physical property variations create substantial measurement and handling errors (nsouli2025advancingorganicchemistry pages 11-11). This review synthesizes the available literature on commercial, capsule-filling, conditioned-solid, and academic low-cost powder-dispensing approaches, with particular attention to their applicability for drug-discovery workflows and self-driving laboratories.

## 2. Major Commercial Gravimetric Platforms

### 2.1 Mettler Toledo Quantos / CHRONECT XPR

The Mettler Toledo Quantos is one of the most widely used automated powder dosing systems in pharmaceutical HTE. It employs a hopper-based module with spiral metal impellers that apply a rotary tapping motion to control powder flow onto an integrated analytical balance (christensen2021automationisntautomatic pages 3-6, jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 1-2). In independent benchmarking by Jiang et al. (2023), the Quantos was tested across thirteen chemically diverse solids at target doses of 20, 200, 500, and 1000 mg. At 200–1000 mg targets, many tractable powders achieved errors within approximately ±0.02–2% (e.g., −0.02% for Al₂O₃ at 1000 mg, 0.16% for NaNO₂ at 1000 mg, −0.01% for pectin at 1000 mg). However, at the 20 mg target, errors increased substantially: −6.00% for Al₂O₃, −5.04% for CaCO₃, −3.96% for NaNO₂, and −6.42% for granulated sugar (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-10). Complete dispensing failures (−100% error) occurred for compressible and hygroscopic materials including ammonium acetate and molecular sieve, attributed to clogging of the spatula outlet (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-10). Dispense times for sodium nitrite ranged from 285 ± 2 s at 20 mg down to 31 ± 3 s at 200 mg and 40 ± 4 s at 1000 mg (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10). The overall average dispensing failure rate across all tested materials was 26.9% (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-11).

The CHRONECT XPR platform, developed by Axel Semrau in partnership with Mettler Toledo, integrates XPR balances with robotic dosing heads and vial logistics for fully automated workflows. It is identified alongside the newer Chemspeed platforms as capable of low- to sub-milligram dispensing, though independent accuracy data were not found in the retrieved literature (nsouli2025advancingorganicchemistry pages 11-11).

### 2.2 Chemspeed GDU-S SWILE, GDU-Pfd, FLEX, SWING, and Crystal Powderdose

Chemspeed Technologies offers a family of automated solid-dispensing platforms. The GDU-S SWILE is distinguished by its positive-displacement mechanism using a piston and glass capillary tube, combined with an internal measurement balance (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 1-2, christensen2021automationisntautomatic pages 3-6). This configuration enables dispensing of very small quantities and has been shown to be "highly effective in sub-milligram to low-milligram dispense quantities," including for sticky or oily solids that defeat passive hopper systems (christensen2021automationisntautomatic pages 3-6). In the foundational multi-company benchmarking study by Bahr et al. (2018), six different powders were dispensed over 3,240 times across three different platforms from 0.1 mg to 50 mg, and the Chemspeed GDU-S SWILE system was found to be "favorable for sub- to low mg dispensing of free-flowing or non-cohesive powders" (biyani2021advancementinorganic pages 4-7).

However, independent testing by Jiang et al. (2023) revealed that Chemspeed accuracy is highly material-dependent. At 1000 mg, errors ranged from −91.01% for Al₂O₃ (attributed to mechanical dispensing failures) and −0.41% for pectin to complete failure (−100%) for ammonium acetate (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-10). At 200 mg with all solids included, the average error spread was approximately 12% (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10). Dispense times for NaNO₂ were 62 ± 23 s at 20 mg and 109–134 s at 200–1000 mg (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10). The overall failure rate was 26.5% (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-11). Failures were attributed to problems with the opening/closing mechanism, abrasion of moving parts, and deliquescent materials adhering inside cartridges (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10).

The Chemspeed SWING is described as more accurate for dispensing larger masses but not well suited for small target quantities, while the newer Chemspeed FLEX can measure low- to sub-milligram quantities (nsouli2025advancingorganicchemistry pages 11-11). The Chemspeed Crystal Powderdose is compatible with ChemBead formulations, achieving <5% RSD across 96-well formats when dispensing conditioned beads (tu2026sticktothe pages 4-5).

### 2.3 Unchained Labs (Freeslate) Junior and Big Kahuna

Unchained Labs platforms (successors to Freeslate/Symyx) are integrated HTE workstations combining solid handling, liquid handling, mixing, temperature control, filtration, and plate logistics. Their newer configurations offer low- to sub-milligram dispensing capability, though independent quantitative accuracy data were not found in the retrieved literature (tu2026sticktothe pages 4-5, nsouli2025advancingorganicchemistry pages 11-11). These systems, along with Chemspeed and Tecan, are identified as dominant vendors in the HTE automation space (christensen2022thedevelopmentand pages 62-68).

### 2.4 Other Commercial Vendors

Zinsser Analytic, Labman, Sartorius, Hamilton, Brooks/Titian, and Tecan offer various automated powder handling and liquid handling solutions. Their platforms are mentioned in the broader HTE literature as part of integrated automation ecosystems, but independent head-to-head powder dispensing benchmarking data comparable to the Bahr et al. studies were not identified for these specific vendors in the retrieved evidence.

## 3. Capsule Micro-Dosing Systems

### 3.1 Capsugel Xcelodose

The Xcelodose® system uses a vibratory "pepper-shaker" approach to fill capsules. A hopper fitted with a screen fine enough to prevent gravity-driven flow discharges powder into capsules via controlled vibration that reduces interparticle friction. The system handles doses from approximately 0.1 mg to several hundred milligrams and operates at approximately 600–1,200 capsules per hour (pinzon2012modellingofdosatora pages 34-38, pinzon2012modellingofdosator pages 34-38). A newer vibratory "pepper-pot" approach can precisely dose as little as 10 µg but is limited to approximately 600 capsules per hour (ding2021aqualityby pages 18-19). Dose-weight uniformity depends strongly on particle size, powder flow properties and bridging behavior, dispenser-head geometry (hole size and number), and the number of taps applied (pinzon2012modellingofdosatora pages 34-38). A stated advantage is that all powder enters the dosage form, producing zero powder waste (pinzon2012modellingofdosator pages 34-38). Dose-specific RSD values were not provided in the retrieved evidence, though acceptable uniformity for clinical trials was reported at 1–45 mg for favorable powder classes (faulhammer2014lowdosecapsulefilling pages 1-2).

### 3.2 3P Innovation Fill2Weigh

The Fill2Weigh system uses a gravimetric, vibratory micro-dosing approach with repeated weighing. Independent performance data were not available in the retrieved literature, though its operating principles are similar to the Xcelodose family.

## 4. Vibratory / Sieve-Chute Micro-Feeders

### 4.1 Khinast/Graz Micro-Feeder

Fathollahi et al. (2020) evaluated a high-precision low-dose micro-feeder developed at TU Graz. This piston/cartridge-based volumetric powder pump with a scraper can feed powders at rates as low as 1 g/h, with testing across a 1–20 g/h range (fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 11-12). It was designed to handle fine, cohesive, sticky, and low-density powders that defeat conventional feeders. However, tests with fine/cohesive API and spray-dried intermediate materials showed flow-rate deviations of 10–20% (fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 11-12), and related work reported variations of 4–20% depending on the material and feed rate (hou2024developmentofa pages 57-61). A key advantage is very low material consumption: tens of milliliters of powder suffice for testing, compared with 1–2 kg required by larger feeders, making it valuable for potent or expensive APIs in early development (fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 12-13).

## 5. Alternative Dispensing Strategies

### 5.1 ChemBeads (Coated-Bead Solid Dispensing)

ChemBeads, developed at AbbVie, represent a paradigm shift in solid dispensing for HTE. Solid reagents are dry-coated onto chemically inert glass beads using resonant acoustic mixing (RAM), which applies low-frequency, high-intensity acoustic energy to distribute the reagent uniformly over the bead surface through weak van der Waals interactions (tu2026sticktothe pages 3-4, tu2026sticktothe pages 2-3). Typical reagent loadings are 0.5–20% w/w, with approximately 5% being a versatile formulation that preserves flowability and homogeneity (tu2026sticktothe pages 2-3). Vibratory sieving after coating removes fines and aggregates to improve flow uniformity.

ChemBeads convert poorly flowing, cohesive, electrostatically charged native powders into uniform, free-flowing particles that can be handled volumetrically by robotic dispensing systems or manually using calibrated scoops (tu2026sticktothe pages 2-3). Automated dispensing achieves RSD values typically below 5% across 96-well formats (tu2026sticktothe pages 4-5), while a manual parallel bead dispenser demonstrates ±5% accuracy for 20 mg targets across 100 consecutive samples (tu2026sticktothe pages 9-10) and ±10% error for smaller quantities (gesmundo2023chembeadsenabledphotoredoxhighthroughput pages 2-3). The technology supports reactions at scales as low as approximately 400 nmol and can deliver 0.25–1 mg of material per well for nanomole-scale screening (tu2026sticktothe pages 7-8, tu2026sticktothe pages 10-12). ChemBeads are compatible with commercial dispensing platforms including Chemspeed Crystal Powderdose, Chemspeed SWILE, Mettler Toledo Quantos, and Unchained Labs Junior (tu2026sticktothe pages 4-5).

### 5.2 Dissolve-and-Dispense Stock-Solution Workaround

The dissolve-and-dispense approach prepares a known-concentration stock solution from the solid, then distributes it using established liquid-handling automation. This is attractive because liquid dispensing can be highly accurate, and acoustic droplet ejection (e.g., Labcyte Echo) enables nanoliter-scale downstream delivery. However, the approach is labor-intensive to prepare, unsuitable for poorly soluble compounds, may require toxic or volatile solvents, and can introduce concentration uncertainty from evaporation, adsorption, or precipitation (tu2026sticktothe pages 1-2, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 1-3). These limitations make direct solid dispensing preferable when solvent addition is undesirable, material is scarce, solubility is limited, or the true solid mass must be delivered without bulk stock preparation (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 1-3, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 3-6).

### 5.3 STORMS: Stochastic Robotic Microsampling

STORMS (STOchastic Robotized MicroSampling), developed by Lisowski et al. (2026), represents a novel approach that deliberately abandons deterministic mass control in favor of stochastic sampling with post-dispensing weighing. A vacuum-assisted coring tool with interchangeable stainless-steel tubes (0.6 mm ID for ~0.1–1 mg; 1.5 mm ID for ~1–10 mg) samples powder from source vials, ejects it into pre-weighed glass capsules that are laser-sealed, and the exact mass is determined gravimetrically (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 9-13, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 3-6). Sub-milligram dispensing achieves an absolute standard deviation of approximately 0.06 mg, though practical precision is limited by the analytical balance's effective repeatability of about 0.2 mg under operating conditions (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 13-15). Each capsule requires approximately 20–30 s, a four- to fivefold throughput improvement over deterministic dispensing (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 13-15, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 6-9). A critical finding is the Carr index threshold: passive sampling achieves approximately 96% success below a Carr index of 30, but drops to approximately 6% above that threshold; vacuum assistance improves performance for higher-Carr-index powders (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 15-18, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 13-15).

## 6. Open-Source and Low-Cost Academic Systems for Self-Driving Labs

### 6.1 Dual-Arm Robotic Dispenser (Cooper Group, University of Liverpool)

Jiang et al. (2023) demonstrated an autonomous biomimetic solid-dispensing system using a dual-arm robotic manipulator that manipulates ordinary spatulas, vials, and hoppers over an analytical balance with fuzzy-logic correction. The system achieved dispensing accuracy as low as 2 mg and, at 200 mg targets with non-challenging solids, demonstrated a mean error of 0.07% with 0.56% standard deviation—outperforming both Quantos and Chemspeed on these materials (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10). However, dispense times were substantially longer: 239–552 s per dose across 20–1000 mg targets (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10). The overall failure rate was 23.1% (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-11). The system can handle some abrasive and coarse materials that damage dedicated dosing heads, but fails with compressible, highly deliquescent, and sticky powders (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-11, jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 1-2).

### 6.2 SALSA 3D-Printed Archimedes Screw Doser

Zhang et al. (2026) developed SALSA, a low-cost automated salt solubility screening module for battery electrolytes that features a 3D-printed Archimedes screw solid doser. The motor-driven screw, housed inside a 3D-printed PLA tube, conveys and dispenses powder with closed-loop feedback from a balance positioned beneath the receiving vial. The system dispenses at least 0.3 g of solid, with the balance providing 0.1 mg resolution (zhang2026salsaalowcost pages 2-3). Interchangeable, customized 3D-printed doser heads accommodate different powder types: a standard funnel for NaNO₃ and a wider-neck, shorter-exit funnel for finer Li₂SO₄ to reduce clogging (zhang2026salsaalowcost pages 5-6). The system is explicitly designed as a low-cost alternative to expensive commercial solid dispensers (zhang2026salsaalowcost pages 2-3).

### 6.3 Self-Driving Tableting DataFactory

Abbas et al. (2025/2026) described an integrated platform for tablet formulation development using automated powder dosing (via a DEC Group FlexPTS volumetric doser), tablet compression, and performance testing within a Bayesian optimization loop. The dosing unit adjusts powder chamber volume via a piston position, collects powder by vacuum, and discharges with compressed air (abbas2025acceleratedmedicinesdevelopment pages 23-26). This illustrates the broader trend toward integrating powder dispensing into closed-loop SDL workflows for pharmaceutical development.

## 7. Dependence on Powder Properties

Across all platforms, dispensing performance is strongly dependent on powder physical properties. Key factors include:

- **Cohesion and flowability**: Cohesive powders with small particles (<50 µm, especially <20 µm) show greater interparticle forces, reduced flowability, and increased weight variability (hou2024developmentofa pages 64-67, faulhammer2014lowdosecapsulefilling pages 4-5). Powders with Carr index >30 (or equivalently, Hausner ratio >1.43) approach a threshold where passive dispensing mechanisms achieve very poor success rates (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 15-18, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 13-15).
- **Electrostatic charge**: Should be minimized through appropriate construction materials, as it can interfere with powder movement and contribute to adhesion or blockage (hou2024developmentofa pages 64-67).
- **Hygroscopicity**: Moisture promotes liquid bridges, surface tension effects, and increased cohesiveness, while also potentially altering bulk density and causing particle softening (hou2024developmentofa pages 64-67).
- **Particle shape**: Irregular shapes increase surface area, cohesive forces, mechanical interlocking, and friction; spherical or rounded particles generally improve flow (hou2024developmentofa pages 64-67).
- **Compressibility**: Highly compressible solids can clog spatula outlets and piston mechanisms, leading to complete dispensing failure (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-10).
- **Abrasiveness**: Can damage moving parts in both SWILE and Quantos systems (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-11).

Hausner ratios of 1.12–1.18 are associated with free-flowing powders suitable for automated dispensing, while higher values indicate increasing difficulty (ekenlebie2015pharmaceuticalprocessoptimisation pages 69-75). The Faulhammer et al. (2014) study of low-dose capsule filling demonstrated that RSD could not be simply correlated with dosing volume, but lower fill weights generally produced higher RSD values, and very fine, low-density, cohesive powders (group II) had significantly more difficulty meeting specifications (faulhammer2014lowdosecapsulefilling pages 9-10, faulhammer2014lowdosecapsulefilling pages 1-2).

## 8. Containment and Cross-Contamination

Published benchmarking studies of automated powder dispensers focus on accuracy and throughput rather than containment performance (OEB/OEL classifications). No independently verified OEB or OEL ratings for any of the reviewed platforms were found in the retrieved literature. In practice, containment for potent APIs is achieved through engineering controls such as enclosed gloveboxes, dedicated/disposable dosing heads, laminar airflow, and validated cleaning procedures. The Xcelodose system is specifically designed for clinical-trial capsule filling with high material recovery and enclosed loading for potent APIs (pinzon2012modellingofdosatora pages 34-38, ding2021aqualityby pages 18-19). STORMS's disposable sealed glass capsules inherently reduce cross-contamination risk (lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 1-3). ChemBead formulations reduce loose airborne dust during dosing, though coating preparation still requires neat-reagent handling (tu2026sticktothe pages 4-5). Cross-contamination between compounds in automated systems requires cleaning validation of all product-contact surfaces, which varies by platform architecture.

## 9. Approximate Cost

Pricing for commercial platforms is proprietary and not reported in published benchmarking studies. Based on market context: Mettler Toledo Quantos modules are estimated at US$30–60k, while complete integrated Chemspeed, Unchained Labs, and CHRONECT platforms represent six-figure capital investments (christensen2022thedevelopmentand pages 62-68, jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 1-2). ChemBead dispensers using manual calibrated scoops are low-cost, while RAM coating equipment and automated bead dispensers add substantial cost (tu2026sticktothe pages 4-5). Academic systems such as the SALSA 3D-printed Archimedes screw doser are explicitly designed as very low-cost alternatives (zhang2026salsaalowcost pages 2-3), though the exact bill of materials was not reported.

## 11. Implications for an Open-Source Gravimetric Powder Doser in Drug Discovery

For an inexpensive, open-source gravimetric powder doser targeting the milligram-to-gram range (as described in the user's context), the pharmaceutical drug discovery application space includes:

1. **Solubility screening**: Weighing solid APIs into vials for dissolution studies, as demonstrated with Quantos-based automated solubility platforms (shiri2021automatedsolubilityscreening pages 4-7).
2. **Stock solution preparation**: The upstream weighing step before dissolve-and-dispense workflows that feed acoustic or pin-tool liquid dispensing onto microplates (tu2026sticktothe pages 1-2, lisowski2026highthroughputsolidmicrosampling pages 1-3).
3. **Salt and polymorph screening**: Preparing diverse solid formulations at mg-scale for crystallization screens.
4. **Formulation development**: Dosing API and excipient powders for miniaturized tablet or capsule studies (abbas2025acceleratedmedicinesdevelopment pages 23-26).
5. **Catalyst/ligand dispensing**: Preparing solid reagents at mg-scale for HTE reaction screening, complementing ChemBead approaches for sub-milligram needs.

The key finding from the benchmarking literature is that at gram-scale targets (≥200 mg), even simple gravimetric feedback systems can achieve errors well below 1% for free-flowing powders (jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 8-10, jiang2023autonomousbiomimeticsolid pages 10-10). The challenge increases at lower targets (<20 mg) and with cohesive/difficult powders. An auger/vibratory feed with load-cell balance in the loop—as described in the user's system—is mechanistically similar to the Khinast micro-feeder concept and the SALSA Archimedes screw doser, suggesting achievable performance in the 1–20 g/h feed rate range with powder-dependent accuracy of approximately 4–20% RSD for challenging materials (fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 11-12, hou2024developmentofa pages 57-61), improving substantially for free-flowing powders at larger doses.

Goal: we want a cheap per-batch flowability number for in-house gas-atomized aluminium alloy powders (Al 4047 now, other Al alloys later) intended for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), with powder SPREADING (recoating) as the use case. A commercial spreadability tester costs tens of thousands of dollars; we think our auger doser (mass-vs-time on a balance at a few tilt angles and auger speeds) can deliver proxy values. Future batches will be sieved before testing. The attached bundle (start with README_EDISON.md) holds a 12-powder battery (blocks A-E: balance noise, static hold, per-revolution feed factor at tilt 0/45/90 deg, continuous rotation at 15/45/90 rpm, single taps), literature powder properties incl. angle of repose (AoR), repeat salt runs on different days, a salt optimization campaign with bulk-phase telemetry at varied tilt x rpm, and same-settings production-dose telemetry for commercial AlSi10Mg (LPBF grade, flows steadily) vs unsieved in-house Al 4047 (clogged by oversize chunks).

Please do the following analyses, with code, figures and tables:

1. Resolution. Separate balance/read noise (block A, sigma_g, drift_g, shock flags) from true flow variability in the block C per-revolution masses at each tilt. Estimate within-run CV per revolution at 0/45/90 deg and the day-to-day (between-run) CV of feed factor from the repeat salt runs (and any other powder with >1 valid run). Compare against estimating feed factor as the slope of cumulative mass vs revolutions (or vs time) over a continuous multi-revolution segment (block D polls; bulk telemetry in the opt/production trials). From these, compute the minimum detectable difference in feed factor (two-sample, alpha 0.05, power 0.8) as a function of revolutions per tilt, and the number of revolutions needed to detect 3%, 5% and 10% differences between two batches tested on the same day.

2. Candidate doser features per powder. Compute: feed factor at each tilt; volumetric fill (feed factor / literature bulk density); tilt gain (e.g. FF90/FF0, FF45/FF0, or a fitted slope of log FF vs sin(tilt)); speed slope (block D mass/rev vs rpm); per-revolution RSD at each tilt; flow-intermittency metrics from the streamed mass-vs-time traces (CV or MAD of the detrended mass-rate, fraction of zero-flow intervals, spectral power at the auger revolution frequency vs broadband); spontaneous discharge in block B; priming (charging) transient length; tap quantum (block E). Rank-correlate each feature with literature AoR and Hausner ratio (state n and caveats; literature values are class-typical), and with each other, and identify the two least-redundant features that best track cohesion/flowability. Treat non-conveying powders as censored rather than as zeros.

3. Al 4047 vs AlSi10Mg at identical bulk settings (40 deg, 100 rpm, 2 Hz taps). From telemetry, characterise the Al 4047 flow decay vs the steady AlSi10Mg flow (rate, decay constant, intermittency) and say which features would distinguish an oversize/jamming problem from intrinsic cohesion.

4. Recommend, concretely: (a) a per-batch doser procedure of at most about 15 minutes (powder conditioning such as drying and sieving, hopper fill mass, tilts, rpm values, number of revolutions, taps, replicates, and a reference powder such as the commercial AlSi10Mg run in the same session); (b) the equations for TWO scalar flowability proxies most relevant to LPBF spreading, e.g. a gravity-assisted volumetric flow index and a flow-intermittency/cohesion index analogous to a rotating-drum dynamic angle and cohesive index; (c) the expected resolution of each from part 1; (d) a simple flag rule relative to the reference powder. Note limitations: the doser shears powder through an auger at low consolidation, while spreading is blade/roller shear of a thin layer, so say which spreading failure modes (oversize streaking, low layer density from fines/cohesion, agglomerates) each proxy can and cannot detect.

Return a concise markdown report with the key numbers in tables.

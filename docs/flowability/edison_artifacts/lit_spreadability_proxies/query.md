Context: we run a small open-hardware auger powder doser: a tube with a helical auger (stepper-driven, 5-100 rpm) fed from a small hopper, a servo that tilts the tube from horizontal (0 deg) toward vertical (90 deg, gravity assisting discharge), a solenoid that taps the tube, and an analytical balance (0.1 mg) under the outlet logging cumulative discharged mass vs time at about 2.5 Hz. Across a 12-powder battery (salts, food biopolymers, silicon, gas-atomized AlSi10Mg) the mass discharged per auger revolution ("feed factor") spans three decades and its rank correlates strongly with literature angle of repose (Spearman rho = -0.84). We now want to use the doser as a cheap per-batch flowability screen for in-house gas-atomized aluminium alloy powder (Al 4047, Al-12Si) intended for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), as a stand-in for a dedicated spreadability tester that costs tens of thousands of dollars. The powder will be sieved (e.g. -63 um or 20-63 um) before testing.

Please synthesize the peer-reviewed literature, with quantitative values and citations, on:

1. Which flowability measurements best predict LPBF powder spreadability / spread-layer quality (layer packing density, coverage, roughness, streaking, part density): Hall/Carney funnel flow, Hausner ratio/Carr index, static angle of repose, rotating-drum dynamic angle of repose and cohesive index (e.g. GranuDrum), avalanche angle / break energy / surface fractal (e.g. Revolution Powder Analyzer), FT4 rheometry (basic flow energy, specific energy, flow-rate index, compressibility), shear-cell flow function. Report reported correlation strengths (e.g. Spierings et al. 2016; Snow, Martukanitz & Joshi 2019; Cordova et al. 2020; Marchetti & Hulme-Smith 2021; others) and which metrics work for aluminium alloy powders (AlSi10Mg, AlSi12) whose low density raises the cohesion-to-weight ratio (granular Bond number).

2. Evidence that screw/auger feeder behaviour correlates with standard flowability metrics: feed factor and its normalisation by bulk density (volumetric fill efficiency), feed-rate variability (RSD), speed dependence of mass per revolution, tilt/inclination dependence of screw conveyors (e.g. Wang, Li, Muzzio & Glasser 2017; Bostijn et al. 2019; Van Snick et al. 2017; inclined screw conveyor studies), and any work using fluctuation statistics of discharged mass, tilting-tube or tilting-box onset angles, or vibration/tap-induced discharge as flowability indices.

3. How rotating-drum indices are defined (dynamic angle vs drum speed, cohesive index as interface-fluctuation standard deviation, avalanche statistics), and whether an analogous index can be computed from fluctuations in the mass-discharge-rate signal of a feeder outlet. 4. What magnitude of change in these metrics corresponds to a practically significant change in spreadability for gas-atomized metal powders (e.g. avalanche-angle or dynamic-angle differences of a few degrees; cohesive-index thresholds; Hausner thresholds), and which batch-to-batch variations of gas-atomized powder (fines fraction below 15-20 um, satellites, sphericity, moisture, surface oxide, particle size distribution width) drive those changes. How do sieving and drying shift these metrics?

5. Recommended test conditions for a reproducible flowability screen: sample conditioning/pre-shear, drying, relative humidity, replicates, reference powders, and known pitfalls (e.g. electrostatic charging, segregation in the hopper, oversize particles jamming).

Finish with a concise recommendation: which two inexpensive, scalar flowability numbers (and how to normalise them) would be the most defensible proxies for LPBF spreadability that a tilting auger + balance could deliver.

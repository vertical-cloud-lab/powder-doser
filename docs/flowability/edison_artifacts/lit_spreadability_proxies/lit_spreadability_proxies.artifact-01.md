| Method | Key metric(s) | Reported correlation with LPBF spreadability/layer quality | Key reference | Suitability for Al-alloy powders | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hall/Carney funnel | Time for a specified mass to discharge; non-flow or jamming outcome | Cordova found that Hall flow changed little among dried and as-received states and could not measure moisturized powders, whereas thin-layer applicators detected reduced layer density. Marchetti and Hulme-Smith found Hall/Carney unsuitable for cohesive powders and obtained no representative correlations because many samples jammed. | Cordova et al. (2020); Marchetti and Hulme-Smith (2021) (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051) | Poor to fair. Useful as coarse incoming inspection for freely flowing Al powder, but low-density or fine AlSi powder may bridge or fail to flow despite being LPBF-processable. | Binary censoring by non-flow; strongly affected by density, orifice, operator, and wall friction; unlike the recoating stress state; insensitive to subtle moisture or drying effects. |
| Hausner ratio / Carr index | HR = tapped density / bulk density; CI = 100 × (1 − bulk density / tapped density) | Across eight AlSi10Mg and six 316L powders, powder-layer density correlated strongly and negatively with HR: PCC = −0.89. General classifications place excellent flow near HR 1.00–1.11 and CI ≤10%, and very poor flow near HR 1.46–1.59 and CI 32–37%. Marchetti found major principal stress at 9 kPa versus HR had adjusted R² = 0.86. | Haferkamp et al. (2021); Marchetti and Hulme-Smith (2021); Gibbons et al. (2024) (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000050) | Good inexpensive comparator when sieve cut and conditioning are fixed; directly relevant to AlSi10Mg layer packing. | Tapping and compaction do not reproduce recoating; sensitive to filling and tapping protocol; density and PSD confound flow; Spierings found HR <1.25 incorrectly classified several fine metal powders as free-flowing. |
| Static angle of repose | Heap angle to horizontal | Snow et al. reported a relationship between angle of repose and camera-derived spreading behavior for three Al-alloy powders. AlSi7Mg populations ranked consistently: coarse and spherical powder 26 ± 3°, intermediate powder 30 ± 4°, and fine and irregular powder 39 ± 4°. Specific energy versus angle of repose gave adjusted R² = 0.78 in steel powders. | Snow, Martukanitz, and Joshi (2019); Muñiz-Lerma et al. (2018); Marchetti and Hulme-Smith (2021) (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000034, pqac-00000055, pqac-00000050) | Good low-cost screening metric for Al batches; sensitive to fines, irregularity, and moisture. | Static heap formation differs from dynamic recoating; sensitive to drop height, base, walls, and operator; cohesive powders may not form a reproducible cone; chemistry-independent thresholds are unreliable. |
| Rotating drum: dynamic angle and cohesive index (GranuDrum) | Dynamic or flowing angle; cohesive index, calculated from the integrated standard deviation of the detected powder–air interface; speed dependence and hysteresis | Brocksieper found that dynamic angle alone did not explain layer roughness Sa, but speed-dependent cohesive index followed blade-recoater Sa trends. It could predict speed response for a known powder, but not absolute spreadability of an unknown powder. AlSi10Mg had a mean avalanche angle of about 39° and the highest cohesive index above 60 rpm. | Brocksieper et al. (2026); Lüddecke et al. (2021); Preud’Homme et al. (2021) (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057) | Particularly promising for low-density Al because the cohesive index measures intermittent cohesive flow and can be evaluated versus speed; AlSi10Mg was discriminated at high drum speed. | Results depend on drum speed, fill, end-wall friction, and aeration; dynamic angle may rise with speed for noncohesive powders but fall for cohesive powders; the cohesive-index relationship depends on recoater design. |
| Revolution Powder Analyzer | Pre-avalanche angle; avalanche-angle variance; surface fractal; volume expansion | A model combining avalanche and surface-distribution variables with particle ellipticity predicted optical AM-flowability ratings with R² = 0.94, p < 0.0001, and RMSE = 0.23. A surface-fractal value near 5 marked deterioration; corresponding avalanche angles were approximately 49° for Fe and 54° for Ni powders. | Spierings et al. (2016) (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041) | Mechanistically attractive for Al because avalanche intermittency and roughness respond to high cohesion-to-weight ratio; alloy-specific calibration is essential because the published model used Fe and Ni powders. | Validated against expert optical flowability rather than direct layer density; thresholds were machine- and alloy-dependent; mean avalanche angle had substantial scatter, so distribution width and surface fractal were needed. |
| FT4 rheometry | Basic flowability energy; specific energy; stability index; flow-rate index; compressibility | Brocksieper found specific energy was the only tested metric that identified AlSi10Mg as both the poorest-flowing and poorest-spreading powder; specific energy and GranuDrum cohesive index showed the greatest potential. In screw-feeding work, conditioned bulk density plus stability index plus specific energy explained 95.7% of feed-rate variation. Basic flowability energy showed weak relationships with most other metrics; specific energy versus angle of repose gave adjusted R² = 0.78. | Brocksieper et al. (2026); Santos et al. (2018); Marchetti and Hulme-Smith (2021) (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051, pqac-00000057) | Strong candidate for AlSi10Mg and AlSi12: low-stress specific energy captures particle friction and cohesion, and AlSi10Mg had the highest specific energy in the three-alloy comparison. | Expensive proprietary instrument; the confined downward stress used for basic flowability energy differs from recoating; absolute values depend on blade path, conditioning, and sample volume; mainly comparative rather than universal design data. |
| Shear cell | Unconfined yield strength; major principal stress; cohesion; flow function; internal and wall friction | No robust direct LPBF-spreadability correlation was established. Marchetti found major principal stress at 9 kPa versus HR had adjusted R² = 0.86, major principal stress at 3 kPa versus angle of repose had adjusted R² = 0.74, and flow-function and cohesion relationships with other bulk metrics. Conventional consolidated shear conditions may not represent thin-layer recoating. | Marchetti and Hulme-Smith (2021); Gibbons et al. (2024) (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051) | Useful for diagnosing cohesive Al powder and hopper behavior, but less defensible as a stand-alone proxy for LPBF spreading than specific energy, cohesive index, or avalanche statistics. | Sample preparation and preconsolidation strongly affect results; the stress state is generally higher and more static than recoating; different shear devices can classify the same powder differently; greater cost and sample demand. |

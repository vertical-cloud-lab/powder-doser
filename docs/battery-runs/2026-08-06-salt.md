# Salt — uniform powder battery, 2026-08-06 (the control run)

Seventh valid-feed run of the issue #116 battery, and the one the whole
dataset is measured against: the frozen block G three-phase parameters
were tuned on salt (the PR #124 demo that landed −0.7 mg), so this run
asks whether a parameter set that fits *one* powder still fits it when
run through the same fixed battery as everyone else.

It does. **Salt is the only powder in the set whose closed-loop doses
land on target** — two of three inside the ±5 mg tolerance, mean error
+0.2 mg, against −27 to −292 mg for the other five. That is the
manuscript's argument in one figure.

## Pre-flight

Tare, tilt 90°, five 360° revolutions at 30 RPM, then 10 taps:

| revolution | delivered (mg) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 20.7 |
| 2 | 12.5 |
| 3 | 12.0 |
| 4 | 10.8 |
| 5 | 9.6 |

**13.12 mg/rev, `feed confirmed`** on the first check — no escalation
needed. 10 taps added 10.5 mg.

The mild monotone *decay* (20.7 → 9.6 mg) is the opposite of the
charging transient white rice flour and CMC showed, and reads as the

after loading.

## Results

### Block A — baseline

Eight no-actuation reads, all exactly 0.0000 g. Noise floor below the
balance's 0.1 mg display resolution, so every number below clears it.

### Block B — static hold

No spontaneous discharge at 0°, 45° or 90° over 15 s. Seventh powder in
a row, and worth stating plainly now that the set is complete: **nothing
in this dataset avalanches through a stationary auger, even fully
vertical.** Flow is auger-gated, not gravity-gated, which is what makes
the auger a meter rather than a valve.

### Block C — feed factor vs tilt (6 × 360° at 30 RPM each)

| tilt | mg / revolution | RSD |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | 5.60 | 12.5 % |
| 45° | 17.10 | 34.8 % |
| 90° | 24.92 | 39.7 % |

Monotonic in tilt, ~4.5× from horizontal to vertical, and all three
tilts classify as `steady` revolution by revolution
(`salt_sequence.png`) — no charging, decay or intermittency inside the
block:

| tilt | revolutions 1–6 (mg) |
|---|---|
| 0° | 4.4, 6.1, 5.9, 5.2, 6.3, 5.7 |
| 45° | 17.2, 17.4, 21.9, 23.8, 15.5, 6.8 |
| 90° | 25.8, 15.0, 42.4, 22.0, 16.7, 27.5 |

The headline surprise is how *low* this is. Salt is the powder the rig
was designed around and the controller was tuned on, and at 24.9 mg/rev
at vertical it sits below white rice flour (37.2) and an order of
magnitude below calcium lactate (232.2) and xanthan gum (186.8). Being
the design baseline evidently means being the powder the *controller*
suits, not the powder that conveys fastest.

But see the drift below — this number is probably a lower bound.

### Block D — speed (3 continuous revolutions, tilt 45°)

| auger RPM | mg / 3 rev | mg / revolution |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | 151.7 | 50.6 |
| 45 | 58.9 | 19.6 |
| 90 | 53.7 | 17.9 |

The 15 RPM trace is the **cleanest pulsation record in the whole
dataset** — three discrete slugs of ~37, ~40 and ~44 mg, one per
revolution, separated by flat plateaus where the balance does not move
at all:

```
 1.2–3.5 s   +37 mg     3.5–5.5 s   +2 mg (plateau)
 5.8–7.5 s   +40 mg     7.5–9.3 s   +2 mg (plateau)
 9.5–11.3 s  +44 mg    11.3–13.6 s  +2 mg (plateau)
```

That is the mass-vs-time / pulsation panel PR #97 wants, and it is a
direct measurement of the quantum a screw feeder can resolve: at 15 RPM
this auger does not produce a stream, it produces one ~40 mg packet per
turn. No dose smaller than that packet can be metered by rotation alone
— which is precisely why phase 3 exists.

The apparent −65 % speed dependence (50.6 → 17.9 mg/rev) is the steepest
in the set, but **do not report it without the caveat in the next
section**: the 45 and 90 RPM points agree with block C at the same tilt
while the 15 RPM point does not, so this slope is entangled with the
drift.

### Block E — tapping is small but, for once, repeatable

| tilt | 360° re-feed rotation | single tap |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | 7.11 mg | 1.25 mg (RSD 34 %) |
| 45° | 45.79 mg | 3.05 mg (RSD 40 %) |

3.05 mg/tap at 45° is far below calcium lactate (20.36) and xanthan gum
(13.61), but the *relative spread* is the important part: 34–40 % RSD,
where white rice flour, CMC and sodium alginate all measured >180 % —
statistically indistinguishable from zero. Salt is the only low-feed
powder with a tap quantum that is small **and** reliable.

That matters more than the magnitude. Phase 3 is a trim actuator, and a
trim actuator needs to be *predictable*, not large. Salt's 1.25 mg tap
at tilt 0° with 34 % spread is exactly what a ±5 mg tolerance band needs,
and it is why salt is the only powder whose doses converge — see block G.

### The drift, and why block C is a lower bound

Blocks C and E both measure the same thing at the same tilt and the same
30 RPM: mass per 360° revolution. For six of the seven runs they agree
to within ~25 %. For salt at tilt 45° they differ by **2.7×**:

| run | tilt 45° block C | tilt 45° block E re-feed | E / C |
|---|---|---|---|
| white rice flour | 12.78 | 9.51 | 0.74 |
| sodium alginate | 9.58 | 10.35 | 1.08 |
| calcium lactate | 198.28 | 165.50 | 0.83 |
| carboxymethyl cellulose | 26.35 | 23.98 | 0.91 |
| xanthan gum | 161.18 | 136.35 | 0.85 |
| **salt** | **17.10** | **45.79** | **2.68** |

(Brown rice flour is omitted: both numbers are at the resolution floor,
so the ratio is meaningless.)

Every other conveying powder drifts slightly *down* across the run, which
is what depletion predicts. Salt drifts sharply *up*, and the same
upward drift shows inside block E itself — the eight re-feed rotations at
0° run 6.0 → 12.4 mg, and at 45° they run 40.4 → 59.3 mg.

It is not a simple function of time, though: block D's 45 and 90 RPM
trials sit between blocks C and E and read 19.6 and 17.9 mg/rev, i.e. at
the *block C* level, while block D's 15 RPM trial — measured a minute
earlier — reads 50.6, at the *block E* level. Two candidate mechanisms,
neither confirmed by this run:

1. **Tap priming.** Block E accumulates 8 taps at 0° before the 45°
   series begins. If tapping's main effect on salt is to pack the
   flights rather than to shake powder off the lip, the indirect
   contribution would dwarf the measured 3 mg/tap direct one.
2. **Tilt-change slumping.** Block D's 15 RPM trial and block E's 45°
   series both immediately follow a tilt change into 45°; the 45/90 RPM
   trials do not. Block C's 45° series also follows a tilt change and
   does *not* show it, which argues against this.

Consequences, in order of importance:

- **Salt's block C feed factor is a lower bound.** Its position in the
  cross-powder table (24.9 mg/rev at 90°, below white rice flour) should
  not be treated as settled. A repeat run is cheap — 17 minutes — and
  salt is the control, so it is worth doing.
- **The block D speed slope for salt is not interpretable.** Report the
  pulsation trace, not the −65 %.
- The comparison is only possible because blocks C and E happen to
  measure the same quantity. That redundancy was not designed in, and
  it is the only within-run drift check the battery has. Worth keeping.

### Block G — the control result: doses land on target

Frozen salt-tuned parameters, identical to every other run: bulk→fine at
0.500 g to go, fine→tap at 0.050 g, done at ±5 mg, bulk anticipation
0.12 g.

| dose | delivered | error | status | time | cycles | taps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.9953 g | **−4.7 mg** | `ok` | 265 s | bulk 16, fine 42, tap 35 | 70 |
| 2 | 0.9965 g | **−3.5 mg** | `ok` | 204 s | bulk 15, fine 39, tap 14 | 28 |
| 3 | 1.0088 g | +8.8 mg | `overshoot` | 113 s | bulk 14, fine 26, tap 0 | 0 |

**Mean error +0.2 mg** (std 7.5 mg), max absolute error 8.8 mg, mean
time to dose 194 s. Against the other six:

| Powder | mean dose error | statuses |
|---|---|---|
| **salt** | **+0.2 mg** | **2 × `ok`, 1 × `overshoot`** |
| calcium lactate | −26.5 mg | 3 × `stalled` |
| xanthan gum | −32.9 mg | 3 × `stalled` |
| carboxymethyl cellulose | −43.2 mg | 3 × `stalled` |
| white rice flour | −138 mg | 3 × `cycle-budget` |
| sodium alginate | −292 mg | 3 × `cycle-budget` |
| brown rice flour | — | 3 × `stalled` at ~0 g |

Every phase does its job, in a way no other powder managed:

- **Phase 1 bulk** converges in 14–16 cycles. The 0.12 g anticipation is
  correct here by construction — it was measured on this powder — so
  bulk hands over near 0.500 g to go rather than dumping several hundred
  mg of unfinished work into the fine phase.
- **Phase 2 fine** takes 26–42 cycles, against the 200-cycle ceiling that
  white rice flour and sodium alginate both hit.
- **Phase 3 tap** actually closes the gap on doses 1 and 2 — 70 and 28
  taps at 1.25 mg each, which is the only time in the dataset the tap
  phase has converged rather than stalled.

**The one failure is instructive.** Dose 3 overshot by 8.8 mg with zero
taps: fine stepped straight past the 50 mg handover and past the
tolerance band in a single increment, so phase 3 never ran. Doses 1→2→3
also got progressively faster (265 → 204 → 113 s) and their errors moved
monotonically upward (−4.7 → −3.5 → +8.8 mg). That is the same upward
drift the C/E comparison shows, now visible in the closed loop: as the
feed factor climbs, a fixed 45° fine increment delivers more mass, and
eventually more than the tolerance band is wide.

So the control result is really two results:

1. The frozen parameter set genuinely works on the powder it was tuned
   on — ±5 mg is achievable on this hardware, and the −27 to −292 mg
   errors elsewhere are a tuning problem, not a mechanical limit.
2. Even on that powder, it is only marginally stable. One dose in three
   fell out of tolerance from drift alone, with no change of powder, no
   change of parameters and 6 minutes between doses.

Point 2 is the stronger argument for the online estimation in #123/#130
than any of the failures: a controller that needs re-tuning between the
first and third dose of the *same* powder cannot be tuned once offline.

## Implications for the three-phase controller (#123/#130)

Adding to the ones raised by calcium lactate and xanthan gum:

1. **The fine increment must be capped by the tolerance band, not by a
   fixed angle.** A 45° increment delivers ~20 mg on salt late in a run;
   the band is ±5 mg. Whenever the measured per-increment mass exceeds
   the band, the increment has to shrink or the loop can only overshoot.
   This is the direct cause of dose 3, and the same mechanism as calcium
   lactate's phase-3 trap seen from the other side.
2. **Estimate the feed factor online *during* the dose, not once per
   powder.** Salt's changed by 2.7× inside a single 17-minute run. A
   per-powder calibration table would have been stale by dose 3.
3. **`overshoot` needs to be a first-class outcome.** Six powders
   produced only undershoots, so every controller implication drawn so
   far has been about delivering *more*. Salt shows the opposite failure
   is reachable, and it is the worse one — powder cannot be un-dispensed.

## Food-safe batch complete

Salt closes the food-safe set (`batch: food-safe-2026-08`): white rice
flour, brown rice flour, sodium alginate, calcium lactate, carboxymethyl
cellulose, xanthan gum, salt. Seven valid runs spanning three orders of
magnitude in feed factor (≤0.3 → 232 mg/rev) under one frozen parameter
set.

Standing gap: **block F (vibration) is missing from all seven runs** —
the DRV2605L motor is not attached. Every run records the skip, so it can
be back-filled.

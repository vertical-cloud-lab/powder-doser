## Pre-flight

Tilt 90 deg, five 360 deg revolutions at 30 RPM, then 10 taps.  Per
revolution: **-3.7, -2.2, +0.3, +92.5, +247.0 mg**; 10 taps added 28.5 mg.
Verdict `feed confirmed`, 66.8 mg/rev averaged over the five.

Three revolutions inside the noise, then the delivery section charged on
revolutions 4 and 5.  The auger was loaded but not hand-rotated, so the
flights filled during the check -- the same shape white rice flour and sodium
sulfate showed, and the opposite of calcium lactate, which reached steady
state on revolution one.  Block C then measured 302.4 mg/rev at the same
tilt, so the *averaged* pre-flight figure understates the real feed factor by
about 4.5x here.  That is a reminder in the same direction as the 2026-08-05
carboxymethyl cellulose run: the pre-flight is a go/no-go on the delivery
path, not a measurement.

The pre-flight also skipped its tare -- the balance was not reporting stable
-- and measured differences from where it sat.  That is the intended
behaviour since 2026-08-20; nothing downstream needs the displayed value to
be zero.

## Block C -- feed factor vs tilt

Six 360 deg revolutions at 30 RPM per tilt.

| tilt | mg / revolution | RSD | median per-trial sigma |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 deg | 57.2 | 30 % | 7.7 mg |
| 45 deg | 210.7 | 7.0 % | 8.3 mg |
| 90 deg | **302.4** | **4.4 %** | 9.0 mg |

Monotonic in tilt.  Unlike calcium lactate, sodium alginate, xanthan gum and
sodium sulfate, it does **not** saturate above 45 deg: 210.7 -> 302.4 mg/rev
is a further +44 %, where those four gained 13-17 %.  Vertical is worth
having for this powder.

**302.4 mg/rev at 90 deg is the highest feed factor measured in this
dataset**, ahead of sodium sulfate (243.6) and calcium lactate (232.2).  It is
roughly 1500x brown rice flour, which anchors the other end.

Revolution by revolution:

| tilt | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 deg | 65.1 | 44.5 | 30.7 | 55.1 | 70.1 | 77.5 |
| 45 deg | 234.2 | 217.8 | 195.9 | 195.4 | 207.2 | 213.7 |
| 90 deg | 300.2 | 312.6 | 321.0 | 294.9 | 283.3 | 302.1 |

All three tilts classify `steady` -- none charging, decaying or intermittent.
Only xanthan gum has done that before.  The 4.4 % RSD at 90 deg is the second
tightest in the dataset behind calcium lactate's 2.2 %, and it was measured in
a *worse* room, which makes it the more robust of the two.

The 30 % RSD at tilt 0 deg is not scatter of the same kind: the six
revolutions run 65 -> 31 -> 78, a dip and recovery rather than noise, and the
per-trial sigma there is 7.7 mg against a 26 mg swing.  Horizontal feed is
genuinely intermittent for this powder even though 45 and 90 deg are not.

## Block D -- speed

Three continuous revolutions at tilt 45 deg.

| auger RPM | mg / 3 rev | mg / revolution |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | 913.9 | **304.6** |
| 45 | 777.7 | 259.2 |
| 90 | 630.7 | **210.2** |

**Mass per revolution falls 31 % across a 6x speed change.**  That puts
silicon with calcium lactate (-33 %) rather than with white rice flour
(+94 %): filling-limited by time, where the flights fill under gravity at a
finite rate so a faster screw carries less per turn.

| Powder | mg/rev, 15 -> 90 RPM | change |
|---|---|---|
| White rice flour | 19.1 -> 37.0 | +94 % |
| Carboxymethyl cellulose | 23.4 -> 28.8 | +23 % |
| Sodium alginate | 11.1 -> 13.0 | +17 % |
| **Silicon -110/+200** | **304.6 -> 210.2** | **-31 %** |
| Calcium lactate | 190.4 -> 128.5 | -33 % |
| Salt (08-12) | 236 -> 126 | -47 % |
| Sodium sulfate | 423 -> 219 | -48 % |
| Xanthan gum | 233.3 -> 105.9 | -55 % |
| AlSi10Mg | 117.7 -> 44.2 | -62 % |

One caveat worth stating rather than burying: block C's 30 RPM point at the
same tilt is 210.7 mg/rev, which sits *below* block D's 90 RPM point (210.2)
rather than between its 15 and 45 RPM points.  Sodium sulfate showed the same
mismatch on 2026-08-20.  Block C steps single revolutions with a pause and a
measurement between each; block D runs three continuously.  Whether that is a
stepped-vs-continuous difference or block ordering cannot be separated from a
fixed-order battery -- randomising tilt and RPM order would settle it, and it
is the same experimental-design point the Edison review raised.

## Block E -- tapping is not resolved here

| tilt | 360 deg re-feed rotation | single tap |
|---|---|---|
| 0 deg | 55.71 mg (RSD 21 %) | **-3.73 mg** (RSD 200 %) |
| 45 deg | 174.59 mg (RSD 8.8 %) | **-3.52 mg** (RSD 189 %) |

The sixteen individual taps run -15.4 to +7.1 mg, ten of them negative.  A
mean that is negative, with a spread twice its own magnitude, is not a small
quantum -- it is the room.  Block A's eight *no-actuation* trials scattered
over 20.2 mg standard deviation in this run, so a genuine 15 mg/tap quantum
would have been invisible too.

**Recorded as not resolved, and excluded from the cross-powder tap table.**
That is a real loss, because it is the number this run would most usefully
have contributed: tap efficacy has so far tracked feed factor (calcium
lactate 20.4 mg/tap at 198 mg/rev, xanthan gum 13.6 at 161), and silicon has
the highest feed factor in the set.  The prediction is a large tap quantum;
this measurement can neither confirm nor refute it.  Block E alone is about
7 minutes and would settle it on a quiet bench.

The re-feed rotations are fine -- 174.59 mg/rev at 8.8 % RSD -- because they
are 50x the disturbance rather than a fifth of it.  Their ratio against block
C at the same tilt and RPM, measured about five minutes earlier, is
**0.83**, inside the 0.74-1.12 band every well-behaved run shows and well
clear of the 2.68 that condemned the 2026-08-06 salt run to a lower bound.

## Blocks A and B

**A** -- eight no-actuation trials at tilt 45 deg: +18.5, -8.2, -28.1, -1.0,
+29.0, +14.2, -9.2, -23.1 mg, standard deviation 20.2 mg, four flagged
`shock`.  On every pre-move run this block returned exactly 0.0000 g.  It is
the honest floor for this run and it is what disqualifies block E's taps.

**B** -- 15 s static holds: +4.9 mg at 0 deg, -32.8 mg at 45 deg, +46.4 mg at
90 deg, all three flagged `shock`.  Every value sits inside block A's spread
and the signs are inconsistent with any physical discharge (horizontal is the
least favourable orientation and read positive; 45 deg read negative).  The
conclusion is unchanged from the previous nine powders: **nothing in this
dataset avalanches through a stationary auger, even fully vertical.**  Flow is
auger-gated, not gravity-gated.

A hold is the one measurement the actuator gate cannot help: there is no
action to bracket against, so a knock during the hold is indistinguishable
from a discharge on physical grounds alone.  Reading block B needs block A
next to it.

## Environment

Pre-run survey, 240 s read-only before anything was touched:

| | |
|---|---|
| sample-to-sample jitter | 1.59 mg |
| stable frames | 4 % |
| drift over the longest quiet stretch (239 s) | -0.7 mg/min |
| mechanical step events > 10 mg | **0 in 240 s** |
| verdict | "even short trials are being disturbed" |

The survey said no.  It was run anyway, because the signature was unusually
favourable: **no shocks and almost no creep, i.e. a continuous disturbance**,
which is the one kind bracket-averaging removes well.  A shock is a transient
that the correction subtracts as a zero step; stationary jitter is beaten by
averaging inside each bracket.  A survey verdict is a summary, not a gate --
what it reports underneath matters more than the verdict line.

During the run itself:

| | silicon | sodium sulfate (2 h earlier) | salt (6 h earlier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| median per-trial sigma | **7.9 mg** | 5.9 mg | 12.4 mg |
| max per-trial sigma | 39.7 mg (a block B hold) | -- | -- |
| shock events | 9 | 9 | 36 |
| shock mass removed | 60 mg | -- | 539 mg |
| drift mass removed | 688 mg | -- | 920 mg |
| re-measured trials | 12 | 3 | 57 |
| clean trials | 5 / 64 (7.8 %) | 12 / 64 (19 %) | 1 / 48 (2 %) |

Against a 210-302 mg/rev signal a 7.9 mg sigma is 2.6-3.8 %, which is why
block C stands.  Against a sub-10 mg tap it is the whole measurement, which is
why block E does not.

The artifact rate decays through the run: every shock correction lands in the
first 2.5 minutes and then stops entirely.  Sodium sulfate showed the same
shape.  Both runs started within a couple of minutes of the operator loading
the auger, so the bench appears to be settling rather than the room quieting
down -- **a few quiet minutes between loading and starting should buy a
cleaner block A and B**, which is exactly where this run's two soft spots are.

## Why block G did not run

The pre-run survey put the environmental error over 180 s at 21.4 mg, against
block G's +/-5 mg acceptance band.  A closed-loop dose runs 2-14 minutes, and
unlike blocks A-E it has no do-nothing interval to bracket against, because
mass genuinely arrives throughout.  Software cannot recover that; it needs the
balance isolated from the bench.

## Pre-flight

Tilt 90 deg, five 360 deg revolutions at 30 RPM, then 10 taps.  Per
revolution: **195, 310, 265, 318, 334 mg**; 10 taps added 48.1 mg.  Verdict
`feed confirmed`, **284.5 mg/rev** averaged over the five.

Unlike silicon (`-3.7, -2.2, +0.3, +92.5, +247.0`) and unlike the
2026-08-11 AlSi10Mg attempt, **there is no empty-flight charging transient**:
revolution one already delivers 195 mg and the remaining four sit within
70 mg of each other.  The column was primed when the battery started, which
is why the pre-flight average (284.5) lands within 16 % of block C's
338.9 mg/rev at the same tilt instead of understating it several-fold.  That
is the exception, not the rule -- the standing guidance still holds that the
pre-flight is a go/no-go on the delivery path rather than a measurement.

The pre-flight skipped its tare (the balance was not reporting stable) and
measured differences from where it sat, which is the intended behaviour
since 2026-08-20.

## Block C -- feed factor vs tilt

Six 360 deg revolutions at 30 RPM per tilt.

| tilt | mg / revolution | sd | RSD | SEM | revolutions 1-6 (mg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 deg | 48.9 | 16.6 | 33.9 % | 6.8 | 28, 75, 48, 58, 34, 51 |
| 45 deg | 230.9 | 31.1 | 13.5 % | 12.7 | 182, 251, 201, 245, 252, 254 |
| **90 deg** | **338.9** | 15.1 | **4.5 %** | 6.2 | 344, 342, 333, 329, 365, 322 |

**338.9 mg/rev at vertical is the highest feed factor in the dataset**, ahead
of silicon (302.4), sodium sulfate (243.6) and calcium lactate (232.2), and
roughly 1700x brown rice flour at the other end.

Two things about the shape:

**It does not saturate above 45 deg.**  Feed climbs a further **+47 %** from
45 deg to vertical.  Every food-safe powder that saturates gains only
13-17 % over the same span (calcium lactate +17 %, sodium alginate +13 %,
xanthan gum +16 %, sodium sulfate +17 %).  Silicon was the first exception at
+44 %, and AlSi10Mg is the second.  Both metal powders keep gaining all the
way to vertical; every non-metal fast powder stops at 45 deg.  Two runs is
not a mechanism, but it is a consistent split worth testing deliberately.

**The 90 deg series is flat and the 45 deg series is not.**  At vertical the
six revolutions vary by 43 mg on a 339 mg mean and classify `steady`.  At
45 deg the first revolution is the lowest of the six (182 mg against a 249 mg
mean of the rest), which is the column recharging after the tilt change.
Dropping it raises the 45 deg mean to 240.7 mg/rev.

## The 45 deg figure is a mild under-estimate

Blocks C and E independently measure mass per 360 deg revolution at the same
tilt and RPM, minutes apart, and the ratio has been the run's internal
consistency check since the 2026-08-06 salt run.

| | block C @ 45 deg | block E re-feed @ 45 deg | E / C |
|---|---|---|---|
| this run | 230.9 | 275.8 | **1.19** |

Six earlier powders sit at 0.74-1.12, and the direction there is usually
slightly *down* as the column depletes.  This run runs the other way and
lands just outside the band.  The explanation is the same as block C's low
first revolution: the auger had been freshly reloaded and the column was
still filling, so later measurements are the better ones.  Two consequences,
stated rather than folded in:

- **Read the 45 deg feed factor as a lower bound.**  The block E re-feed
  figure of 275.8 mg/rev at 3.6 % RSD -- the tightest per-revolution spread
  in this run and among the tightest in the dataset -- is the better estimate
  of steady-state 45 deg feed.
- **The 90 deg figure is unaffected**; it was measured last, after the column
  had been turning for six minutes, and its own sequence is flat.

## Block D -- mass per revolution falls with speed

Three continuous revolutions at tilt 45 deg.

| auger RPM | mg / 3 rev | mg / revolution |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | 1066.9 | **355.6** |
| 45 | 920.8 | 306.9 |
| 90 | 547.0 | **182.3** |

**-49 % across a 6x speed change**, which puts AlSi10Mg with the
filling-limited-by-time family: flights fill under gravity at a finite rate,
so a faster screw gives each flight less time to fill.

| Powder | mg/rev, 15 -> 90 RPM | change |
|---|---|---|
| White rice flour | 19.1 -> 37.0 | +94 % |
| Carboxymethyl cellulose | 23.4 -> 28.8 | +23 % |
| Sodium alginate | 11.1 -> 13.0 | +17 % |
| Silicon -110/+200 | 304.6 -> 210.2 | -31 % |
| Calcium lactate | 190.4 -> 128.5 | -33 % |
| Salt (2026-08-12) | 236 -> 126 | -47 % |
| **AlSi10Mg** | **355.6 -> 182.3** | **-49 %** |
| Sodium sulfate | 423 -> 219 | -48 % |
| Xanthan gum | 233.3 -> 105.9 | -55 % |

This **reproduces the one finding that survived the invalidated 2026-08-11
run**.  That run measured -62 % over the same sweep, but at a plate stuck at
0 deg; this one measures -49 % at a genuine 45 deg.  The sign and rough
magnitude agree, so the speed dependence is a property of the powder rather
than an artefact of the stuck tilt.

Block D's 15 and 45 RPM points (355.6, 306.9) both sit *above* block C's
30 RPM point (230.9), which they should not if the two blocks agreed.  Block
E's later re-feed at the same 30 RPM gives 275.8.  So the ordering across the
run is C(30) < E(30) < D(45) < D(15) -- consistent with a column that kept
charging, not with a speed curve that is wrong.  Block D ran between them and
should be read as steady-state.

## Block E -- the tap quantum is not resolved

| tilt | 360 deg re-feed | single tap | tap RSD | negative taps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 deg | 54.0 mg | +5.89 mg | 220 % | 2 of 8 |
| 45 deg | 275.8 mg (RSD 3.6 %) | **-1.91 mg** | 742 % | **6 of 8** |

The 45 deg mean is *negative* and six of the eight individual taps are
negative.  Against block A -- eight trials in which nothing was actuated at
all, which scattered over **88.2 mg** with a standard deviation of 28.9 mg --
these taps bound nothing.  Recorded as **not resolved**, not as a small
quantum, and excluded from the cross-powder tap table.

This is now the **third consecutive high-feed powder** whose tap quantum the
bench environment has swallowed (sodium sulfate 2026-08-20, silicon
2026-08-20, this run).  That matters for a specific open question: tap
efficacy has appeared to track feed factor, with the only two resolved
quanta being calcium lactate (20.4 mg/tap at 198 mg/rev) and xanthan gum
(13.6 at 161) -- both measured *before* the move into the fume hood, in a
much quieter room.  Every powder measured since that would test the
hypothesis at the high end has been unmeasurable for environmental reasons.
The gap is systematic and is a property of the bench, not of the powders; it
closes when the balance is isolated, and block E alone is ~11 minutes.

## Block A and block B

**Block A** -- 8 no-actuation reads at tilt 45 deg: mean -2.3 mg, sd 28.9 mg,
range -48.1 to +40.1 mg.  Every valid run before the fume-hood move returned
deltas of exactly 0.0000 g here, so this run's noise floor is roughly three
orders of magnitude worse than the dataset's early runs.  It is the number
every other block in this run has to clear, and blocks C, D and E clear it
comfortably while block E's taps do not.

**Block B** -- 15 s static holds: +13.3 mg at 0 deg, -33.7 mg at 45 deg,
+40.2 mg at 90 deg.  All three sit inside block A's spread and the signs are
physically inconsistent (horizontal is the least favourable orientation and
read positive; 45 deg read negative).  Consistent with the previous ten
powders -- nothing in this dataset avalanches through a stationary auger,
even fully vertical -- but **this run does not independently establish it**.
A hold is the one measurement the actuator gate cannot help, because there is
no action to bracket against.

## The environment

The pre-run survey said not to run:

| | |
|---|---|
| sample-to-sample jitter | 1.497 mg (elevated) |
| stable frames | 4 % |
| drift over the longest quiet stretch | -0.7 mg/min (low) |
| **mechanical step events > 10 mg** | **0 in 240 s** |
| verdict | *"even short trials are being disturbed"* |

The run went ahead deliberately, on the same reasoning as the 2026-08-20
silicon run: the verdict line is a summary, and what sits underneath it
matters more.  Zero shocks with almost no creep means the disturbance is
**continuous**, and continuous jitter is the one kind that averages down
inside a bracket.  A shock is a transient the correction subtracts as a zero
step; stationary jitter is not, and waiting for a busy room to fall silent
never terminates.

Measured during the run:

| | this run | silicon (08-20) | sodium sulfate (08-20) |
|---|---|---|---|
| median per-trial sigma | **10.4 mg** | 7.9 mg | 5.9 mg |
| max per-trial sigma | 35.6 mg | -- | -- |
| shock events (host) | 15 | 9 | 9 |
| drift removed | 923.7 mg | 688 mg | -- |
| re-measured trials | 38 | 12 | 3 |
| clean trials | **1 / 64** | 5 / 64 | 12 / 64 |

A worse room than either 2026-08-20 run.  But the signal is also the largest
in the dataset, so in relative terms the run is comparable: 10.4 mg against
338.9 mg/rev is **3.1 %**, and block C at 90 deg came back at 4.5 % RSD with
a 6.2 mg SEM -- +/- 1.8 % on the mean.

The pipeline did visible work: 38 of 64 trials were re-measured after a
shock landed in their bracket, and 923.7 mg of drift plus 171.8 mg of shock
were removed.  Only one trial in 64 was clean.

## The cost of retries

Re-measuring a disturbed trial is free in a cohesive powder and expensive in
a fast one, and this run is the first where that is large enough to matter.

| | |
|---|---|
| measured mass, 64 accepted trials | **9.12 g** |
| mass delivered during the 38 discarded attempts | **6.45 g** |
| total drawn from the auger (balance) | **14.97 g** |

**43 % of the powder this run consumed went into measurements that were
thrown away.**  That is not a fault -- a retried trial has to repeat the
action, and a discarded 45 deg re-feed rotation moves a real 275 mg of
AlSi10Mg whether or not its bracket was clean.  But it means the retry
policy's cost scales with feed factor: the same 38 retries would have cost
brown rice flour under 10 mg.

Two practical consequences:

- **Budget fast powders at roughly 2x the nominal run, in a noisy room.**
  The protocol's 4-10 g estimate assumes few retries; this run needed 15 g.
- **It is a second, independent argument for isolating the balance.**  The
  granite is usually justified by making block G possible; it would also stop
  a fast metal powder spending nearly half its material on rejected brackets.

The run-log column reports the 9.12 g measured figure, which is the right
number for "how much powder does this measurement represent".  The number to
use for "is the auger running low" is the 14.97 g balance figure.

## Why block G did not run

The survey put the environmental error over 180 s at **14.5 mg** against
block G's +/-5 mg tolerance.  A closed-loop dose runs 2-14 minutes and,
unlike blocks A-E, has **no do-nothing interval to bracket against** --
mass genuinely arrives throughout, so the actuator gate cannot separate
powder from baseline movement.  That block needs the balance isolated
(granite plus a compliant pad, with the doser platform decoupled from the
balance's surface), not more software.

## QC

Recorded `valid_for_cross_powder_comparison = true`, `verdict = ok`, with
three caveats carried in the run document rather than folded away:

1. **The tap quantum is not resolved** and must not enter the cross-powder
   tap table.
2. **Block B does not independently establish** the absence of gravity
   discharge; it is consistent with it.
3. **Block C at 45 deg is a lower bound** (E/C = 1.19, column still
   charging).  The 90 deg figure is the reliable one.

Anyone comparing per-revolution *spread* across powders should treat this
run's RSDs as upper bounds -- the block A floor was 88 mg here where every
pre-move run returned 0.0000 g.  Comparing *means* is fine.

## What this replaces

The 2026-08-11 run reported AlSi10Mg as having "the flattest tilt response in
the dataset" -- 82.6 / 75.6 / 68.3 mg/rev at nominal 0 / 45 / 90 deg, easing
*downward* toward vertical.  Those were three repeated measurements at a
plate stuck at 0 deg, and the gentle decline was the column depleting over
the run.  With the tilt axis working, AlSi10Mg has one of the **steepest**
tilt responses measured: **6.9x from horizontal to vertical**.  The earlier
run stays `valid_for_cross_powder_comparison = false`; this one supersedes
it.

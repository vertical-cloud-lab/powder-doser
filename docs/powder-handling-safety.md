# Safety Guidelines — Loading and Dispensing Non-Food-Safe Powders

Scope: manual handling of fine metal powders (AlSi10Mg, elemental Si,
and the broader LPBF element palette in
[`candidate-powders.md`](candidate-powders.md)) while loading the
doser's auger/hopper and dispensing into crucibles, **in ordinary
ambient lab conditions** (no glovebox, no inert or dehydrated
atmosphere yet). Keeping-the-powder-dry practices are covered in
[`candidate-powders.md`](candidate-powders.md); this document covers
keeping the *operator and lab* safe.

> **These are working guidelines, not a substitute for your
> institution's EHS requirements or the supplier SDS.** Read the SDS
> for each specific powder lot before first use, and keep a copy at
> the workstation.

## 1. Know the hazards

Gas-atomised LPBF feedstocks are typically 15–63 µm, with a fines
tail well into the respirable range (< 10 µm). The two hazards that
dominate everything below:

- **Inhalation.** Fine Al/AlSi10Mg and Si particles are respirable
  dusts. Chronic aluminium fine exposure is associated with
  respiratory harm; Si fines are a lung hazard; several palette
  elements (Ni, Co, Cr, Mn, V) are toxic and/or sensitising, and Ni
  and Co compounds are carcinogen-classified. Treat every powder in
  the palette as a respiratory hazard.
- **Combustible / reactive dust.** Fine aluminium (including
  AlSi10Mg, which also contains Mg) is a combustible metal dust — a
  dispersed cloud can deflagrate, and a settled layer can ignite from
  sparks, static discharge, or hot surfaces. Ti and Zr fines can be
  pyrophoric. Burning metal powder **cannot** be extinguished with
  water or a standard ABC extinguisher.

## 2. PPE — minimum for any open-powder operation

Put PPE on *before* opening any powder container; keep it on until
cleanup is complete.

- **Respirator:** at minimum a well-fitted N95; **a half-face
  elastomeric respirator with P100 filters is strongly preferred**
  for metal fines, and required for Ni/Co/Cr-bearing powders.
  Users should be fit-tested per your EHS program. A dust mask worn
  loose over a beard does not count.
- **Eyes:** safety glasses; goggles if pouring or transferring more
  than a few grams.
- **Gloves:** nitrile, tight-fitting. Change immediately if torn or
  visibly contaminated; never touch phone/keyboard/face with powder
  gloves.
- **Body:** lab coat, snap/button closed, sleeves down. Prefer
  cotton or FR-treated coats — synthetic fleece both melts in a
  flash fire and generates static. Closed-toe shoes, long pants, tie
  back long hair.
- **Static:** ground yourself (touch a grounded metal surface, or
  wear a grounding strap) before handling Al/Mg/Ti/Zr powders,
  especially in dry air.

## 3. Workspace setup (engineering + admin controls)

- Work inside a fume hood or with local exhaust ventilation (LEV) at
  the transfer point whenever possible; otherwise choose a
  low-traffic, draft-free bench so powder is not carried around the
  lab.
- **Eliminate ignition sources** within the work area: no open
  flames, hot plates, grinding, or sparking tools; keep the doser's
  motors/electronics powered off during loading.
- Lay down a disposable bench liner (it makes spill capture and
  cleanup dramatically easier).
- Use metal or conductive scoops/spatulas and metal or ESD-safe
  containers; avoid pouring powder through plastic funnels that
  charge triboelectrically. Pour slowly and from a small height to
  minimise dust clouds.
- Post the area ("metal powder handling — PPE required") and keep
  bystanders out during transfers.
- Know where the nearest **Class D extinguisher or bucket of dry
  sand / Met-L-X agent** is before you start. If you only have
  ABC/water extinguishers, get a Class D source before handling
  aluminium powder in more than gram quantities.
- No food, drink, or cosmetics in the powder area. Ever.

## 4. Loading the auger / hopper

1. Don full PPE (§2); confirm the doser is powered off and the
   workspace is set up (§3).
2. Ground yourself, then open the stock bottle inside the
   hood/LEV zone. Keep the bottle open only as long as the transfer
   takes (this is also the keep-it-dry rule).
3. Transfer with a scoop or by slow pour through a conductive
   funnel directly into the hopper — do not free-pour from height,
   and do not tap or shake containers to dislodge powder (it
   aerosolises fines).
4. Cap the stock bottle immediately and wipe its threads with a damp
   wipe before returning it to the desiccator.
5. Visually check the auger inlet and doser exterior; damp-wipe any
   powder that landed outside the hopper *before* powering on. A
   settled layer on or near motors is an ignition risk.
6. Log the powder, lot, mass loaded, and ambient T/RH.

## 5. Dispensing

- Keep the respirator and other PPE on during dispensing — auger
  dosers and vibration stages generate airborne fines at the outlet.
- Position the outlet close to the crucible/receiving vessel to
  minimise free-fall height and dust generation.
- Stay with the machine; if you see powder leaking from seals or
  accumulating on the mechanism, stop, power down, and clean before
  continuing.
- Do not leave open crucibles of dispensed powder sitting out —
  cover or move them to the next process step promptly.

## 6. Cleaning the scale (and other equipment)

The balance sits directly under falling powder and accumulates fines
in exactly the places you touch. Clean it **after every session** and
between different powders (cross-contamination ruins alloy work and
mixes incompatible dusts):

1. With PPE still on, power off the scale and remove the weighing
   pan/crucible fixture.
2. **Never use compressed air or "dusting" blasts** — this creates
   exactly the dispersed metal-dust cloud you must avoid, and blows
   contaminant into the load cell.
3. Brush loose powder gently with a soft dedicated brush into a
   waste container, working slowly to avoid raising dust. A HEPA
   vacuum may be used **only if it is rated for combustible/
   conductive dust** — an ordinary shop or lab vacuum can ignite
   metal powder internally.
4. Wipe the pan, draft shield, and surrounding surfaces with a
   *damp* (not wet) lint-free wipe; damp-wiping captures fines
   without splashing water into electronics. Dispose of wipes as
   solid contaminated waste, not regular trash.
5. Let everything dry fully, reassemble, and re-verify the scale
   with a check mass before the next run.
6. Clean the doser's drip tray, outlet, and the bench liner the same
   way: brush → damp wipe → bag the liner.

## 7. Spills, waste, and fire

- **Small spill:** don't blow on it, don't dry-sweep vigorously.
  Damp-wipe or gently brush into a metal container.
- **Large spill:** clear the area, remove ignition sources, and
  clean up slowly with brush/damp wipes or a combustible-dust-rated
  vacuum. If in doubt, call EHS.
- **Waste:** collect powder-contaminated wipes, liners, gloves, and
  unusable powder in a closed, labelled metal container; dispose via
  EHS as metal-dust waste. Do not put aluminium fines in ordinary
  trash where they can mix with water or oxidisers.
- **Fire:** for burning metal powder use **Class D agent or dry
  sand only** — never water, never ABC. If a fire is beyond an
  immediate smother, evacuate and pull the alarm.

## 8. After every session

- Doff in order: gloves last after wiping down surfaces; wash hands
  and forearms before leaving the area.
- Bag the doser with desiccant per the storage guidance in
  [`candidate-powders.md`](candidate-powders.md).
- Log what was dispensed, any spills or anomalies, and check the
  desiccator indicator.

## Quick checklist

| Phase | Must-dos |
|---|---|
| Before | SDS read · PPE on (P100/N95, goggles, nitrile, cotton lab coat) · ignition sources off · Class D agent located · bench liner down · grounded |
| Loading | Doser off · slow scoop/pour, low height · cap bottle immediately · damp-wipe strays before power-on |
| Dispensing | PPE stays on · outlet close to crucible · stop on leaks |
| Cleaning | No compressed air · soft brush + damp wipes · combustible-dust-rated vacuum only · re-verify scale with check mass |
| After | Waste to labelled metal container · wash hands · bag doser with desiccant · log the run |

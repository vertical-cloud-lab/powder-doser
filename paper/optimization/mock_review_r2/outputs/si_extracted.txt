abbreviated as: taps bulk/trickle, bulk tilt (◦), trickle tilt (◦), tap tilt (◦), bulk speed (rpm), switch
threshold (g to go), stopping band δ (mg).

Table S8: How the manuscript addresses each item of Elsevier’s machine-learning requirements for Additive
Manufacturing. Two statistical models are essential to the conclusions: the SAAS GP of the calibration campaigns,
and the Spearman correlations of Section 3.6. The planned multi-task GP and the DEM emulator will be added to
this table when their results exist.


Item                Where and how

1. Several models      The SAAS GP (one per objective) chooses doses and recommends settings; Spearman
                      rank correlations test property links; a linear model, a MAP GP and a constant serve as
                          baselines (Table S6).
2. Model formulation   Purpose: sequential design and prediction (regression). Factors: eight settings (Table 2 of
                       the main text), two categorical; responses: time per dose and absolute error; co-factors
                      not modelled: day, fill state of the tube, humidity (their effect is shown in Section 3.2).
                        Supervised.
3. Dataset            42 doses of campaign 1 (Table S2) and 43 of campaign 2 (Table S4); the snapshot of all
                         records and the scripts are public. Outcome encoding of failed doses is stated in Section 2.4.
4. Data support mod- Screening corners give a balanced design (Table S1); main effects are large compared
elling                 with the scatter at the centre point for the error, not for time. Campaign 2 violates the
                      assumption of a stationary response (Section 3.2), which is why its BO doses are not used
                            for conclusions.
5. Is ML necessary?    Not shown at this sample size: a linear model ranks held-out doses as well as the GP
                       (Table S6). The GP is used for its calibrated predictive uncertainty, which the acquisition
                         function needs; the recommendation it made held up in eight repeats.
6. Choice of model    SAAS prior because 8 settings and fewer than 50 doses; samples per parameter: 42 doses
                            for a GP with 8 length scales, an outcome scale and a noise term per objective.
7. Training and testing  Leave-one-out cross-validation (Table S6, Fig. S1); NUTS with 256 warm-up and 128
                         retained draws (thinning 16); hyperparameter posteriors summarized in Table S7. Versions:
                 Ax 0.4.3 for the campaign, BoTorch 0.18 for the checks.
8.   Prediction perfor- RMSE, rank correlation, interval coverage, standardized residuals and their normality
mance                 (Table S6); residuals of the error objective are heavy-tailed (overshoots), which no model
                         captures; the validation block of the recommended settings fell inside the 95% predictive
                            intervals.
9. Impact              Against hand tuning (the prior practice):  3.1 times faster (2.7 at matched afterflow
                          constant), exact p = 0.004; against picking the best observed dose: more accurate, p = 0.005
                         (Section 3.1). [Multi-task benchmark against Sobol, single-task and carry-over baselines to be
                         added.]

**Title:** *Auger-Based Powder Dosing as a Mechanistic Probe of Powder Flow Behavior: Multi-Task Bayesian Calibration and Physics-Based Property Inference*

**Abstract:**
> Dispensed mass from an auger-based powder doser depends on actuator settings and powder properties, so every calibration curve doubles as a compact probe of cohesion, friction, and packing behavior. We frame calibration of an open-source doser as AI-driven, multi-objective, multi-task Bayesian optimization, with objectives of dose accuracy, repeatability, dispensing time, and accessible dose range. Each powder is a related task—primarily alloy precursors, in elemental or master-alloy form depending on handling constraints and hazards, dosed under inert atmosphere, alongside feedstocks such as AlSi10Mg and stainless steel—and multi-task models share information across powders to cut per-powder calibration effort. Alongside gravimetric parameter sweeps, we are exploring discrete element modeling with cohesive-frictional contact laws and measured particle-size distributions, enabling inference of effective cohesion and friction from dosing data, to be checked against shear-cell and Hall-flow measurements. Linking dosing response to these properties may anticipate downstream behavior such as spreadability and packing uniformity.

> AI-guided alloy design needs small, accurate blends of many different feedstock powders, and an automated powder doser has to be recalibrated each time the powder changes. We frame that recalibration as multi-objective Bayesian optimization (BO) of an open-source auger doser, with dose accuracy, repeatability, dispensing time and accessible dose range as objectives. On table salt, a 42-dose BO campaign found settings that hit a 0.5 g target in 8 of 8 validation doses with a median error of 1.8 mg in 78 s, against 245 s for hand-tuned settings. We have also dosed AlSi10Mg, Al 4047, Al 6063, 630 bronze and two size fractions of Si. Across a 13-powder set, delivered mass spanned a 24-fold range and ranked with literature angle of repose. Because dispensed mass depends on both the actuator settings and the powder, each calibration curve is also a compact probe of cohesion, friction and packing. We therefore treat each powder as a related task, so that a multi-task model can share information across powders and reduce the calibration effort for each new one. In parallel, discrete element simulations with cohesive-frictional contacts and measured particle-size distributions are being developed to infer effective cohesion and friction from dosing data, to be checked against shear-cell and Hall-flow measurements. If dosing response tracks these properties, it may also anticipate downstream behavior such as spreadability and packing uniformity.

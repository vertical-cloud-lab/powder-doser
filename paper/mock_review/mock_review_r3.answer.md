## Handling editor — Alán Aspuru-Guzik persona

**Scope and significance.** This is a plausible *Digital Discovery* hardware-focused Full Paper: a low-cost, open, single-channel gravimetric powder doser addresses a real obstacle in self-driving laboratories, and the documented, human-directed generative-CAD process is an unusual second contribution. The important advance over the earlier draft is **real** open-loop testing on 13 powders and closed-loop measurements at 50 mg, 200 mg, and 1 g (main §§2.3–2.5; Tables 2–3; Figs. 3–5; SI Table S3). Its candor about no-flow powders, missed small doses, a failed vibration driver, and noisy balance conditions is valuable. Novelty lies in the accessible hardware and unusually traceable design case study, not a demonstrated autonomous alloy-discovery platform or a proven superiority of AI design.

**Initial recommendation: major revision.** In the selected seven-powder subset, **24/24 valid 1 g doses** met ±5%; across **all valid 1 g doses**, Table 2 and SI Table S3 instead give **24/39**, with four other powders failing and two not dosed. At 50 mg, **14/28** met ±10%; at 200 mg, **18/30** met ±5%. Those denominators, the differing test conditions, and the fact that the **tested auger was redesigned by people in Fusion 360** must be unmissable. I would invite revision rather than reject: the measured results support a useful, bounded case study, but fuller metrology, provenance, and reporting of exclusions are necessary for a Full Paper.

## Independent report 1 — Milad Abolhasani persona: self-driving laboratories and automation

**Summary.** The authors built a balance-controlled auger module with rotation, tilt, and tapping, documented its AI-assisted development, and tested heterogeneous powders. The candid failure data make the single-channel result potentially useful for laboratories planning autonomous workflows. They do not yet show an integrated, unattended multi-powder laboratory system.

**Major comments**

1. **Separate conditional performance from deployable performance.** Main §2.5 and the abstract highlight 24/24 successful 1 g doses on seven powders, but Table 2/SI Table S3 show 24/39 valid 1 g doses across the 11 powders actually dosed, while barium chloride and fumed silica lack valid closed-loop tests. Present a compact target-by-target table of successes, misses by mechanism, non-attempts, screened-out runs, and firmware termination labels. State how the powder subset was selected. The 1 g finding is impressive *within that subset*, not a 13-powder success rate.
2. **Characterize the feedback loop at its real operating points.** Main §3.4 uses a fixed ±5 mg firmware stop, 45° fine rotations, then pairs of taps; §2.5 reports 11 of 12 small-dose misses on free-flowing powders were overshoots. Show representative time-resolved mass, command, and stopping traces at 50, 200, and 1000 mg, plus the distribution of final increments and time in each phase. Explain whether the existing serial records allow a retrospective assessment of thresholds; prospective parameter sweeps would be needed to establish a better controller.
3. **Make unattended-use claims proportionate to what was run.** Main Fig. 1e depicts a control loop; Fig. 6 is explicitly *future work*, and SI Table S4 records postponed/excluded runs, misalignment, caking, and balance-read failures. Define the human tasks between doses (filling, positioning, taring, checking the cup/outlet, clearing faults), and report consecutive unattended doses and recovery behavior if demonstrated. Otherwise describe the result as a benchtop, single-channel module designed *for possible SDL integration*.
4. **Provide a practical baseline or limit comparisons.** Main §2.7 contrasts approximately $185 in listed electronics with commercial systems, but §2.5 reports 53, 152, and 185 s median times *among doses within limit* at the three targets. A side-by-side manual balance or simpler auger baseline with time, error, failed attempts, and operator involvement would establish the actual advantage. In its absence, avoid implying equivalent capability to commercial dispensers.

**Minor comments**

1. Main §3.4 calls SI Table S3 the record of “every dose”; Table S3 is **grouped summary data**, while SI §S5 points to `doses_all.csv` for per-dose records. Correct this description and provide a versioned, accessible data snapshot.
2. Explain why the first-round balance-stability flag and second-round five-reading bracket (§3.4) produce comparable outcomes; stratify 1 g results by round and location in Fig. 5/SI Table S3.
3. The Fig. 1b glove-box photograph shows a demonstration, whereas §2.3 says validation involved an open bench and fume hoods. Label the photograph explicitly as illustrative, not the measurement setup.
4. Define a dose that is “within limit” but marked “stalled” by firmware, and show both counts side by side (§2.5; SI Table S3).

**Recommendation: major revision.**

## Independent report 2 — Johannes G. Khinast persona: powder feeding and gravimetric metrology

**Summary.** The real protocol-C rotation results, tap test, dose summaries, and explicit description of failed powders are significant improvements. The results show a strong dependence on material and target mass. Measurement conditions and feeder operating limits still require firmer characterization.

**Major comments**

1. **Quantify measurement uncertainty during actual dispensing.** Main §2.3 reports eight idle readings with spreads up to **88 mg** after 20 August; SI Fig. S2 corroborates a large change, and main §3.4 switches from a stability flag in round 1 to five bracketed readings in round 2. Eight stationary values, some with zero displayed spread, are not a dynamic uncertainty budget for 50 mg targets with a ±5 mg acceptance window. Measure or extract balance response following stepper/servo/tap actuation, stabilization times, drift, and post-stop discharge. Report which outcomes are robust to plausible mass-reading errors, separately by round/location.
2. **Clarify material-dependent failures without overdiagnosing their cause.** Main §2.3 says fine silicon made a 7 mg pulse at 45° and fumed silica delivered at most 0.25 mg/revolution in an unscreened run; Fig. 3a labels both as non-conveying “at any tilt.” These are failures to deliver a *resolvable, repeatable amount*, not necessarily literal zero movement. Fine silicon cohesion and fumed silica trapped air are plausible explanations, not established mechanisms. Add a consistent detection criterion and distinguish blockage checks, caking, true non-delivery, and below-noise readings (main §3.3; SI Table S4).
3. **Complete the feeder specification.** Main §3.1 usefully supplies the **tested** 10.4 mm pitch, 0.5 mm flight, 83 mm flighted length, 21 mm bore, 3 mm outlet, and 20:44 motor-to-auger gearing. SI Fig. S1 instead shows the earlier **AI-designed** full-length, 10 mm-pitch, 2 mm-thick flight. Add a tested-versus-early-design dimension table, tolerances/print orientation, clearance at the rotating collars, and actual nozzle/exit dimensions so readers cannot substitute SI Fig. S1 for the tested feeder.
4. **Characterize the physical powder envelope.** Main Fig. 3 explicitly notes uncontrolled fill level and unmeasured particle descriptors; §2.6 notes unmeasured cup misses and hold-up. Report independent particle-size distribution, bulk/tapped density, moisture/conditioning and a flowability proxy where possible; at minimum measure starting fill and residual powder. For barium chloride, quantify when caking occurred and how many attempted doses were excluded (Table 2; SI Table S4). If the work cannot add measurements, avoid generalizing the Fig. 3c grouping into a powder classifier.
5. **Do not treat unavailable actuation and compromised speed data as characterized operating capabilities.** Main Table 3 protocol F has zero trials because the vibration driver failed, and §2.4 calls the protocol-D speed exponent only indicative because of fixed sweep order, no intermediate zeroing, and 15–54% extra rotation. Repair the driver and repeat controlled tap/rotation/vibration comparisons only if claiming vibration benefits; repeat a randomized, corrected speed sweep if retaining a speed-law claim. Otherwise omit or plainly quarantine these results.
6. **Test use-dependent drift and validate the acceptance rationale.** Main §§2.5–2.6 disclose uncontrolled fill, no fixed starting flight position, and slow doses stopped by a 200-cycle budget. Repeat doses at defined fill fractions and across wear/cleaning cycles, especially with AlSi10Mg; report holdup and PLA abrasion/fouling. Derive the claim that the chosen ±5%/±10% dose limits add “less than one atomic percent” alloy-composition error, taking into account component masses and molar masses (§2.5), rather than relying on references alone.

**Minor comments**

1. Clarify that SI Table S1’s ~**$185** excludes printed material (~260 g PLA, <$10), custom PCB, and the analytical balance; its itemized prices add to **$185.90**, so state the rounding convention.
2. Main §2.4 says a static hold gives a “clean shut-off”; specify that zero displayed change was seen only in the **33 early, quiet-bench holds** and that later runs were noise limited.
3. Label speed/rpm and rotation throughout as *auger* units, not motor units; the main §3.2 correction from servo settings **0/45/90 to tube tilt 0°/22.5°/45°** is especially important in reusable data.
4. SI §S2 checkout says to “vibrate” although the driver failed in all protocol-F tests. Say whether a successful checkout of that actuator is actually possible on the supplied build.

**Recommendation: major revision.**

## Independent report 3 — Adriana Schulz persona: large language models and generative CAD

**Summary.** The interesting contribution is a documented account of engineers specifying interfaces and checking AI-produced CAD, including failures, followed by human CAD reconstruction. The manuscript now properly identifies the human-designed tested auger, but several visual and rhetorical choices still blur the boundary between *AI-derived design history* and *physical validation of the final human-redesigned machine*.

**Major comments**

1. **Give a per-part, stage-specific provenance map.** Main abstract and §2.2 call the parts AI-designed first; §3.1 says the tested auger was modelled in Fusion 360 and has a substantially different flight and gearing. SI Table S2 says **each of eight tested printed parts** was modelled by people in Fusion 360, while main Fig. 1 caption describes plates/brackets as earlier AI-modelled parts. Resolve this apparent conflict by distinguishing the **physical part installed**, the CAD geometry shown in each figure, any AI-derived starting design, subsequent human remodel, and file used to print/test each part. SI Fig. S1 must not stand in for performance evidence of its untested earlier auger.
2. **Bound the comparison among AI tools and human CAD.** Main §2.2.0.3 says Zoo performed better spatially, one or two iterations equalled seven or eight coding-agent iterations, and every AI route was slower and lower quality than an experienced engineer. It also concedes these were team impressions, and the three hours of edited human-redesign video are not matched timing data. Either add an explicit comparison protocol with identical tasks, prompts, start states, human intervention and elapsed/active times, or recast these as illustrated experiences from this project without comparative ranking.
3. **Analyze the design record actually invoked as a contribution.** Main §§2.2 and 4 give **128 design-log entries** but defer their quantitative analysis; SI §S7 gives **754 agent requests and 789/877 agent-authored commits** spanning CAD, firmware, analysis, and writing. Those are not CAD productivity or autonomous-design percentages. Tabulate CAD-specific parts, human vs agent modifications, iteration count, failure types, and detection by automated check versus human inspection, with inclusion rules and examples linked to Table 1/Fig. 2. A modest descriptive audit of existing records suffices; no new model benchmark is essential if claims are modest.
4. **Specify the reproducible human–AI workflow and its limits.** Main §§2.2.0.1–2.2.0.2 and 3.5 sketch issue → code → rendering → pull request → review, but do not give an example dimensioned input, interface/tolerance checklist, criteria for accepting or freezing a part, or exact check coverage. Show these in a one-page protocol linked to a frozen repository release. State clearly that humans selected the auger concept, supplied dimensioned drawings, judged each part, and eventually redesigned the tested auger and other parts.
5. **Moderate generalization.** Main §4 calls the method “as transferable as the hardware,” whereas the case involves one team, shifting tools, design changes, and a final conventional-CAD rebuild. Describe it as a candidate workflow and articulate limits from team expertise, part complexity, tool version and review effort. Main Fig. 6’s Onshape multi-doser is a separate, future conventional-CAD development, not another success of the AI workflow.

**Minor comments**

1. Main §§2.2.0.1–2.2.0.3 have malformed heading numbers; use §§2.2.1–2.2.3.
2. Fig. 2c’s AI-geared auger and pinion should be marked clearly as **not the tested 20:44 pair**; main §2.2.0.3 gives the earlier pair as 16:48.
3. Main §3.5 promises public “model identifiers, and dates ... summarised in the SI,” but SI §S7 lists general model families and a broad time period. Supply a dated tool/model inventory, or narrow the promise.
4. Archive a specific release/DOI with CAD, firmware, logs, scripts and per-dose data; a moving GitHub URL is weaker evidence for reproducibility (main §3.5; SI §S7).
5. Main Fig. 1’s rendered tap collar/solenoid are “modelled from photographs.” Say whether this render represents the built geometry faithfully rather than letting it serve as proof of tested AI-made components.

**Recommendation: major revision.**

## Cross-document consistency and readability audit

**Numerical and procedural checks.** I summed the numerator/denominator entries in main Table 2 and cross-checked SI Table S3: **50 mg 14/28; 200 mg 18/30; 1 g 24/39; all valid doses 56/97**. The abstract’s 24/24 is **not arithmetically wrong**: it selects seven powders (six salt doses plus six sets of three); its nine 1 g doses from three alloy-relevant powders also match Table 2. Main §4’s “half to 60%” matches the small-target totals. But a reader needs the all-powder totals next to these favorable subsets, plus **valid-dose versus attempted-run** accounting from SI Table S4. “All 24” does not include 15 failed valid 1 g doses, two powders without valid 1 g results, or excluded runs. SI §S5 calls Table S3 a summary and points to the external per-dose CSV; main §3.4 incorrectly says every dose is *listed in Table S3*. I could verify the attached PDF summaries, **not the external repository’s raw per-dose data or exact numbers of excluded individual dose attempts**.

**Other reconciliations or discrepancies.** Main §§2.1, 3.2; Fig. 1d; Table 3; and SI §S6 consistently set the **physical tube’s maximum tilt at 45°**. The raw servo setting reaches **90**, but 2:1 gearing converts that to **45° tube tilt**; do not call it a 90° physical tilt. Main §2.3/Fig. 3a say three powders “did not move at any tilt,” whereas §2.3 records a **7 mg pulse** for fine silicon at 45° and weak fumed-silica movement: replace the categorical phrase with “no reliably measurable per-revolution delivery.” Main Fig. 1 caption’s AI-modelled **plates and brackets** sits uneasily beside SI Table S2’s statement that **all eight tested printed parts were modelled in Fusion 360**; state whether the former are render-only historical geometries or were actually printed in the tested rig. Main Fig. 2c and SI Fig. S1 show an earlier AI auger, while main §3.1 and Fig. 1c identify a materially different *tested*, human-modelled auger. Main §3.5 says model IDs/dates are summarized in SI, but SI §S7 offers only family-level names and 2026 dates. The article’s single-channel cost claim is defensible **with exclusions**: SI Table S1 lists ~$185 in purchased items (itemized **$185.90**), excluding <$10 printed PLA, custom PCB and balance; the abstract’s “under $200, plus an analytical balance” can be read as excluding the board too. The production manuscript still has author-email, DOI and journal-template placeholders on p. 1.

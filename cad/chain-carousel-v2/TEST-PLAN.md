# Chain test plan, v2 (vertical twin chain)

What the rig has to answer before anyone builds a station for it: **can two #35 chains in vertical planes carry 13 loaded carriages round four 32T corners in step, and stop each one at the station repeatably, with every module staying shut and its powder staying in?** Each test lists what to measure, how, and the pass mark. Numbers in *italics* are predictions from the model, so a result far from them means the model or the build is wrong.

Run the tests in order. T0–T2 take an afternoon and need no electronics beyond the driver. Log everything with [`test/chain_rig.py`](test/chain_rig.py) (CSV under `test/runs/`), commit the CSVs, and post a summary in [#128](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/powder-doser/issues/128).

| # | Test | Setup | Measure | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | **Fit, by hand** | Coupling spider out, all 13 carriages and modules | Turn the drive shaft one full lap of the loop (104 pitches, 3.25 turns). At each corner, feeler gauge between neighbouring modules' gears and between each gear and the corner shaft | No contact or binding, and every plate stays on its snap hooks over the top. *Tightest gaps in the CAD sweep: carriage to carriage on a corner 4.3 mm, gear to corner shaft 5.8 mm (6.4 with the printed blank's slightly smaller gear disc), neighbouring gears on a corner about 7 mm* |
| T1 | **Tension and phase** | Jack screws, top bearings' bolts loose | Push each long side sideways at mid-span with 5 N and read the deflection. With a square on each carriage on a vertical run, compare the height of its front and back tab screws | Deflection 3–4 mm on all four long sides; record the jack-screw turns. Front-to-back height difference under 0.5 mm on every carriage. A twist means a sprocket slipped on its shaft |
| T2 | **Drive torque** | All 13 modules on, driver disabled, spider in | Force gauge on a 100 mm lever clamped to the coupling: turn the loop slowly, log the peak and the mean | *Under 0.5 N·m: the loop is balanced, so this is bearing and chain friction plus a few hundred grams out of balance at most.* The NEMA 34 has 12 N·m. A high or lumpy reading means binding at a corner |
| T3 | **Index repeatability (the key number)** | Homed (`chain_rig.py home`), dial indicator on the front plate at the station touching carriage 1's front tongue along the travel | `chain_rig.py --ask repeat --moves 100`: out one module and back, read the dial after each return. Then `lap` x 5: read every carriage as it arrives | Return-to-station spread (max - min) under 0.5 mm. Per-carriage offsets on a lap are expected (chain pitch tolerance, about 1 mm over 104 pitches); they are fine if they repeat within 0.3 mm lap to lap, because software can store them |
| T4 | **Station push-up, hold-downs and snap hooks** | Carriage 1 at the station; force gauge pushing up through the carriage window on the mounting plate's underside; dial on the carriage plate beside the hinge | 0 to 30 N in 5 N steps, with and without the two hold-downs. Then the force at which the plate's free end pulls off its snap hooks | Lift under 0.3 mm at 20 N with both hold-downs. Without them, the lift shows how much of the docking force the chains would otherwise carry (Aug 18 pitch, [12:41]). The snap force is what the station's tilt has to beat: *a few newtons; trim the hooks' 0.8 mm overlap if it is much more than 10 N* |
| T5 | **Tilt clearance** | Carriage 1 at the station | Lift module 1's free end by hand to 45° and 90°; feeler gauge to the corner shafts and to the module hanging from the top run | *At 90° the cap end is about 27 mm under the gear of the module on the top run, and the plate clears the bottom corner shafts by about 17 mm.* No contact anywhere through the swing |
| T6 | **Transit vibration and powder** | ADXL343 on module 1's plate near the outlet; auger filled with salt, outlet uncapped | 100 index moves at each of 25, 50 and 100 mm/s (250 mm/s² accel), logging peak and RMS acceleration per move; weigh the auger before and after each block. Then 10 full laps at the chosen speed and weigh again | No powder loss (under 1 mg on the HR-100A) at the chosen speed. **New for a vertical loop:** every module rolls one full turn about its own axis per lap and hangs upside down on the top run, so the laps test the "keep one end capped" question from the July 15 meeting directly |
| T7 | **Endurance** | All 13 modules loaded, 50 mm/s | `chain_rig.py endurance --moves 2000` (about 150 laps), unattended but in sight; stop on any driver alarm | Zero driver alarms, hall index edges within 0.3 mm of their first-lap step counts, motor and driver under 60 °C. Before and after: 20-pitch length of both chains (*190.50 mm new*; replace at 3% stretch), the A-1 tab screws, the sprocket and bearing set screws, and the snap hooks (no creep, plates still held) |
| T8 | **Corners on video** | Phone slow-motion at each of the four corners | Slow index round each corner | Carriage plates clear the sprocket teeth (*3.6 mm*), modules clear the shafts and each other, tongues enter the hold-downs square |

## What the results decide

- **T3 sets the station's capture range.** If the return spread is under 0.5 mm, the kinematic couplings in the Aug 18 pitch need only a small lead-in. If it is several mm, the station needs a locator (a V-notch in the tongue and a spring plunger in the hold-down) before the couplings engage.
- **T1 and T7 say whether the corner shafts keep the chains in step.** Each shaft carries a front and a back sprocket, so the chains can only drift apart if a sprocket slips on its shaft. If one does, the fix is keyed shafts or a flat under each set screw.
- **T4 says whether the hold-downs are enough**, and sets the force the tilt actuator needs to beat the snap hooks.
- **T6 picks the transit speed** and says whether an open outlet survives a lap of rolling and hanging upside down. If it doesn't, the module needs a cap before anything else on this rig matters.

## Logged columns

`run, utc, cmd, module, target_steps, pos_steps, move_s, index_edge_steps, alarm, dial_mm`. A step is 1/4000 of a sprocket turn (CL86T DIP set to 4000 pulses/rev), 0.076 mm of chain. One module is a quarter turn of the 32T sprocket, exactly 1000 steps.

Question: Has anyone inferred or predicted the particle size or particle size distribution of a powder from gravimetric dispensing or feeding data, that is, from balance mass-versus-time readings, mass-flow-rate fluctuations, or the mass delivered per screw revolution of a screw/auger feeder, loss-in-weight feeder or automated powder dosing instrument?


No. ## Finding

**I did not find a published, validated demonstration that predicts a powder’s particle size or particle-size distribution (PSD) *from gravimetric feeder or dispenser data alone***—whether balance mass-versus-time readings, fluctuations in the derived mass-flow rate, or mass delivered per screw revolution. This is a qualified literature-search finding, not proof that no such method exists. The closest studies establish that particle size *affects* feeding, or use feeder signals to infer packing and equipment behavior, but they do not report size or PSD as the predicted output. (bostijn2019amultivariateapproach pages 1-6, fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 1-2, fathollahi2024understandingpowderbehavior pages 3-5, kobayashi2024feedfactorprofile pages 3-4)

The distinction between what was measured and what was predicted is central:

*Table: The closest published studies use particle size as an independently measured input or infer feeder phenomena such as density change and screw layering. None of the listed studies reconstructs particle size or PSD from gravimetric feeder data alone.*

**The closest signal-based inference** is Fathollahi and colleagues’ 2024 loss-in-weight study. They logged hopper weight, mass flow, screw speed, and *feed factor*—grams delivered per revolution—at 1 Hz. Changes in the feed-factor profile were interpreted as evidence of powder densification within feeding cycles and powder layering on the screws across cycles. PSD, however, was measured separately by laser diffraction, not reconstructed from that profile. Their Figure 4 illustrates the two feed-factor trends used for those *operational* diagnoses. (fathollahi2024understandingpowderbehavior pages 3-5, fathollahi2024understandingpowderbehavior pages 8-10, fathollahi2024understandingpowderbehavior media b6404b79)

**There is a plausible size signal, but not a demonstrated inverse estimator.** In a micro-feeder study with an outlet balance, finer and more compressible powders generally showed different effective-density and feeding trajectories from coarser powders; the authors used the gravimetric data to characterize density variation and improve feed-rate prediction, not to estimate size. In a lactose-feeding comparison, the finest grade had the lowest feed factor, yet two grades with nearly identical PSDs also fed differently under some configurations. An earlier dispensing review reported an inverse relationship between dose and particle size for *narrow size fractions in a particular tube*, but did not turn that relationship into a general, validated balance-trace-to-PSD method. (fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 4-5, fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 1-2, fathollahi2020performanceevaluationof pages 5-7, jaspers2025optimizingcontinuousfeeding pages 5-8, yang2007meteringanddispensing pages 9-10)

**Why mass per revolution is not itself a size measurement.** If screw geometry is fixed, mass delivered per revolution reflects how much of its available volume the powder fills *and the density of that filled powder*. Both can change with hopper level, consolidation, flowability, cohesion, adhesion to the screws, and operating conditions. In gravimetric mode, feedback also changes screw speed to hold mass flow near its setpoint, potentially suppressing differences in the flow-rate trace while leaving differences in feed factor. Consequently, different PSDs can plausibly yield similar signals, and similar PSDs can yield different signals; a full PSD is especially difficult to identify uniquely from one aggregate mass measurement. This is an inference from the reported feeder physics and comparisons, not an experimentally proven impossibility. (engisch2014lossinweightfeedingin pages 22-27, bostijn2019amultivariateapproach pages 22-26, fathollahi2024understandingpowderbehavior pages 8-10, jaspers2025optimizingcontinuousfeeding pages 5-8)

A **powder- and instrument-specific classifier or estimate of a limited size statistic**, such as a change in median size or fines content, remains a reasonable research hypothesis. Demonstrating it would require independently measured PSD labels for systematically varied size fractions of the *same material*, synchronized balance and screw-speed records, controlled density/moisture/fill-level and operating conditions, and validation on held-out batches. Existing studies provide candidate trace features—feed-factor level and decay, cycle trends, and feed-rate variability—but do **not** establish the accuracy or generalizability of such an inverse model. Where actual online fineness was reported alongside feeder-derived flow information in a pulverized-fuel study, the size measurement came instead from a separate optical particle-size probe. (bostijn2019amultivariateapproach pages 10-14, bostijn2019amultivariateapproach pages 14-18, fathollahi2024understandingpowderbehavior pages 8-10, archary2016massflowand pages 13-16, archary2016massflowand pages 1-3)
